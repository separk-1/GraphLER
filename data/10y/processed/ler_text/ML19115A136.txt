Abstract (Limit to 1400 spaces, i.e., approximately 14 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On March 27, 2017, during refueling outage 01 R24 at 0840 hours, the Unit 1 Main Steam Isolation Valve (MSIV) as-found closure
time test results indicated that four MSIVs failed to close within the required Technical Specification (TS) time of less than or equal
to five seconds. The safety significance of this event was minimal.
The cause of two of the four MSIV slow closure timing events was inadequate procedure on how to apply Super 0-Lube to valve
stem bottom 0-ring of the airpack manifold solenoid, and the cause of the other two slow closure timing events was environmental
conditions in the Unit 1 MSIV Room.
Corrective actions included replacing the 1· 0203-1 B and 1· 0203-1 D MSIV airpack manifolds in 01 R25, revising the MSIV airpack
manifold maintenance procedure regarding the amount of Super 0-Lube to be applied, and revising operating procedures to
prevent MSIV closure time drift observed to occur at MSIV room temperatures below 90 degrees F.
This condition is being reported in accordance with 10 CFR 50. 73(a)(2)(i)(B), which requires reporting of any operation or condition
that was prohibited by the plant's TS.

EVENT IDENTIFICATION
Four Main Steam Isolation Valves (MSIVs) Closure Times Exceed Technical Specifications Limit
A. CONDITION PRIOR TO EVENT
Unit: 1 Event Date: March 27, 2017 Event Time: 0840 hours
Reactor Mode: 4 Mode Name: Cold Shutdown Power Level: 0%
B. DESCRIPTION OF EVENT
On March 27, 2017, while Unit 1 was in Mode 4 for refueling outage 01 R24, Operations was performing
surveillance, "MSIV Closure Timing," when four of the eight Unit 1 Main Steam [SB] Isolation Valves [ISV] (MSIVs),
1-0203-1 B, 1-0203-1 D, 1-0203-2C, and 1-0203-20 failed to close within the required cold shutdown Technical
Specification (TS) limit of greater than or equal to three seconds and less than or equal to five seconds. The closure
times for those four MS IVs were 5. 7, 5.2, 5.1, and 5.6 seconds, respectively. The other four MS IVs all closed within
the required TS time.
This condition is being reported in accordance with 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(i)(B), which requires reporting of any
operation br condition that was prohibited by the plant's Technical Specifications.
C. CAUSE OF EVENT
MS IVs 1-0203-2C and 1-0203-20 are located in the Unit 1 MSIV Room and MS IVs 1-0203-1 B and 1-0203-1 D are
located in the Unit 1 Drywell. The cause of the slow closure timing events for the 1-0203-1 B and 1-0203-1 D MS IVs
was due to an inadequate procedure on how to apply Super 0-Lube to valve stem bottom 0-ring of the airpack
manifold. The slow closure times were due to excess Super 0-Lube on airpack manifold solenoids. The
maintenance procedure for applying the Super 0-Lube was not specific on the amount of lubricant to be used on
the bottom 0-ring.
The cause of the slow closure timing events for the 1-0203-2C and 1-0203·.20 MSIVs was due to environmental
conditions in the Unit 1 MSIV Room. Beginning in June 2009, the average temperature in the Unit 1 MSIV room
was significantly reduced due to the replacement of the MSIV room coolers. An abnormal drift between the as-left
closure time from the previous outage to the as-found closure time of the current outage has been observed to be
correlated with cooler room temperatures. A similar temperature-correlated drift was not observed for the MSIVs
located inside the drywell, where the temperature is consistently warmer than the MSIV Room._

D. SAFETY ANALYSIS
System Design
The design of the MSIVs is to prevent reactor coolant [AD] inventory loss and protect plant personnel in the event of
steam line breakage outside the isolation valves, and to complete the primary containment [NH] boundary after a
Loss of Coolant Accident (LOCA). The MSIVs are 20-inch air/spring operated, balanced "Y"-type globe valves.
There are four main steam lines and each steam line has two isolation valves, one inside (inboard MSIV) and one
outside (outboard MSIV) of primary containment.
Updated Final Safety Analysis Report (UFSAR) Section 6.2.4.1 provides the valve closure time for the main steam
line is based on the main steam line break accident discussed in Section 15.6. Ensuring the MSIV closure time less
than or equal to five seconds, sufficient coolant will remain in the reactor vessel to provide adequate core cooling.
The valves are designed to close and to be leak-tight during the worst conditions of pressure, temperature, and
steam flow following a break in the main steam line outside the containment. The MS IVs are leak tested in
accordance with the 10 CFR 50 Appendix J program. UFSAR Section 15.6 provides that a maximum MSIV closure
time of 10.5 seconds would limit the total amount of liquid and steam lost from the primary system to prevent the
core from being uncovered, and this closure time will ensure the radiological doses are well below the guidelines set
forth in 10 CFR 100.
Proper adjustment of the MSIV closure times prevents operation outside of the operational and design limits.
Safety Impact
Technical Specifications and the In-Service Testing program require the MSIVs to close in a three to five second
time range. Five seconds is fast enough to prevent a gross release of fission products, and three seconds is slow
enough to minimize the severity of the pressure transient resulting from isolating the main steam lines during full
power operation. Slow MSIV stroke times would challenge the steam release limits of a steam line break outside of
containment should the corresponding inboard valve simultaneously fail to close.
The Unit 1 MSIVs 1-0203-1 B, 1-0203-1 D, 1-0203-2C, and 1-0203-2D failed to close within the required Technical
Specification limit of less than or equal to five seconds. The closure times for those four MSIVs were 5.7, 5.2, 5.1,
and 5.6 seconds, respectively, however, the four MSIVs all closed inside the UFSAR limit of less than or equal to
10.5 seconds. Although the required five second Technical Specification limit was not met for these four MSIVs, the
MS IVs were capable of performing the required UFSAR safety function during the operating cycle prior to 01 R24.
Furthermore, since the MSIVs were not required to be operable or available at the time of discovery, this condition
did not create any actual plant or safety consequences since the unit was not in an accident or transient condition
requiring the use of main steam line isolation valves during this period of time.
Risk Insights
The closure times for the four MSIVs were 5. 7, 5.2, 5.1, and 5.6 seconds and recorded as slow, but the MSIVs did
fully close. The MSIV success criterion in the Plant Probabilistic Risk Assessment (PRA) model, however, does not
depend on closure time. Therefore, a difference of a fraction of a second between actual MSIV closure time and the
acceptance criterion for the Technical Specification closure time has no effect on the PRA model. As a result, there
is no effect on Core Damage Frequency (CDF) or Large Early Release Frequency (LERF).
In conclusion, the failure of the four MSIVs to close within the required time would not have caused the station to
exceed the inventory and dose release limits. Furthermore, the impact on risk of this event is negligible. Therefore,
the overall safety significance of this event was minimal.

E. CORRECTIVE ACTIONS
Immediate:
1. The 1-0203-1 B and 1-0203-1 D MSIV airpack manifolds were replaced during the outage, 01 R25 in 2019.
2. Procedures concerning the MSIV Room Cooler operation have been revised to maintain a consist temperature
in the MSIV Room.
Follow-up:
1. The MSIV lubrication surveillance will be revised to provide additional information on the amount of Super 0-
Lube to be used in the airpack manifold solenoid 0-rings.
F. PREVIOUS OCCURRENCES
The station events database, LERs, and INPO ICES were reviewed for similar events at Quad Cities. Specifically,
this event was primarily attributed to an inadequate procedure regarding the amount of Super 0-Lube on the airpack
manifold bottom 0-rings and cooler temperatures in the MSIV Room. Based on the causes of this event and
associated corrective actions, the events listed below, provided insight into resolution of this event.
• Station Issue Report (IR) 1213432, Unit 1 Outboard MSIV 1-0203-2C Failed QCOS 0250-04 (05/09/2011) -The
1-0203-2C outboard MSIV closure time exceeded the acceptance criteria for cold timing, which is less than or
equal to five seconds. The stroke time recorded was 5.2 seconds. The cause was dirty contacts within the
control circuitry creating an electrical time delay. This event occurred after the MSIV Room environmental
conditions changed, which led to the current action to control the temperature in the MSIV Room. Therefore, IR
1213432 provided useful insights associated with this event.
• LER 2013-002-00 Outboard Main Steam Isolation Valves (MS IVs) Stroke Times Exceeded (03/11 /2013) - All
four Unit 1 outboard MSIVs closed slowly due to age and wear degraded actuator seals. The vendor
recommended a replacement frequency of eight years for the actuators. This event was the second occurrence
of slow closure times after the MSIV Room environmental conditions changed, which led to the current action to
control the temperature in the MSIV Room. Therefore, LER 2013-002-00 provided useful insights associated
with this event.

16. Abstract (Limit to 1326 spaces. i.e .. approximately 13 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On December 28, 2025, at 1606 CST, LaSalle Station Unit 1 was manually scrammed due to degrading containment
parameters associated with a leak from the 'B' reactor recirculation pump line. The scram was not complex. All control rods
fully inserted as expected; no Emergency Core Cooling Systems actuated or injected into the core.
This event did not pose a threat to the health and safety of the public. This event was reported via Event Notification #58100
under 1 OC FR 50.72(b)(2)(iv)(B) as an event that resulted in actuation of the Reactor Protection System when the Reactor was
critical. Subsequent Event Notification #58102 under 10 CFR 50.72(b)(3)(ii)(A) reported a degraded reactor coolant system
pressure boundary due to a through-wall leak identified on a reactor recirculation system three-quarter inch vent valve.
Failure analysis attributed the cause of the leakage to vibration induced cyclic fatigue. Corrective actions include replacement
of the affected component and planned inspections/modifications to similar equipment configurations.

NARRATIVE
Plant and System Identification
LaSalle Country Station Unit 1 is a General Electric Boiling Water Reactor with 3546 Megawatts Thermal Rated Core
Power. The affected systems were the Reactor Protection System (RPS) and Reactor Recirculation (RR) System.
Condition Prior to Event
Unit(s): 1 Date: December 28, 2025 Time: 1606 CST
Reactor Mode(s): 1 Mode(s) Name: Power Operation Power Level: 93 percent
No systems, structures, or components that were inoperable at the start of the event contributed to the event.
Description
On December 28, 2025 at 1606 CST, LaSalle County Station Unit 1 was manually scrammed due to degrading
containment parameters caused by unidentified drywell leakage. Following the manual scram, personnel entered the Unit
1 drywell to investigate the source of the leakage. The investigation identified a degraded reactor coolant system
pressure boundary due to a through wall leak from an RR system vent valve.
Cause
Through wall leakage was identified on the bonnet and body-to-bonnet seal weld of the upstream manually operated vent
valve for a reactor recirculation pump discharge flow control valve. Failure analysis determined the leakage was caused
by vibration induced fatigue cracking.
Reportability and Safety Analysis
The safety significance of this event was minimal. All control rods fully inserted and all systems responded as expected to
the scram. Makeup capability was adequate to compensate for the leak. Emergency Core Cooling Systems were not
needed, but operable and capable of fulfilling their intended safety functions. This event did not meet the NEI 99-02
definition of an unplanned scram with complications. The event did not constitute a safety system functional failure
(SSFF) as defined in accordance with NEI 99-02, "Regulatory Assessment Performance Indicator Guideline."
RPS actuation is reportable under 10 CFR 50. 72(b )(2)(iv)(B) as an event or condition that results in the actuation of the
RPS when the reactor is critical. An ENS report was made to the NRC at 2026 EST on December 28, 2025 (EN #58100).
Upon determination of the leakage source, ENS report #58102 under 10 CFR 50.72(b)(3)(ii)(A) addressed the degraded
reactor coolant system pressure boundary.
Corrective Actions
Immediate corrective actions taken in response to the condition were:
• Removed the failed valve assembly and installed blind flange connection
• Initiated failure analysis and causal investigation
Planned corrective actions are:
• Installation of replacement vent valve assembly
• Inspection and/or replacement of similar piping and valve assemblies

Previous Occurrences
LER 373-2018-004-00
On March 22, 2018, while LaSalle Unit 1 was in Mode 1, leakage from the reactor coolant system pressure boundary
required entry into technical specification actions for required shutdown. The source of the leakage was identified as a
through-wall pipe leak on a reactor recirculation vent line. This was reported in accordance with 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(ii)(A)
as a principal safety barrier being in a degraded condition, and 10 CFR 50. 73(a)(2)(i)(A) for a required plant shutdown in
accordance with technical specifications. Cause of the pipe leakage was determined to be fatigue cracking.

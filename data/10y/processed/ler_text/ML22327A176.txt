16. Abstract (Limit to 1560 spaces, i.e., approximately 15 single-spaced typewritten lines) 
 On September 26, 2022, operators at the Susquehanna Steam Electric Station performed quarterly stroke time 
testing of Unit 2 Emergency Service Water (ESW) to Turbine Building Closed Cooling Water and ESW to 
Reactor Building Closed Cooling Water isolation valves. Performance of this surveillance results in the 
inoperability of Unit 2, Division 2 of the Core Spray system. This surveillance was performed while Unit 2, 
Division 1 of the Core Spray system was out of service for planned maintenance, resulting in both divisions of the 
Core Spray system being inoperable and a loss of safety function. The condition was identified during the 
subsequent shift while performing routine review of operator logs. The condition is being reported in 
accordance with 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(D) as a condition that could have prevented fulfillment of a safety 
function. 
 
The cause of the event was determined to be inadequate performance of a loss of safety function determination, 
which failed to identify a loss of safety function resulting from concurrent inoperability of both divisions of Core 
Spray. This cause was attributed to human performance. There were no actual consequences to the health and 
safety of the public as a result of this event.

EVENT DESCRIPTION 
On September 26, 2022, operators at the Susquehanna Steam Electric Station performed quarterly stroke 
time testing of the Unit 2 Emergency Service Water (ESW) [EIIS System Code: BI] to Turbine Building 
Closed Cooling Water (TBCCW) [EIIS System Code: CC] and ESW to Reactor Building Closed Cooling 
Water (RBCCW) [EIIS System Code: CC] isolation valves [EIIS Component Code: ISV]. Performance of this 
surveillance results in the inoperability of Unit 2, Division 2 of the Core Spray system [EIIS System Code: 
BG]. This surveillance was performed while Unit 2, Division 1 of the Core Spray system was out of service for 
planned maintenance, resulting in both divisions of the Core Spray system being inoperable and a loss of 
safety function. The condition was identified during the subsequent shift while performing routine review of 
operator logs.  
 
The following timeline demonstrates the sequence of events: 
09/25/2022 ~ 09:00: The quarterly surveillance original start time is postponed due to conflicting work on the 
Unit 2 RBCCW heat exchangers [EIIS Component Code: HX]. A condition report is generated for the 
surveillance to be rescheduled after the completion of the Unit 2, Division 1 Core Spray planned 
maintenance. 
09/25/2022 ~ 19:45: Unit 2, Division 1 Core Spray planned maintenance begins. Limited Condition of 
Operation (LCO) 3.5.1, “Emergency Core Cooling Systems (ECCS), Reactor Pressure Vessel (RPV) Water 
Inventory Control, and Reactor Core Isolation Cooling (RCIC) System,” Condition A is entered to support this 
work.  
09/26/2022 ~ 05:00: During operations shift turnover, the need to track the postponed surveillance was 
discussed. However, the oncoming Unit Supervisor completed turnover with the misunderstanding that the 
surveillance needed to be completed during the Unit 2 RBCCW heat exchanger restoration. 
09/26/2022 ~ 09:00: The surveillance is rescheduled for 09/27/2022, after the Unit 2, Division 1 Core Spray 
planned maintenance was scheduled to be complete. 
09/26/2022 ~ 14:00: Unit 2 RBCCW heat exchanger work is reported completed. Operations begins 
preparation for performance of the quarterly surveillance. During this process, operations inadequately 
performed a loss of safety function determination using a previously completed copy of the station procedure, 
which did not identify the Unit 2, Division 1 Core Spray ongoing planned maintenance. 
09/26/2022 ~ 14:08: Operations entered Condition C of LCO 3.7.2, “Emergency Service Water (ESW) 
System,” due to Division 2 ESW being inoperable for performance of the surveillance. A peer check for entry 
into the LCO condition was not received as required by station procedures. Investigation identified this as the 
time the loss of safety function occurred. 
09/26/2022 ~ 14:17: The surveillance on Division 2 ESW is completed. Operations exited Condition C of LCO 
3.7.2.

09/26/2022 ~ 19:00: The subsequent Operating crew on the next shift identified the loss of safety function 
during routine review of operator logs; this review determined the condition existed for approximately 9 
minutes. The condition was entered into the corrective action program.  
 
This condition is being reported in accordance with 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(D) as a condition that could have 
prevented fulfillment of a safety function. 
 
CAUSE OF EVENT 
The cause of the event was determined to be inadequate performance of a loss of safety function 
determination by the Operator. The review failed to identify a loss of safety function resulting from concurrent 
inoperability of both divisions of Core Spray, Division 1 of Core Spray for planned maintenance and Division 
2 of Core Spray due to performance of a surveillance. This cause was attributed to human performance. 
 
ANALYSIS/SAFETY SIGNIFICANCE 
Based on engineering analysis of the event, Unit 2, Division 2 Core Spray could have performed its safety 
function for this condition.  The Unit 2, Division 2 core spray room temperatures were evaluated with two core 
spray pumps in operation without room coolers. Assuming the worst case, bounding scenario of complete 
rupture of the TBCCW/RBCCW piping with the ESW isolation valves open, the resulting Core Spray room 
temperatures would not have exceeded functionality limits. Therefore, Core Spray could have fulfilled its 
safety function.  
 
This event will not be counted as a safety system functional failure for the NRC performance indicator based 
on the engineering analysis supporting the system’s ability to fulfill the safety function. There were no actual 
consequences to the health and safety of the public as a result of this event. 
 
CORRECTIVE ACTIONS 
1. Coaching was performed with applicable operators per the station accountability and engagement 
process procedure.  
2. Revoked licensed operator qualifications for applicable operators pending remediation. 
3. Perform focused observations of performance of loss of safety function determinations to assess Senior 
Reactor Operators proficiency levels in the task.

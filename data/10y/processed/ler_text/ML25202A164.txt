16. Abstract (Limit to 1326 spaces, i.e., approximately 13 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On 4/23/2025, Catawba Unit 1 was in Mode 1 operating at approximately 100 percent power. At 0248 hours, the 1B
Emergency Diesel Generator (EOG) was started for a 24 hour surveillance. Approximately 11 hours into the
surveillance a leak originated on the common fuel supply line under a compression fitting. The leak was estimated
to be greater than 0.5 gpm and at 1457 hours the 1 B EOG was secured and declared inoperable at 1459 hours.
Repairs were made and the 1B EOG was returned to service on 4/25/2025 at 0600 hours. Investigation revealed a
circumferential crack located within a tubing fitting. The crack originated prior to the attempted 24 hour surveillance
but was not through-wall and there was no indication of leakage. The crack propagated during the surveillance due
to high cycle fatigue. The existence of the crack, even though no leakage was present, prior to the 24-hour
surveillance is evidence that the 1B EOG was inoperable for a time longer than allowed by Technical Specifications.
This event is being reported pursuant to 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(i)(B) as operation or condition prohibited by Technical
Specifications. There was no impact to the health and safety of the public.

NARRATIVE
BACKGROUND
The following information is provided to assist readers in understanding the event described in this LER.
Applicable Energy Industry Identification System [EIIS] and component codes are enclosed within brackets.
Catawba's unique system and component identifiers are contained within parentheses.
The onsite standby power source for each 4160 volt Engineered Safety Features (ESF) bus at Catawba is a
dedicated EOG. For each unit, EDGs A and Bare dedicated to ESF buses ETA and ETB, respectively. Each
EOG starts automatically on a Safety Injection signal (i.e., low pressurizer pressure or high containment
pressure) or on an ESF bus degraded voltage or undervoltage signal. After the EOG has started, it will
automatically tie to its respective bus after offsite power is tripped as a consequence of ESF bus undervoltage
or degraded voltage, independent of or coincident with an SI signal. The EDGs will also start and operate in
the standby mode without tying to the ESF bus on an SI signal alone. Following the trip of offsite power, a
sequencer strips loads from the ESF bus. When the EOG is tied to the ESF bus, loads are then sequentially
connected to its respective ESF bus by the automatic load sequencer. In the event of a loss of preferred
power, the ESF electrical loads are automatically connected to the EDGs in sufficient time to provide for safe
reactor shutdown and to mitigate the consequences of a Design Basis Accident such as a Loss of Coolant
Accident (LOCA).
There are also provisions to accommodate the connecting of the Emergency Supplemental Power Source
(ESPS) to one train of either unit's Class 1E AC Distribution System. The ESPS consists of two 50% capacity
non-safety related commercial grade EDGs. Manual actions are required to align the ESPS to the station and
only one of the station's four onsite Class 1E Distribution System trains can be supplied by the ESPS at any
given time. The ESPS is made available to support extended Completion Times in the event of an inoperable
EOG as well as a defense-in-depth source of AC power to mitigate a station blackout event. The ESPS
remains disconnected from the Class 1 E AC Distribution System unless required for supplemental power to
one of the four 4.16 kV ESF buses.
The primary purpose of the Diesel Fuel Oil System (FD) [DC] is to store and supply fuel to the diesel
generators' engines for a period of operation of no less than seven days. Each diesel is equipped with two
45,000 gallon fuel oil storage tanks with a combined capacity sufficient to operate the diesel at full load for a
period of no less than seven days, plus a margin to allow for periodic testing. Fuel oil is transferred by gravity
from the storage tanks to the day tank which is located within retaining walls inside the Diesel Building. The
day tank capacity is sufficient to operate the diesel generator engine for a minimum of 60 minutes at full load.
The fuel oil is pumped from the day tank to the engine by an engine driven fuel oil pump. Excess fuel not
consumed by the engine is returned to the day tank via the fuel return line.

Technical Specifications:
Catawba TS 3.8.1 governs the DGs for each unit that is in Modes 1, 2, 3, and 4. Limiting Condition for Operation
(LCO) 3.8.1 (b) requires two operable DGs capable of supplying the Onsite Essential Auxiliary Power Systems.
With one LCO 3.8.1 (b) DG inoperable, the inoperable DG must be restored to operable status within 72 hours
per Required Action 8.6. LCO 3.8.1 (d) requires two DGs from the opposite unit necessary to supply power to the
shared systems and the Nuclear Service Water System (NSWS) pump(s). With one LCO 3.8.1 (d) DG
inoperable, the inoperable DG must be restored to operable status within 72 hours per Required Action D.6.
If an inoperable DG is not restored to operable status within 72 hours, the unit must be placed in Mode 3 within €
hours and in Mode 5 within 36 hours per LCO 3.8.1 Required Actions 1.1 and 1.2.
In order to extend the Completion Time for an inoperable DG from 72 hours to 14 days, it is necessary to ensure
the availability of the ESPS within 1 hour of entry into TS 3.8.1 LCO and every 12 hours thereafter.
EVENT DESCRIPTION
On 4/23/2025, Catawba Unit 1 was in Mode 1 operating at approximately 100 percent power. At 0248 hours, the
1B Emergency Diesel Generator (EOG) was started for a 24 hour surveillance. Approximately 11 hours into the
surveillance a leak originated on the common fuel supply line under a compression fitting. The leak was
estimated to be greater than 0.5 gpm and at 1457 hours the 1B EOG was manually secured and at 1459 hours
the 1B EOG was declared inoperable. Repairs were made and the 1B EOG was returned to service on
4/25/2025 at 0600 hours. Prior to the aborted 24 hour surveillance, the 1B diesel successfully performed an
operability test on 3/26/2025.
An analysis was performed to evaluate past operability for the 1B EOG, specifically reviewing the amount of
additional EOG operating time prior to complete fracture of the fuel supply line, fuel oil supply and demand and
the potential for the 1 B EOG to continue receiving a sufficient rate and volume of fuel with a fractured supply
line. This analysis concluded the 1B EOG would have continued to operate with a complete fracture of the fuel
line; however, the fuel storage available to support the seven day mission time of the 1B EOG would be
challenged. This analysis was finalized on 6/13/2025, starting the 60-day reportability clock for this LER.
This event is reportable under 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(i)(B) as any operation or condition which was prohibited by
the plant's Technical Specifications. Forensics determined a crack originated some time ago on the fuel supply
line and propagated during the attempted 24 hour surveillance, resulting in a fuel leak that would have
challenged the seven day mission time for the 18 EOG. This condition existed for a time period exceeding the
14-day allowed outage time for an inoperable EOG.

NARRATIVE
CAUSAL FACTORS
The affected section of tubing was inspected and subsequently removed and sent for metallurgical analysis. The
leak was caused by an outer diameter initiated through wall circumferential crack that extended approximately
300 degrees around the tube circumference within a compression fitting. The failure was attributed to high-cycle
fatigue, consistent with mechanical vibration occurring during engine operation. Beach marks and multiple arced
crack arrest lines on the fracture surface that emanated from the origin are indicative of multiple periods of active
crack growth aligning with periodic operation of the diesel. Oxidation along the beach marks at the origin sugges1
the crack initiated some time ago. While the investigation identified the crack initiated some time ago, the
location was hidden within the compression fitting, was not through wall prior to the surveillance, and there was
no previous indication of leakage. There is no history of maintenance at the fitting where the crack was located
and there was no operating history of leaks or cracks at this location on any emergency diesel generator at
Catawba.
The tubing configuration on the 1 B EOG introduced additional mechanical stress at the tubing to after-cooler wall
interface, creating a rubbing condition. While it is noted the leak was not at the location of the rubbing, it is
possible the vibration due to the tubing contact with the aftercooler wall was translated to the crack location.
An additional contributing factor to the crack was a loose horizontal clamp in close proximity to the fitting where
the crack was located. The exact impact of the clamp being loose cannot be determined nor how long the clamp
was loose. Catawba currently performs a 6-year preventative maintenance task to replace missing or broken
clamps as necessary. The last completion of this activity for the 1 B EOG was on 9/2/2020, with no issues noted.
Additionally, there are no specific preventative maintenance requirements for clamp inspection or replacement
per the Enterprise Preventative Program Manual.
CORRECTIVE ACTIONS
Immediate:
1. Remove and replace affected section of fuel oil tubing and perform metallurgical analysis on leak location.
2. Remove and replace similar location of fuel oil tubing for the opposite train ( 1A ) EOG and perform
metallurgical analysis.
Planned:
1. Reroute the 1B EOG fuel oil tubing to eliminate contact from the side of the aftercooler wall.
2. Revise preventative maintenance tasks to add additional instructions and criteria.
3. Remove and replace similar locations of fuel oil tubing for the 2A and 28 EOG and perform metallurgical
analysis.
SAFETY ANALYSIS
The fuel leak of the 1B Emergency Diesel Generator presents a reduction in the reliability and availability of the
Catawba Unit 1 emergency power system. There was no plant transient, radiological release, or other challenge
to normal plant operation to cause any impacts to the public health and safety.

NARRATIVE
Catawba Unit 1 has a considerable level of defense-in-depth besides having two DG trains. The ESPS provides
a means to restore AC power to the 1B Essential Bus (1 ETB) if the 1B DG failed. Similarly, another recovery
action is the ability to align offsite power from Unit 2 if available via the Shared Auxiliary Transformer SATB. The
Safe-Shutdown Facility (SSF) also provides an alternative means of maintaining safe shutdown conditions with
the SSF Diesel Generator powering the Standby Makeup Pump for reactor coolant pump (NCP) seal cooling and
instrumentation and controls for operation of Turbine-Driven Auxiliary Feedwater Pump. The final level of safety
is the ability to use FLEX equipment and strategies to provide alternative core cooling to prevent core damage.
With these factors taken together there was no impact to the health and safety of the public.

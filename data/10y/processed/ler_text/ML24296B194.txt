16. Abstract (Limit to 1326 spaces, i.e., approximately 13 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On May 02, 2024, the Prairie Island Nuclear Generating Plant (PINGP) Units 1 and 2 were operating at 100
percent power while performing fuel handling operations. For a 33-minute period between 12:52 and
13:25, radiation monitors 1R-37 and 2R-30 were both out of service while movement of irradiated fuel
assemblies was occurring. On July 11, 2024, while completing a review of the design and licensing basis
of radiation monitors 1[2]R-37, 1[2]R-30, it was determined that these monitors are required for Auxiliary
Building Special Ventilation System (ABSVS) operability during movement of irradiated fuel assemblies per
Technical Specification (TS) 3.7.12. Changes to fuel handling procedures have been initiated. TS Bases
for the ABSVS were further clarified and communicated to the operating crews. This will ensure
compliance with TS going forward when irradiated fuel is being moved.
This event is reportable under 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(i)(B) for an Operation or Condition Prohibited by
Technical Specifications.

NARRATIVE
Plant Operating Conditions Prior to the Event:
Unit 1 – Mode 1, 100 percent Power.
Unit 2 – Mode 1, 100 percent Power.
EVENT DESCRIPTION
On May 02, 2024, the Prairie Island Nuclear Generating Plant (PINGP) was performing fuel handling operations in the
Spent Fuel Pool area in support of a Cask loading campaign. For a 33-minute period between 12:52 and 13:25, Radiation
Monitors [IL] 1R-37 (Unit 1, Train A) and 2R-30 (Unit 2, Train B) were both out of service while movement of irradiated fuel
assemblies was occurring. This impacted the Auxiliary Building Special Ventilation System [VF] (ABSVS) and caused
both Train A and Train B of the ABSVS to be inoperable during the movement of irradiated fuel assemblies. The
implications of this condition were not known at the time.
Radiation monitors 1[2]R-37, 1[2]R-30 are vent gas monitors that monitor for potential radiological releases out of the
Auxiliary Building Normal Ventilation (ABNV) exhaust stacks. One of the safety functions of these monitors is to send an
auto-start signal to the ABSVS upon detecting high radiation. The Unit 1 ABNV exhaust stack is monitored by 1R-37
(Train A) and 1R-30 (Train B) while the Unit 2 ABNV stack is monitored by 2R-37 (Train A) and 2R-30 (Train B). The
ABSVS is a single system that serves both Unit 1 and Unit 2. The system consists of two redundant trains: Train A and
Train B. Actuation of either train of the ABSVS will isolate the ABNV exhaust stacks on both Units.
The high radiation signal from 1[2]R-37 and 1[2]R-30 is the credited ABSVS actuation signal during a Fuel Handling
Accident (FHA) event. With 1R-37 out of service (OOS), the A Train ABSVS would not receive this auto-start signal from
a release through the Unit 1 ABNV exhaust stack thus making Train A inoperable during the movement of irradiated fuel
assemblies. With 2R-30 OOS, the B train ABSVS would not receive this auto-start signal from a release through the Unit 2
ABNV exhaust stack thus making Train B inoperable during the movement of irradiated fuel assemblies.
Technical Specification (TS) Limiting Condition for Operation (LCO) 3.7.12 Auxiliary Building Special Ventilation System
(ABSVS) requires "Two ABSVS trains shall be OPERABLE" and is applicable in MODES 1, 2, 3 and 4 and "During
movement of irradiated fuel assemblies”. The radiation monitors only impact the operability of an ABSVS train during
movement of irradiated fuel assemblies.
There are no conditions in TS 3.7.12 that apply to two Trains of ABSVS being inoperable during movement of irradiated
fuel assemblies due to radiation monitors out of service. With no applicable action provided in Tech Spec 3.7.12, LCO
3.0.3 applied since LCO 3.0.3 establishes the actions that must be implemented when an LCO is not met and an
associated action is not provided. LCO 3.0.3 states that the unit shall be placed in a MODE or other specified condition in
which the LCO is not applicable and delineates the time limits for placing the unit in a safe MODE. However, since the
high radiation signal is only required for LCO 3.7.12 during movement of irradiated fuel, a unit shutdown would not provide
appropriate remedial measures. The appropriate action in response to two trains of ABSVS being inoperable during
movement of irradiated fuel assemblies due to radiation monitors out of service would be to immediately suspend the
movement of irradiated fuel assemblies, but that did not happen.
This event is reportable under 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(i)(B) for an Operation or Condition Prohibited by Technical
Specifications.

DATES AND APPROXIMATE TIMES OF MAJOR OCCURRENCES
October 19, 2023, 1R-37 Auxiliary Building Ventilation Gas Radiation Monitor was OOS due to a failed sample pump.
With 1R-37 OOS, the A Train ABSVS would not receive a start signal on high radiation out of the Unit 1 ABNV exhaust
stack. 1R-37 remained OOS for an extended period.
February 13, 2024, a change to the Technical Specification Bases was completed to clarify that the high radiation initiation
of ABSV is required to trip the ABNVS.
May 02, 2024 [Event Date] from 12:52 to 14:59, 2R-30 was OOS for calibration. During this time, B train ABSVS would
not receive a start signal on high radiation out of the Unit 2 ABNV exhaust stack.
May 02, 2024 movement of irradiated fuel took place from 0820 to 1325.
July 11, 2024, while completing a review of the design and licensing basis of radiation monitors 1[2]R-37 and 1[2]R-30, it
was identified that the issues with 1R-37 out of service may impact ABSVS operability per Technical Specification (TS)
3.7.12 while moving irradiated fuel assemblies.
July 26, 2024 [Discovery Date] a Past Operability Review (POR) was completed which reviewed the time periods when
movement of irradiated fuel occurred at the same time as one or more of the associated radiation monitors were out of
service to see if a past inoperability of the ABSVS occurred. The POR identified numerous occurrences when one or
more of the radiation monitors were out of service during movement of irradiated fuel.
August 13, 2024, a concern was raised questioning the conclusion of the POR. It was identified that the POR incorrectly
concluded operability of the ABSVS was maintained throughout these identified occurrences. A revision to the POR was
then initiated.
August 20, 2024, the site completed a revision of the POR. In this revision, the site correctly identified the periods of time
of inoperability of the ABSVS due to the associated radiation monitors being out of service. One of the instances identified
was a period when two radiation monitors were out of service impacting both Train A and Train B of the ABSVS. The
revised POR concluded that both Train A and Train B ABSVS were inoperable during movement of irradiated fuel
assembles on May 02, 2024 from 12:52 to 13:25 for a period of 33 minutes.
It was not recognized that LCO 3.0.3 was applicable for the condition the plant was in, and the appropriate action in
response to two Trains of ABSVS being inoperable during movement of irradiated fuel assemblies due to radiation
monitors out of service would have been to immediately suspend the movement of irradiated fuel assemblies, but that did
not happen.

ASSESSMENT OF SAFETY CONSEQUENCES
During this event, there was always a redundant radiation monitor capable of detecting a potential radiological release out
of either unit's exhaust stack which would have resulted in a Train A or Train B ABSVS actuation during a postulated FHA
depending on which unit's exhaust stack the high radiation was first detected. Actuation of either the Train A or Train B
ABSVS results in the elimination of both Unit 1 and Unit 2 ABNV exhaust stacks as potential radiological release sources.
This performs the safety function credited by the ABSVS during an FHA event.
The health and safety of the public and site personnel were not impacted during this event.
CAUSE OF THE EVENT
TS Bases were changed to clarify operability requirements of the ABSV system without ensuring the license holders
understood the changes to TS 3.7.12 Bases.
Operating procedures were not updated to include steps to evaluate TS 3.7.12 if radiation monitors were inoperable or out
of service.
CORRECTIVE ACTIONS
TS Bases for the ABSV system were further clarified and communicated to the operating crews.
Changes to fuel handling procedures have been initiated. This will ensure compliance with TS going forward when
irradiated fuel is being moved.

There were no previous similar events in the past three years.

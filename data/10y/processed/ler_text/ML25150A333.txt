16. Abstract (Limit to 1326 spaces, i.e., approximately 13 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On June 28, 2024 at 0110 CDT, Monticello Nuclear Generating Plant was in Mode 1 at 100 percent power and performing OSP-RHR-0556
“Residual Heat Removal (RHR) Water Fill Verification” Technical Specification Surveillance Procedure. When the RHR Low Pressure Coolant
I Injection (LPCI) Motor Valve MO-2012 "RHR Division 1 LPCI Injection Outboard Valve" was attempted to be cycled, the Outboard Motor
I Valve opened approximately one inch then stopped. With the Outboard Motor Valve incapable of opening, the A LPCI injection path was
inoperable for injection as an Emergency Core Cooling system; and due to the plant design of the LPCI Loop Select Logic, this rendered both
I subsystems of LPCI inoperable. Subsequent evaluation determined that the Outboard Motor Valve was considered inoperable when it was
I configured closed based on the potential to have Reactor pressure on the inboard side of the valve due to leakage past the associated RHR
I Division 1 LCPI Injection Inboard Motor Valve.
I The cause of the event was that the Outboard Motor Valve's motor pinion was not secured to the motor shaft because an incorrect-sized set
screw was installed. The shorter motor pinion set screw relaxed, allowing the motor pinion to move axially along the motor shaft until it no
longer engaged the worm shaft clutch gear.
I On June 29, 2024, the Outboard Motor Valve's motor pinion was adjusted back into position on the motor shaft. On August 7, 2024, the
I incorrect motor pinion set screw on the Outboard Motor Valve was replaced with the correct set screw.

EVENT DESCRIPTION
On June 28, 2024 at 0110 CDT, Monticello Nuclear Generating Plant (MNGP) was in Mode 1 at 100 percent power and
performing OSP-RHR-0556 “Residual Heat Removal (RHR) Water Fill Verification” Technical Specification Surveillance
Procedure. When the RHR Low Pressure Coolant Injection (LPCI) [EIIS CODE: BO] Motor Valve MO-2012 “RHR
I Division 1 LPCI Injection Outboard Valve” was attempted to be opened via the control room hand switch, the Outboard
I Motor Valve opened approximately one inch then stopped. With the Outboard Motor Valve incapable of opening, the A
LPCI injection path was inoperable for injection as an Emergency Core Cooling system; and due to the plant design of
the LPCI Loop Select Logic, this rendered both subsystems of LPCI inoperable. Specifically, if the B Recirc loop was
I determined to be broken, the automatic logic would be incapable of opening the path to the A LPCI injection path and
I neither division would automatically inject. Subsequent evaluation determined that the Outboard Motor Valve was
I inoperable when it was configured closed based on the potential to have Reactor pressure on the inboard side of the
I valve due to leakage past the associated Inboard Motor Valve MO-2014 "RHR Division 1 LCPI Injection Inboard valve."
This event is reportable per 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(i)(B) for a condition prohibited by Technical Specifications and 10 CFR
I
50.73(a)(2)(v)(B) and 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(D) for an event or condition that could have prevented the fulfillment of a
I
safety function of structures or systems that are needed to remove residual heat and mitigate the consequences of an
accident based on the safety function at the RHR LPCI system level. This issue was evaluated for a Safety System
Functional Failure (SSFF) in accordance with NEI 99-02. This event is a Safety System Functional Failure (SSFF) due to
meeting reporting criteria per 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(B) and 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(D).

EVENT ANALYSIS
On June 27, 2024 at 2158 CDT, Technical Specification 3.5.1 "ECCS - Operation" Condition D "Two LPCI subsystems
inoperable for reasons other than Condition C and G" was entered for performance of Technical Specification
Surveillance Procedure 0255-04-IA-1-2 “RHR Loop B Quarterly Pump and Valve Tests.” Prior to exiting Condition D of
I Technical Specification 3.5.1, OSP-RHR-0556 was performed. On June 28, 2024 at 0110 CDT while the Outboard
I Motor Valve was attempted to be opened via the control room hand switch, the Outboard Motor Valve opened
approximately one inch then stopped, resulting in the inability to exit Condition D of Technical Specification 3.5.1. The
plant had no other Structures, Systems, or Components that were inoperable at the start of the event that contributed to
the event.
While this was later determined to be incorrect, initial troubleshooting identified that the most likely cause of the
I Outboard Motor Valve failure to stroke was the clutch mechanism was disengaged partially from the motor pinion as a
I result of foreign material from failure of the tripper spring. The Outboard Motor Valve was repaired and satisfactorily
retested, then Condition D of Technical Specification 3.5.1 was exited on June 29, 2024 at 1533 CDT.
I
I During performance of the root cause evaluation interviews, it was identified that during the Outboard Motor Valve
repairs performed on June 29, 2024, the motor pinion was found extended on the motor shaft and prior to reassembly, it
was adjusted back into position. The vendor technical manual specified that the motor pinion should have a set screw
and lock wire installed, so it should not have been able to move.
I Further physical investigation on the Outboard Motor Valve and specifically the motor pinion was performed on August
I 7, 2024. The Outboard Motor Valve was successfully stroked open and closed, then the motor pinion was found to be
held in place with a set screw and lock wire, with no movement along the motor shaft. The set screw was removed for
inspection, and it was determined to be the incorrect set screw. Additionally, indications on the motor shaft revealed
that motor pinion had potentially moved axially in the past. A review of past maintenance records did not identify a time
where the set screw had been replaced.
An incorrect set screw would have allowed the motor pinion to travel axially along the motor shaft and eventually
disengage with the worm shaft clutch gear. When the motor pinion disengaged with the worm shaft clutch gear, the
I Outboard Motor Valve could not be operated via the control room hand switch which starts the motor actuator.
I On April 03, 2025, the determination was made that the Outboard Motor Valve should have been considered inoperable
I when it was configured closed with the incorrect motor pinion set screw and had the potential to have Reactor pressure
I on the inboard side of the valve due to leakage past the Inboard Motor Valve. With the Outboard Motor Valve
I incapable of opening, the A LPCI injection path was inoperable for injection as an Emergency Core Cooling system;
I and due to the plant design of the LPCI Loop Select Logic, this rendered both subsystems of LPCI inoperable.
I Technical Specification 3.5.1 Condition D requires restoration of one LPCI subsystem to Operable status within 72
hours. When Condition D is not met, Condition F requires entry to Mode 3 within 12 hours. Therefore, the plant was in
I
I a condition prohibited by Technical specifications in the following time frames:
I • October 22, 2023 at 0207 CDT through November 15, 2023 at 1611 CST.
I • April 18, 2024 0255 CDT through June 29, 2024 at 1533 CDT.
The plant was in a condition that could have prevented the fulfillment of a safety function in the following timeframes:
I
• October 18, 2023 at 1407 CDT through November 15, 2023 at 1611 CST.
I
• April 14, 2024 at 1455 CDT through June 29, 2024 at 1533 CDT.

ASSESSMENT OF SAFETY CONSEQUENCES
I Actual safety consequences of the event were very low for both normal operation and design basis event operation.
I High Pressure Emergency Core Cooling System (ECCS), the Low Pressure ECCS subsystem of Core Spray, and the
I remaining functions of RHR including Drywell Spray and Suppression Pool Cooling were not affected. A probabilistic
I risk assessment was performed to estimate the impact of the unavailability of the Outboard Motor Valve on Core
I Damage Frequency and Large Early Release Frequency. The results were within the bounds of "Very Low Safety
I Significance" described in NRC Inspection Manual Chapter 0609. There were no radiological or industrial impacts
associated with this event. The health and safety of the public and site personnel were not impacted during this event.
CAUSE OF THE EVENT
I The Outboard Motor Valve failed to operate because the motor pinion was not secured to the motor shaft because an
incorrect-sized set screw was installed. The shorter motor pinion set screw relaxed, allowing the motor pinion to move
axially along the motor shaft until it no longer engaged the worm shaft clutch gear.
CORRECTIVE ACTIONS
I On June 29, 2024, the Outboard Motor Valve’s motor pinion was adjusted back into position on the motor shaft. On
I August 7, 2024, the incorrect motor pinion set screw on the Outboard Motor Valve was replaced with the correct set
screw.
PREVIOUS SIMILAR EVENTS
There were no previous similar events in the past three years.

16. Abstract (Limit to 1326 spaces, i.e., approximately 13 single-spaced typewritten lines}
On October 22, 2025, at 1535, the Clinton Unit 1 reactor was manually scrammed due to low turbine Main Electro-Hydraulic
Control oil system (Main EHC) reservoir level caused by a leak that had developed on Turbine Control Valve #3 (TCV3).
This event is reportable in accordance with 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(iv)(A) for "Any event or condition that resulted in manual or
automatic actuation of any of the systems listed in paragraph (a)(2)(iv)(B) of this section." The specific system listed in that
paragraph that actuated was the "Reactor protection system (RPS) including: reactor scram or reactor trip."

NARRATIVE
CONDITION PRIOR TO THE EVENT
Unit: 1 Event Date: October 22, 2025, Event Time: 15:35 CST
Mode: 1 Mode Name: Power Operation Reactor Power: 86%
EVENT DESCRIPTION
On October 22, 2025, 1504, operators observed abnormal valve conditions (main turbine bypass valves open and TCV3
indicated closed) and initiated off-normal procedures to lower power in response to unexpected loss of feedwater heating.
Main EHC reservoir level was observed to be dropping rapidly (approximately 1 inch/minute), prompting an immediate
operator action to makeup reservoir level. Due to the large leak being un-isolable and Main EHC fluid level reaching the
low reservoir level in which Main EHC pumps are required to be secured, a manual scram was inserted at 1535 with the
reactor at 86% power. Final Main EHC fluid reservoir level was -24.5" and an estimated 440 gallons of EHC fluid leaked
during this event.
CAUSE OF THE EVENT
The apparent cause of the Main EHC leak was a failure of four tie rods associated with TCV3 due to high cyclic fatigue as
a result of inadequate torquing during the most recent refurbishment by the vendor.
Post-SCRAM inspections identified significant mechanical damage to the TCV3 actuator, including a dislodged backing
plate, and sheared four of the eight tie rods. Two of the remaining four tie rods were found to be under-torqued (one finger
tight, the other at under 200 ft-lbs). The TCV3 actuator has since been replaced to correct the source of the leak. During
the extent of condition investigation, Turbine Control Valve #1 (TCV1) actuator tie rods failed an initial resonance test and
were replaced to preclude a similar failure. While preparing to install the replacement actuator for TCV1, the tie rods on the
new actuator were torque-tested and three were found to be under torqued at 350 ft-lbs rather than the prescribed torque
of 500 ft-lbs. In addition, the lower O-ring was visible in the groove when observed with a borescope from inside the
cylinder. When properly torqued, the lower O-ring was no longer visible.
SAFETY CONSEQUENCES
There were no safety consequences impacting plant or public safety as a result of this event. The trip was not complex,
with all systems responding normally post-trip. Decay heat was removed post-trip by discharging steam to the Main
Condenser using the Turbine Bypass Valves which have a separate electro-hydraulic oil system. There was no loss of any
safety function that would have prevented fulfillment of actions necessary to 1) shutdown the reactor and maintain it in a
safe shutdown condition, 2) remove residual heat, 3) control the release of radioactive material, or 4) mitigate the
consequences of an accident.

NARRATIVE
CORRECTIVE ACTIONS
The station completed replacement of the TCV3 and TCV1 actuators, as well as revised the procurement requirements to
require a Certificate of Conformance from the vendor regarding tie rod torquing requirements for these types of
components. A Corrective Action Program Evaluation (CAPE) Investigation was conducted to further investigate the
cause.
PREVIOUS OCCURRENCES
A review of Licensee Event Reports for the past five years identified no previous similar occurrences at Clinton Power
Station.

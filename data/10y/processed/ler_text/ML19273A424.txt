ABSTRACT (Umit to 1400 spaces, i.e., approximately 15 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On June 23, 2019, with Units 1 and 2 at 100% power, the packing gland on the 1B Emergency Service Water Pump
(ESWP) failed during periodic pump testing and the pump was declared inoperable. The packing gland was repaired and
the pump was returned to service on June 24, 2019. During the next scheduled pump test on July 21, 2019, another
packing failure occurred. Subsequent investigation revealed that both packing failures were caused by the angle drive to
pump shaft not being aligned when the pump was replaced in May 2019. The decision making was not appropriate in that
only first line supervisors were consulted before making the decision not to per:form the alignment. The decision should
have been elevated to ensure a broader group of personnel were involved in the decision.
The ESWP 1 B to angle drive alignment was performed in accordance with procedures and returned to service.
Additionally, corrective actions will include a revision to the maintenance procedure and other changes focusing on
. improving proficiency and decision making.
This report is submitted pursuant to 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(i)(B) due to operations prohibited by Technical Specifications.
Based on a risk assessment, the risk impact was determined to be of very low significance.

NARRATIVE
1.0 DESCRIPTION OF THE EVENT
Three diesel-driven vertical Emergency Service Water Pumps (ESWPs) [EIIS-81-P] are provided at the James River
intake structure [EIIS-KE-NN] to deliver service water (SW) makeup to the intake canal [EIIS-KE-NN] in the event of a
total loss of station power coincident with a design basis Loss of Coolant Accident (LOCA). The three (3) ESWPs
serve both Surry units. Two of three ESWPs provide sufficient heat removal for the design basis LOCA unit and the
non-LOCA unit. Technical Specification (TS) 3.14.A.4 states that when the reactor coolant system temperature or
pressure exceeds 350 degrees For 450 psig, three ESWPs shall be operable. TS 3.14.B allows one ESWP to remain
inoperable for a period not to exceed 14 days.
On April 29, 2019, ESWP 1B was tagged out in preparation for pump replacement. The pump was replaced without
performing an angle drive to pump alignment and on May 4, 2019, the pump was started for its return to service run.
During this run, multiple vibration points were elevated. The source of these elevated vibrations were determined to be
resonance and did not impact operability. On May 7, 2019, the pump was successfully returned to service. On May 8,
2019, grouting was installed under the pump pit plate and on May 28, 2019, the pump was tested with results showing
that the vibrations had decreased indicating the grout changed the resonance frequency of the angle drive to pump
shaft assembly.
On June 23, 2019, during periodic testing, ESWP 1 B was secured due to abnormal sounds after pump start. The
pump was declared inoperable as a result of a failure of the pump packing gland. The failure was attributed to
loosening of the packing gland follower clamping bolts during pump operation at elevated vibration levels. Following
repacking, application of a liquid threadlocker to the pump gland follower clamping nuts, and successful testing, the
pump was declared operable on June 24, 2019.
During the next scheduled pump test on July, 21, 2019, ESWP 1 B was again secured and declared inoperable due to a
packing failure. Subsequent investigation showed that the pump shaft and angle drive were misaligned resulting in
excessive shaft movement, stuffing box bushing wear, and packing failure. On July 25, 2019, the ESWP 1 B angle
drive to pump shaft were aligned and the pump declared operable.
It was determined that the prior packing failures were caused by angle drive to pump misalignment which occurred in
May 2019. At three (3) separate times during this period of ESWP 1 B inoperability (i.e., from May 7 to July 25), ESWPs
1A and 1 C were separately removed from service for scheduled maintenance, but at no time was either pump unable
to be returned to operable status within the 2-hour time-critical operator action starting time should a design basis
event have occurred. As a result there was not a loss of safety function.
ESWP 1 B was determined to be unable to perform its design function since the time that the pump was replaced and
as a result exceeded its 14-day TS allowed outage time. In addition, the period of time ESWP 1A and 1 C were
separately removed from service resulted in exceeding the TS 3.0.1 criterion. Therefore, this report is submitted
pursuant to 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(i)(B) as an operation or condition prohibited by Technical Specifications.
2.0 SIGNIFICANT SAFETY CONSEQUENCES AND IMPLICATIONS
The risk associated with the ESWP 1 Bout-of-service condition is very low since the likelihood of a design basis
accident coincident with loss of off-site power and a challenge to intake canal cooling water volume is extremely
unlikely. Additionally, ESWP 1A and 1C were available during the period ESWP 1B was inoperable. Therefore, the
impact of the condition was very low, and there were no safety consequences because of this event.

3.0 CAUSE OF THE EVENT
This event was caused by the angle drive to pump shaft not being aligned when the pump was replaced in May 2019.
The decision making was not appropriate in that only first line supervisors were consulted before making the decision
not to perform the alignment. The decision should have been elevated to ensure a broader group of personnel
(technical experts in vertical pump alignment and their leaders) were involved in the decision.
4.0 IMMEDIATE CORRECTIVE ACTIONS
ESWP 1 B pump to angle drive alignment was performed in accordance with station procedures. The pump was tested
and returned to service on July 25, 2019.
5.0 ADDITIONAL CORRECTIVE ACTIONS
Although ESWPs 1A and 1C have been in service for multiple years since their last replacement, they were inspected
for extent of condition. Results showed the packing gland follower bolts/nuts were tight with no visual signs of damage.
Additionally, satisfactory vibration levels and a history of successful performance runs, without gland follower damage
or packing extrusion, indicated that no significant misalignment existed between the pump and angle drives.
6.0 ACTIONS TO PREVENT RECURRENCE
The maintenance procedure will be revised to provide additional guidance and require an angle drive to pump
alignment when the angle drive is removed.
Other actions focusing on improving proficiency and decision making for the groups involved will be implemented by
the Corrective Action Program.

16. Abstract (Limit to 1326 spaces, i.e., approximately 13 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On January 4, 2025 at 0855 EST, with Unit 1 and Unit 2 at 100% rated thermal power, both trains of the Control Room
Emergency Air Temperature Control System (CREATCS) were simultaneously inoperable. This condition occurred when
the A-A Main Control Room (MCR) chiller experienced an oil migration event and tripped on oil failure while the B train of
CREATCS had been previously declared inoperable for a B-B MCR chiller maintenance outage. The A train of CREATCS
was later restored at 1408 EST on January 4, 2025 following the addition of MCR chiller oil, adjustment of the chiller’s
temperature control valve (TCV) and subsequent observation of stable chiller parameters. Applicable technical
specification (TS) limiting conditions for operability (LCO) were entered for less than six hours as a result.
This condition is being reported in accordance with 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(D) as a condition that could have prevented
fulfillment of a safety function needed to mitigate the consequences of an accident.

I. Plant Operating Conditions before the Event
Watts Bar Unit 1 and Unit 2 were at 100 percent Rated Thermal Power (RTP) before the event.
II. Description of Event
A. Event Summary
On January 4, 2025 at 0855 EST, with Unit 1 and Unit 2 at 100% rated thermal power, both trains of
the Control Room Emergency Air Temperature Control System (CREATCS){EIIS:VI} were
simultaneously inoperable. This condition occurred when the A-A Main Control Room (MCR) chiller
experienced an oil migration event and tripped on oil failure while the B train of CREATCS had been
previously declared inoperable for a B-B MCR chiller maintenance outage. The A train of CREATCS
was later restored at 1408 EST on January 4, 2025 following the addition of MCR chiller oil,
adjustment of the chiller’s temperature control valve (TCV){EIIS:TCV}, and subsequent observation of
stable chiller parameters. Applicable technical specification (TS) limiting conditions for operability
(LCO) were entered for less than six hours as a result.
This condition is being reported to the Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) in accordance with
10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(D) as a condition that could have prevented fulfillment of a safety function
needed to mitigate the consequences of an accident.
B. Status of structures, components, or systems that were inoperable at the start of the
event and that contributed to the event
The B-B MCR chiller had been removed from service for a maintenance outage when the A-A
MCR chiller trip occurred.

H. Operator actions
Operators ensured that the chiller restarted with maintenance personnel present to perform oil
additions and necessary adjustments.
I. Automatically and manually initiated safety system responses
There were no automatic or additional manual safety system responses.
III. Cause of the event
A. Cause of each component or system failure or personnel error
The most likely cause of the A-A MCR chiller trip was a low oil level within the sump due to oil
migration to the chiller’s evaporator. Oil migrated during low load conditions which produced
low compressor discharge pressures and an overall low refrigerant flow velocity. The low
sump oil level caused a loss of oil pressure and a subsequent oil failure trip.
B. Cause(s) and circumstances for each human performance related root cause
No human performance issues were attributed to this event.
IV. Analysis of the event
The CREATCS provides temperature control for the control room following isolation of the control
room. The CREATCS consists of two independent and redundant trains.
On January 4, 2025 at 0855 EST both trains of the CREATCS were simultaneously inoperable. This
condition occurred when the A-A Main Control Room (MCR) chiller experienced an oil migration event
and tripped on oil failure while the B train of CREATCS had been previously declared inoperable for a
B-B MCR chiller maintenance outage. The A train of CREATCS was later restored operable at 1408
EST after maintenance personnel added oil to the chiller system, adjusted the chiller’s Metrex TCV,
and operators observed stable parameters. Both Trains of MCR temperature control (CREATCS)
were lost for less than six hours. The temperature within the MCR was maintained below the design
limit.

V. Assessment of Safety Consequences
Both Trains of MCR temperature control (CREATCS) were lost for less than six hours. The
temperature within the MCR was maintained below the design limit of 104 degrees Fahrenheit.
A. Availability of systems or components that could have performed the same function as
the components and systems that failed during the event
There are no other systems that would perform the function of CREATCS.
B. For events that occurred when the reactor was shut down, availability of systems or
components needed to shutdown the reactor and maintain safe shutdown conditions,
remove residual heat, control the release of radioactive material, or mitigate the
consequences of an accident
Not Applicable.
C. For failure that rendered a train of a safety system inoperable, estimate of the elapsed
time from discovery of the failure until the train was returned to service
The A train CREATCS was returned to service in approximately 5.22 hours.
VI. Corrective Actions
This event was entered into the TVA Corrective Action Program and is being tracked under
Condition Report 1982838.
A. Immediate Corrective Actions
Oil was added to the MCR chiller system and the associated Metrex TCV was adjusted to
maintain a discharge pressure between 225-235 psig.
B. Corrective Actions to Prevent Recurrence or to reduce the probability of similar events
occurring in the future
Operator rounds instructions were updated with new monitoring values to ensure the
discharge pressure of the chiller system is maintained within acceptable bands. Associated
chiller preventive maintenance instruction revisions are planned to update discharge
pressures. Systems Engineering is also monitoring chiller oil levels and discharge pressures
to ensure further TCV adjustments are not required.

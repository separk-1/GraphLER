Abstract (Limit to 1400 spaces, i.e., approximately 14 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On February 19, 2019, at 1307 EST, with the reactor at 100 percent Core Thermal Power and steady state conditions, plant
personnel notified the Main Control Room that both doors in the Secondary Containment Airlock on the Reactor Building Fifth
Floor were opened and could not close. The Airlock Doors were opened simultaneously for a period of approximately five
minutes (i.e., from 1253 to 1258 EST). A technician had opened both air lock doors simultaneously when he egressed through
first door and grabbed the handle of the second airlock door and inadvertently pushed the door open before the opposing door
had closed. The interlock device for the doors had failed. The interlock is designed to prevent both doors from being opened
simultaneously. Technical Specification (TS) Surveillance Requirement (SR) 3.6.4.1.3 was not met. The maximum Secondary
Containment (SC) pressure observed during that time remained within TS limits. There were no radiological releases associated
with this event. Declaring SC inoperable as a result of not meeting TS SR 3.6.4.1.3 is reportable under 10CFR50.72(b)(3)(v)(C)
and this written report is required under 10CFR50.73(a)(2)(v)(C) as an event or condition that could have prevented the
fulfillment of a safety function needed to control the release of radioactive material. The doors were closed in approximately 5
minutes and SC was restored.

DESCRIPTION OF THE EVENT
On February 19, 2019, at 1307 Eastern Standard Time (EST), with the reactor in steady state operations, the main control
room was notified that both airlock doors [[DR]] for the Secondary Containment (SC) [[NH]] on the reactor building fifth
floor were opened. The airlock doors were opened simultaneously for a period of approximately five minutes (i.e., from
1253 to 1258 EST). A technician had opened both air lock doors simultaneously when he egressed through first door and
grabbed the handle of the second airlock door and inadvertently pushed the door open before the opposing door had
closed. The airlock door locking pins became stuck once the doors were simultaneously opened preventing either door ,
from being closed. The interlock [[IEL]] device for the doors had failed. The interlock is designed to prevent both doors
from being opened simultaneously. Technical Specification (TS) Surveillance Requirement (SR) 3.6.4.1.3 was not met
with both doors opened. The locking pins were released and airlock doors were closed after approximately 5 minutes.
The airlock and secondary containment were restored. The maximum SC pressure observed during that time remained
within TS limits for SC operability.
There were no radiological releases associated with this event. Declaring SC inoperable was a result of not meeting TS
SR 3.6.4.1.3. The condition was reported to the NRC Operations Center per event notification number 53888 under
10CFR50.72(b)(3)(v)(C) and this written report is required under 10CFR50.73(a)(2)(v)(C) as an event or condition that
could have prevented the fulfillment of a safety function needed to control the release of radioactive material.
SIGNIFICANT SAFETY CONSEQUENCES AND IMPLICATIONS
There were no safety consequences or radiological releases associated with this event. At no time during this event was
there a potential for endangering the public health and safety.
The SC reactor and auxiliary building fifth floor airlock maintains one door closed when traversing into or out of the reactor
building fifth floor. A negative pressure is maintained in the reactor building and portions of the auxiliary building.
Radioactive materials remain in the auxiliary building and reactor building and any release of radioactive material is
monitored for radiation before exhausting to the environment during normal operation. When both air lock doors were
open for approximately 5 minutes on February 19, negative pressure was maintained in SC and the auxiliary building.
The SC vacuum remained within the TS operability limit of greater than or equal to 0.125 inches of vacuum water gauge
per TS Surveillance Requirement (SR) 3.6.4.1.1. Therefore, there was no unmonitored release during the event.
CAUSE OF THE EVENT
The technician turned the auxiliary building fifth floor door handle and inadvertently pushed on the auxiliary building door
prior to the reactor building door being closed and latched. The technician didn't recognize the risk of turning the handle
prior to verifying the opposing door being closed and latched and relied on the interlock to hold the reactor building door
closed. The reactor building fifth floor airlock door did not latch and the airlock doors interlock failed, allowing the auxiliary
building fifth floor door to open while the reactor building fifth floor door still was open. The interlock device failed.

CORRECTIVE ACTIONS
Contacted Security and Operations to reset pins and close door. Performed a walk down of the area. The
airlock doors are now posted as "Emergency Exit Only" until the reactor building and auxiliary building door
interlock is repaired.
A Human Performance Department Reset was issued as a result of this event. The event briefing sheet was presented to
the Radiation Protection department staff. Radiation Protection Management communicated to the staff the importance of
ensuring the first door is closed prior to opening the second door, and not challenging the interlock.
The interlocking device is being reviewed. When the appropriate design or adjustment is determined, the interlock will be
repaired.
PREVIOUS OCCURRENCES
The negative pressure in SC was maintained by Reactor Building Heating Ventilation and Air Conditioning (RBHVAC)
[[VA]] and remained within the TS requirement during this event. There are no previous occurrences that involved the
human performance error of simultaneous opening of both air lock doors.

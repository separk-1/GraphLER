Abstract (Limit to 1400 spaces, i.e., approximately 14 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On 7 /11 /19 a pin hole leak was identified on the 18-inch distribution piping of the C Spray Network while the spray network was
in operation for Spray Pond cooling. Subsequent NOE inspections identified wall thinning over a large arc of the piping's
circumference; approximately 34 feet of piping required replacement. On 08/25/19, the extent of condition inspection of the A
Spray Network identified similar corrosion damage. The condition was determined to be reportable for a condition prohibited by
Technical Specifications and a common cause inoperability of independent trains. All four Spray Networks were inspected, and
corrosion damage was identified on all four of the Spray Networks' 18-inch distribution piping.
Although the 18-inch spray network distribution piping was degraded, the spray networks and the ultimate heat sink were
determined to be functional. The cause of the pipe corrosion damage observed on the 18-inch distribution piping of each spray
pond spray network was flow-induced film removal accompanied by high humidity electrochemical atmospheric corrosion. This
condition was not identified prior to the piping becoming inoperable because there was a lack of understanding of this potential
failure mechanism. The affected piping was replaced and an NDE inspection and proactive pipe replacement strategy will be
established to ensure long term reliability of the above ground spray pond spray network piping.

Unit Condition Prior to the Event
Limerick Generating Station (LGS) Units 1 and 2 were both operating in Operational Condition (OPCON) 1 at 100 percent
power at the time of the event. There were no other structures, systems, or components inoperable at the time of the
event that contributed to the event.
Description of the Event
On 7/11/19 a pin hole leak was identified on the 18-inch diameter distribution piping of the C Spray Network while the
spray network was in operation for Spray Pond cooling. Subsequent NOE inspections identified wall thinning over a large
arc of the piping's circumference; approximately 34 feet of the 18-inch diameter piping required replacement. With this
condition present, the spray network was successfully operating and performing its intended function.
The Spray Pond [BS] serves both units at Limerick Generating Station (LGS) and is designed to supply and cool the
safety-related cooling water for the Emergency Service Water (ESW) [CC] and the Residual Heat Removal Service Water
(RHRSW) [CC] systems. The Spray Pond contains four spray networks. Each network contains 240 spray nozzles with all
the piping and spray nozzles above the water level supported by concrete pillars. Only one spray network is required for
the safe shutdown of one unit and any two networks provide sufficient cooling to safely shut down both units.
During the piping preparation for Non-Destructive Examination (NOE) additional through-wall piping pin holes were
identified. Due to the number and tight physical spacing between defects, ASME Code Case N-513 could not be applied
to this condition to provide an interim operability justification. Subsequently, the C RHRSW spray pond spray network was
isolated and declared inoperable on 07 /13/19 and Limerick entered an emergent 30-day dual unit Tech Spec LCO. The
degraded condition on the east side of the supply manifold was corrected and the LCO was exited on 07/30/19. An extent
of condition plan was developed to perform NOE on the remaining three spray pond spray networks.
On 08/26/19 the A Spray Network was removed from service and the LCO was entered for an extent of condition
inspection. On 08/27/19 during piping preparation for NOE, pin holes were identified on the east side of the 18-inch
distribution piping at the same location as the C Spray Network piping. The piping corrosion damage was identified on the
18-inch distribution piping on both the east and west sides. The piping wall thickness readings on the east side were
below the minimum required thickness; however, the piping wall thickness readings on the west side were above the
required minimum thickness. This condition was determined to be reportable for a condition prohibited by Technical
Specifications and common cause inoperability of independent trains. The east side 18-inch distribution piping was
replaced and the LCO was exited on 09/07/19.
On 09/10/19 the B Spray Network was removed from service and the LCO was entered for the extent of condition
inspection. No pin holes were identified; however, NOE inspection identified similar corrosion damage on the 18-inch
distribution piping on both the east and west sides. The piping wall thickness readings on the east side were below the
minimum required thickness; however, the piping wall thickness readings on the west side were above the required
minimum thickness. The east and west side 18-inch distribution piping was replaced and the LCO was exited on
09/21/19.
On 09/23/19 the D Spray Network was removed from service and the LCO was entered for the extent of condition
inspection. No pin holes were identified; however, NOE inspection identified similar corrosion damage on the 18-inch
·distribution piping on both the east and west sides. The piping wall thickness readings on the east side were below the

Analysis of the Event
Each spray pond spray network is fed water by a single 30-inch diameter pipe. A tee divides the flow into two (east and
west side) 18-inch diameter sections. This manifold distributes all the water to the spray nozzles by seven individual 10-
inch diameter piping headers equipped with spray nozzle trees, that contain four spray nozzles per tree. The NOE
identified degradation in the 18-inch piping; no significant degradation was identified in the 30-inch or 10-inch piping. The
A and C Spray Networks exhibited specifically oriented, localized wall thinning and through-wall pin holes on th~ 18-inch
distribution piping while the B and 0 Spray Networks exhibited specifically oriented, localized wall thinning, but no
pinholes.
The apparent cause of the pipe corrosion damage observed on the 18-inch distribution piping of each spray pond spray
network was flow-induced film removal accompanied by high humidity electrochemical atmospheric corrosion. The
required standby alignment for the spray pond spray networks results in the piping normally being drained and open to the
atmosphere. When the system is placed in service, the air from the network piping and the subsequent flow oscillations,
driven by the piping geometry, results in removal of any scale and corrosion products formed during the time the piping
was out of service. When the system is not in service the newly cleaned pipe surface is exposed to the atmosphere in
high humidity conditions. The corrosion footprint in the areas of excessive pipe corrosion and through-wall defects
matches the corrosion footprint of excessive atmospheric corrosion.
NOE inspection of spray pond spray network piping has previously been performed on the A and 0 Spray Networks at
thirteen locations on each spray network. The intent was that the inspection results would be representative of all spray
network piping. NOE inspection was last performed in June 2011. Two of the thirteen inspection locations were located
on 18-inch distribution piping and the NOE results for these inspections identified reasonably healthy piping with no
evidence of the excessive focused wall thinning. However, only localized scanning (spot readings) at the top, bottom and
sides of piping was performed.
LGS has experienced pin hole leaks in other RHRSW and ESW piping and is proactively replacing portions of the piping
throughout the plant. The previous efforts to prioritize RHRSW and ESW pipe replacements determined the Spray
Network piping was low risk. This was based on limited NOE data that indicated no significant corrosion issues and a lack
of understanding of the corrosion mechanism.
Safety Consequences
Although the piping was degraded, the spray networks and the ultimate heat sink were considered functional. Routine
operation of the spray networks demonstrates that the spray networks remained capable of performing their function
under non-faulted conditions. The faulted conditions described in the UFSAR (seismic events and tornado (excluding
missiles)) are not postulated to occur coincident with a design basis accident.
The as-found NOE data for the A Spray Network was used to perform a realistic structural integrity assessment of the
piping. This assessment concluded that none of the stresses resulting from the faulted events exceeded the ASME code
allowable stress values. This evaluation supports the judgment that the piping would remain intact and not result in a

Paper tracings and photographs of the through wall flaws of the A Spray Network piping were used to perform an
assessment to determine if the flaws could, under faulted loading conditions, connect and result in localized material
yielding and formation of hairline type slits that would cause additional external leakage. The conclusion from this
assessment was this could occur in a few locations, but the size of any newly formed hairline slits would be tolerable and
not result in a significant amount of bypass leakage that would adversely impact the heat removal function of a spray
network. Slightly less than 140 gallons per minute (gpm) was the computed bypass leakage which is well within analyzed
limits.
Although not expected or anticipated, the consequences the postulated loss of some or all the Spray Pond spray networks
due to tornado missiles is specifically addressed in the LGS UFSAR. The Limerick Spray Pond spray networks have no
protection against tornado generated tornado missiles. The UFSAR and existing plant special event procedures provide
remediating actions to successfully address and mitigate loss of spray networks. These actions could also be utilized as a
mitigation strategy for any of the postulated external events given the long-time frames involved in a scenario with
degraded spray pond spray networks.
Cause of the Event
The cause of the pipe corrosion damage observed on the 18-inch distribution piping of each spray pond spray network
was flow-induced film removal accompanied by high humidity electrochemical atmospheric corrosion.
The condition was not identified prior to the piping becoming inoperable because there was a lack of understanding of this
failure mechanism.
Corrective Actions
•All inoperable 18-inch diameter distribution piping was replaced.
• Complete the Extent of Condition inspection on the C Spray Network.
• Establish NOE inspection and proactive pipe replacement strategy to ensure long term reliability of the above ground
spray pond spray network piping.
• Enhance current NOE inspection strategy for ESW and RHRSW piping to proactively inspect pipe segments with limited
NOE inspection history.
Previous Similar Occurrences
Prior to the C Spray Network piping through wall leak identified on 07/11/19, all RHRSW System and ESW piping through- .;-,-.,..
wall leaks and minimum wall thickness issues have occurred on piping that is normally filled with water. LGS has
extensive operating experience dating back to early plant life indicating that corrosion of ESW and RHRSW piping that is
normally filled with water is characterized as general corrosion with more severe localized attacks (pitting) resulting from
under deposit corrosion influenced by micro-biologically induced corrosion (MIC).

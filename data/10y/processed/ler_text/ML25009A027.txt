I.    DESCRIPTION OF EVENT: 
 
A. PRE-EVENT PLANT CONDITIONS: 
 
Prior to the event, NMP2 was in Mode 1 operating at 100% power. 
 
B. EVENT: 
 
On 9/23/2024 failure of the Main Generator Field Circuit Breaker (41M-2EXSX01) [EXC] lead to a Generator Field Loss of 
Excitation [TL]. This resulted in a Main Generator Lockout which caused a Turbine Trip [TA] and automatic anticipatory 
RPS actuation [JC] at 07:20. High resistance across Main Generator Field Circuit Breaker (41M-2EXSX01) caused a large 
voltage drop across the breaker main contacts. The Auto Voltage Regulator (AVR) [90] control system responded to the 
lower excitation through the feedback and increased output to compensate. The increased output resulted in increased 
voltage drop, heat at the contacts, a subsequent fire, and Loss of Field Excitation. The resulting Turbine Trip and Turbine 
Stop and Control Valve closure caused an automatic anticipatory RPS actuation. 
This event is reportable in accordance with 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(iv)(A). 
 
C. INOPERABLE STRUCTURES, COMPONENTS, OR SYSTEMS THAT CONTRIBUTED TO THE EVENT: 
 
None. 
 
D. DATES AND APPROXIMATE TIMES OF MAJOR OCCURRENCES: 
 
September 23, 2024: 
 
07:20: All Condensate Booster pumps, the ‘A’ Feedwater pump, and the ‘C’ Feedwater (FWS) Pump, tripped. In addition, 
the A and B recirc pumps tripped from fast speed. This is all indicating that a slow transfer occurred. It was verified that 
generator output breaker R925 and R230 auto opened as expected due to the generator lockout relay 86-1-2SPGZ01 
actuating (Reference EE-001B and N2-OP-68). Reactor water level decreased below Level 2 (L2, 108.8") reaching 98.5” 
due to the trip of the ‘A’ and ‘C’ Feedwater pumps. This resulted in multiple Primary Containment Isolation System (PCIS) 
isolations. Groups 2, 3, 6, 7, 8, and 9 isolated as expected per N2-OP-83. The L2 actuations also caused Reactor Core 
Isolation Cooling (RCIC), High Pressure Core Spray (HPCS) and Division 3 diesel generator to auto start, as designed. 
RCIC and HPCS injected to restore level above L2. The Reactor Building (RB) ventilation system isolated and Standby 
Gas Treatment (SBGT) and Control Room Emergency Filtration (CREF) systems started as designed. These are all 
expected responses for reaching a L2. Reactor pressure reached a peak of 1102 psig. Reactor pressure was controlled by 
the turbine bypass valves. (Reference IR 04804263) 
 
07:20: HPCS and RCIC were used to control reactor water level between 160” and 200”. 
 
07:38: Operations received report from RP tech that they see and smell smoke on the Turbine Building Elevation 306'. 
Operator was dispatched to investigate. 
 
07:39: Operations reported visible flames on the Main Generator Field Circuit Breaker. Operator attempted to extinguish 
the fire using portable CO2 extinguishers.

E. METHOD OF DISCOVERY: 
 
This event was self-revealed when the Turbine Control Valve Fast Closure signals actuated RPS ‘A’ and ‘B’, resulting in 
unit scram. 
 
F. SAFETY SYSTEM RESPONSES: 
 
All safety systems responded per design. 
 
II.    CAUSE OF EVENT: 
 
The cause of the event was a mechanical fault internal to the breaker caused a high resistance connection to develop 
ultimately resulting in loss of the generator field. 
 
The internals of the breaker melted during the event destroying evidence. The internal damage 
prevented the identification of a specific sub-component failure including potential process, or 
programmatic causes associated with the sub-component maintenance, construction, or testing. 
 
III.    ANALYSIS OF THE EVENT: 
 
The scram did not have any impact to the health and safety of the public. All safety systems responded per design. 
 
NMP2 tripped due to increased resistance in the contact of the ‘A’ (-) phase of the 41M field breaker. The increased 
resistance caused the breaker to burn further degrading the contacts, which increased the resistance until it dropped the 
generator excitation. This was identified by generator output voltage drop. The feedback loop from the AVR increased 
current to recover the generator field which caused the output voltage to increase and generator output voltage to recover.

The increased current further increased temperature which also increased resistance on the ‘A’ (-) phase breaker 
contacts. The feedback loop between resistance increase, generator field voltage drop, AVR current control, and breaker 
contact temperature, continually increased temperature and degraded the breaker contacts. The AVR continued to 
respond until it hit a max current limit at which point it could not improve generator field voltage although field voltage 
continued to degrade. This resulted in the loss of the generator field. This tripped the 40-1-2SPGZ02 relay based on 
settings calculated in EC-167 which takes into account generator reactive capability. This in turn initiated the 
86-1-2SPGZ01 relay which triggered the generator lockout sequence and resulted in the turbine trip and SCRAM.    
 
IV.    CORRECTIVE ACTIONS: 
 
A. ACTION TAKEN TO RETURN AFFECTED SYSTEMS TO PRE-EVENT NORMAL STATUS: 
 
During the N2F47 forced outage the station replaced the 41M-2EXSX01 Main Generator Field Breaker and cubicle (panel) 
assembly (W/O C93993280). 
 
B. ACTION TAKEN OR PLANNED TO PREVENT RECURRENCE: 
 
Preventive Maintenance program changes for the breaker have been implemented with thermography performed every 
two weeks and monitoring of generator field voltage and current. 
The Corrective Action to Prevent Recurrence is to modify the generator field circuity to remove the generator field breaker. 
This will remove the component and cubicle and prevent a similar failure mechanism.

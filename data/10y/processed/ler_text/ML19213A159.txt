Abstract (Limit to 1400 spaces, i.e., approximately 14 single-spaced typewritten lines)
At 21:27 Eastern Daylight Time (EDT) on June 12, 2019, during routine testing on Unit 2, the High Pressure Coolant
Injection (HPCI) turbine experienced an overspeed trip. Following the overspeed trip, the turbine coasted down and
reset, then restarted and rolled to rated conditions as-designed. As a result, the response time of the system exceeded
the 60-second acceptance criteria, thereby rendering the system inoperable. Reactor Core Isolation Cooling (RCIC)
System and Automatic Depressurization System (ADS) remained operable during this event.
Failure of Unit 2 HPCI to meet the 60 second response time acceptance criteria was caused by a momentary failure of
the Electric Governor – Remote (EG-R) to control HPCI Turbine speed during the initial turbine roll. The EG-R Actuator
was replaced and the testing was repeated successfully with the response time acceptance criteria met. Historical data
was reviewed on Unit 1 and it was confirmed that this condition was isolated to Unit 2.
The safety significance of this event is minimal. There was no impact on the health and safety of the public or plant
personnel. This event is being reported in accordance with 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(D).

Background
Initial Conditions
At the time of the event, Unit 2 was in Mode 1 (i.e., Power Operation), at approximately 100 percent of rated
thermal power (RTP). There was no inoperable equipment that contributed to the event.
Reportability Criteria
The High Pressure Coolant Injection (HPCI) [BJ] inoperability is being reported in accordance with 10 CFR
50.73(a)(2)(v)(D) as a condition that could have prevented the fulfillment of the function needed to mitigate
the consequences of an accident. The event was initially reported to the NRC on June 13, 2019 (i.e., Event
Number 54116).
Event Description
At 21:27 Eastern Daylight Time (EDT) on June 12, 2019, during routine testing, the Unit 2 High Pressure
Coolant Injection (HPCI) turbine experienced an overspeed trip. Following the overspeed trip, the turbine
coasted down and reset, then restarted and rolled to rated conditions as-designed. As a result, the response
time of the system exceeded the 60-second acceptance criteria, thereby rendering the system inoperable.
Event Cause
Failure of Unit 2 HPCI to meet the 60 second response time acceptance criteria was caused by a momentary
failure of the Electric Governor – Remote (EG-R) to control HPCI Turbine speed during the initial turbine roll.
Safety Assessment
There was no adverse impact on the health and safety of the public. The safety significance of this event is
minimal. The event occurred during testing, and Reactor Core Isolation Cooling (RCIC) System and
Automatic Depressurization System (ADS) remained operable during this event. Though the HPCI system
did not meet the surveillance requirement response time, the system was available to perform its safety
function of injecting the required flow rate into the Reactor Pressure Vessel.
Corrective Actions
The following corrective actions were completed.
• On June 15, 2019, the EG-R Actuator was replaced and the testing was repeated successfully with
the response time acceptance criteria met.
• Historical data was reviewed on Unit 1 and it was confirmed that this condition was isolated to Unit 2.
This action was complete on June 13, 2019.

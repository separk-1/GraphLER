Abstract (Limit to 1400 spaces, i.e., approximately 14 single-spaced typewritten lines)
At 04:02 Eastern Daylight Time (EDT) on March 25, 2019, an actuation of the four Emergency Diesel Generators
(EDGs) occurred. Unit 1 was in Mode 1 at approximately 100 percent power and Unit 2 was in Mode 4 at 0 percent
power. Unit 2 was in the process of aligning the electrical distribution system to power the emergency buses via the Unit
Auxiliary Transformer (UAT) when a main generator differential lockout relay actuated; thereby starting the EDGs. The
emergency buses remained energized from offsite power via the Startup Auxiliary Transformer (SAT) and, therefore, the
EDGs did not tie to their respective buses. The EDGs responded per design to this event.
The cause of event was a historical design change error. During replacement of the original Main Power Transformers
(MPTs), the fact that the new transformers have lower second order harmonic in-rush current was not identified and, as
a result, appropriate protective relay settings were not implemented.
Corrective actions include revising operating procedures to preclude EDG automatic starts on Main Generator primary
lockout when restoring the UAT backfeed.

Background
At the time of the event, Unit 1 was in Mode 1 (i.e., Power Operation), at approximately 100 percent rated
thermal power (RTP). Unit 2 was in Mode 4 (i.e. Cold Shutdown), at 0 percent RTP. There was no
inoperable equipment that contributed to the event.
Reportability Criteria
This event is reportable per 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(iv)(A) because the plant experienced a valid start of its four
Emergency Diesel Generators (EDGs) [EK]. The event was initially reported to the NRC on March 25, 2019
(i.e., Event Number 53955).
Event Description
At 04:02 Eastern Daylight Time (EDT) on March 25, 2019, an actuation of the EDGs occurred. Unit 2 was in
the process of aligning the electrical distribution system to power the emergency buses via the Unit Auxiliary
Transformer (UAT) [EA] when a main generator differential lockout relay actuated; thereby starting the
EDGs. The emergency buses remained energized from offsite power via the Startup Auxiliary Transformer
(SAT) and, therefore, the EDGs did not tie to their respective buses. The EDGs responded per design to this
event. Troubleshooting and testing confirmed that the lockout was not a result of an actual fault condition or
degraded equipment.
Event Cause
The direct cause of this event was a Main Generator primary lockout that occurred when generator breaker
Power Circuit Breaker (PCB) 29A was closed during the alignment of the Unit 2 electrical distribution system
to power the emergency buses via the UAT. The lockout occurred due to a trip of differential relay
2-GEN-87GT-2-A. The cause of event was a historical design change error.
The function of the differential relay is to identify a fault inside the zone of protection by comparing the
currents flowing into the zone with the currents flowing out of the zone. If the difference is greater than a set
percentage, the relay will actuate. The 87GT-2 relay compares the sum of the currents through PCBs 29A
and 29B with the sum of the currents through the main generator and UAT. It is primarily designed to detect
a fault in the main power transformer but the zone of protection also includes portions of the isophase bus
and 230kV system. A difference in these currents indicates a fault. In the event, PCB 29B and the no-load
disconnect switch were open, so the relay was comparing the current flow through PCB 29A with the current
flow into the UAT.
When system voltage is applied to a transformer at a time when normal steady-state flux is at a different
value from that existing in the transformer, a current transient occurs, known as magnetizing in-rush current.
BSEP uses HU-1 relays for the differential protection. These relays have a differential unit, a harmonic
restraint unit, and an indicating instantaneous trip unit. The differential unit and harmonic restraint unit
contacts are in series. Per the vendor manual, the differential unit may or may not close its contact during

current is at least 15 percent of the fundamental component. Thus, prevention of spurious trips on in-rush is
reliant on the in-rush current containing at least 15 percent second harmonics, which may not be the case
with the relatively new main transformers. The original Main Power Transformers (MPTs) [EL] were
replaced in 2004 for Unit 1 and 2005 for Unit 2. The harmonic restraint settings for the differential relays
were not reduced to account for the lower second order harmonic in-rush current associated with new
transformers.
The actual second harmonics of the event were at the point where the harmonic restraint on the differential
relay could have actuated and blocked the trip (i.e., approximately 15 percent). Given the various
tolerances inherently associated with electromechanical relays and sensing devices, it was concluded that
the harmonics were not high enough to actuate the harmonic restraint and block the trip. This conclusion is
supported by the relative age of the MPTs and the UATs. Had the harmonic restraint settings for the
differential relays been reduced when the new transformers were installed, this event would not have
occurred.
Safety Assessment
The safety significance of this event is minimal. The emergency buses remained energized from offsite
power during the event and the EDGs responded per design to this event.
Corrective Actions
The corrective actions below are currently planned. Any changes to the corrective actions and schedules
noted below will be made in accordance with the site’s corrective action program.
• Revise procedures 1(2)OP-50, "Plant Electrical System Operating Procedure," to disable the EDG
automatic start on Main Generator primary lockout prior to restoring UAT backfeed. This action is
currently scheduled to be completed by October 3, 2019.
Previous Similar Events
A review of events for the past three years identified the following previous similar event where re-alignment
of the electrical distribution systems resulted in actuation of the EDGs.
• Event Notification (EN) 52778, completed on May 31, 2017, reported an invalid actuation of the EDGs
occurred when powering the 2C balance-of-plant (BOP) bus from the SAT. The event was a result of
failure to install flexible links between the SAT and the 2D BOP bus. The corrective actions associated
with EN 52778 could not have reasonably been expected to prevent the condition reported in
LER 1-2019-001.

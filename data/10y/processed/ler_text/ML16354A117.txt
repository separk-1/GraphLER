On October 26, 2016, Units 1 and 2 were in Mode 1 at 100 percent rated thenTJal power. Engineering personnel
determined that the Turbine Driven Auxiliary Feedwater (TDAFW} Pump exhaust vent and the Condensate Storage Tank
(CST) vents were not adequately protected from tornado generated missiles. Subsequent evaluations on November 29,
2016 resulted in the determination that the Atmospheric Relief Valves (ARVs} off the Main Steam Lines were also not
adequately protected from tornado generated missiles.
Operations subsequently declared the affected components inoperable, implemented Enforcement Guidance
Memorandum (EGM} 15-002, "Enforcement Discretion for Tornado-Generated Missile Protection Noncompliance" and the
required compensatory measures, and then declared the affected equipment operable but non-conforming.
These tornado missile vulnerabilities existed since the original plant construction. Immediate compensatory measures
include implementation of the severe weather procedures if a tornado is anticipated via a tornado watch or warning,
notification to Maintenance and Operations departments that tooling is onsite for removal of these components should they
become damaged from a tornado generated missile, and immediate inspection of these components following an event
that could have generated missiles. Additional comprehensive corrective actions and permanent elimination of the
vulnerabilities are planned in accordance with EGM 15-002.

NARRATIVE
A. Plant Operating Conditions Before the Event:
Unit 1, Mode 1, 100 percent power
Unit 2, Mode 1, 100 percent power
No plant transients are associated with this event. Thus, no Structures, Systems, or Components (SSCs) were
inoperable at the start of this event which contributed to this event.
Background NRC Documents:
Enforcement Guidance Memorandum (EGM) 15-Q02, "Enforcement Discretion for Tornado-Generated Missile Protection
Noncompliance," provides guidance to exercise enforcement discretion when an operating power reactor licensee does
not comply with a planfs current site-specific licensing basis for tornado-generated missile protection. Specifically,
discretion would apply to the applicable Technical Specification (TS) Limiting Condition(s} for Operation (LCO) that would
require a reactor shutdown or mode change in the event a licensee could not meet TS LCO required action(s) within the
TS completion time.
Interim Staff Guidance DSS-lSG-2016-01, "Clarification of Licensee Actions in Receipt of Enforcement Discretion
Per Enforcement Guidance Memorandum EGM 15-002," provides interim staff guidance to facilitate staff understanding of
expectations for consistent oversight associated with implementing enforcement discretion for tornado missile protection
noncompliance(s) per EGM 15-002.
Appendix A to DS5-ISG-2016-01 provides guidance for acceptable initial and comprehensive compensatory measures
for licensee use in implementing the enforcement discretion outlined in EGM 15-Q02. The licensee should declare (log)
the utilization of EGM 15-002, inform the resident inspector, and enter the issue into the corrective action program. For
initial compensatory measures, it is expected that the measures listed are already in place at sites that may be affected by
severe weather, such as tornados and/or hurricane force winds. The measures should be verified as current and readily
deployable within a very short timeframe.
B. Description of the Event:
On October 26, 2016, during the evaluation of protection of TS equipment from damage from tornado generated missiles,
Vogtle Site Engineering identified nonconforming conditions in the plant design such that the specified TS equipment did
not meet the current design basis for protection against tornado generated missile impacts. Three systems contained
equipment that were affected to the extent that an impact on operability was credible. The TDAFW Pump could be
rendered inoperable due to a tornado generated missile strike on the exhaust vent piping that extends outside of the
pump house. The CST vents could be rendered inoperable due to a tornado generated missile strike, as well as the
ARVs which extend above the roof of the main steam valve rooms. These SSCs were declared inoperable and EGM 15-
002 was invoked. Compensatory measures were implemented within the allowed time by the applicable LCOs and the
associated systems were declared operable but nonconforming.
This condition is being reported per the following criteria:
• 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2}(i}(B} for any condition that is prohibited byTS.
• 10 CFR 50. 73(a)(2)(v)(D) for any condition that could have prevented the fulfillment of a safety function of
structures or systems needed to mitigate the consequences of an accident

C. Cause of the Event:
Due to the Historical nature of this vulnerability, a spec~ic cause was not able to be identified.
D. Safety Consequences:
As documented in EGM 15-002, tornado missile scenarios that may lead to core damage are very low probability events
because safety-related SSCs are typically designed to withstand effects of tornados. For a tornado missile-induced
scenario to occur, a tornado would have to hit the site and result in the generation of missiles that would hit and fail
vulnerable, unprotected safety-related equipment, and/or unprotected safety-related subcomponents in a manner that is
non-repairable and non-recoverable. In addition, because plants are designed with redundancy and diversity, the tornado
missiles would have to affect multiple trains of safety systems and/or means of achieving safe shutdown.
The NRC has completed a generic risk analysis of potential tornado missile protection noncompliances to examine the risk
signifrcance of these scenarios. This assessment documents a conservative, bounding-type analysis of the risk sign~icance
for plant facilities. The generic analysis assumed that core damage would occur~ a tornado hit a plant located in the most
active tornado region in the country and that it caused a tornado-generated missile to fail all emergency core cooling
equipment at the plant with no ability to recover. Given this conservative assumption, it has been determine that the core
damage frequency (CDF} associated with tornado missile-related noncompliances are well below CDFs requiring
immediate regulatory action. In summary, the generic bounding risk analysis performed by the NRC concluded that this
issue is of low risk significance.
During a postulated design basis tornado, the conditions documented could have resulted in a loss of function for the
TDAFW pump, CSTs, or the ARVs. All these systems are used to mitigate the effects of postulated accidents by providing
an alternate path for core heat removal. No actual safety consequence occurred, as Vogtle did not experience an actual
tornado missile event Therefore, enforcement discretion until June 10 , 201 B, will not impose significant additional risk to
public health and safety.

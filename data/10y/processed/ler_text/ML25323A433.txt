16. Abstract (Limit to 1326 spaces, i. e., approximately 13 single-spaced typewritten lines) 
On July 31, 2025, at 1744, the Main Control Room (MCR), shared by Units 1 and 2, experienced a loss of ventilation 
due to the Control Room supply damper fully closing. Closure of the damper isolated the ventilation flow to the 
Control Room which resulted in both trains of the Control Room Emergency Ventilation System and Control Room 
Emergency Temperature System being inoperable, resulting in Technical Specification Limiting Conditions for 
Operation (LCOs) 3.7.8 and 3.7.9 not being met, respectively, for Units 1 and 2. At time 1745, the Units exited the 
associated LCOs after the damper returned to its normal, open position. Field troubleshooting isolated the cause of 
the intermittent closure of the damper to a MCR ventilation smoke detector. The smoke detector functions to shut 
the MCR supply damper by energizing the associated solenoid valve. This event is reportable in accordance with 10 
CFR 50. 73(a)(2)(v)(D) and 10 CFR 50. 73(a)(2)(vii). The direct cause of the smoke detector failure was an electrical 
short, due to condensation, on the control board of the Control Room Air Conditioning (A/C) return duct smoke 
detector (OAE5347) that sends an automatic closure signal to the Control Room A/C dampers.

B. DESCRIPTION OF EVENT 
On July 31 , 2025, at 1744, the Main Control Room (MCR), shared by Units 1 and 2, experienced a loss of 
ventilation due to the MCR supply damper fully closing. Closure of the damper isolated the ventilation flow to 
the MCR which resulted in both trains of the Control Room Emergency Ventilation System (CREVS) and 
Control Room Emergency Temperature System (CRETS) being inoperable, resulting in Technical Specification 
(TS) Limiting Conditions for Operation (LCO) 3.7.8 and 3.7.9 not being met, respectively, for Units 1 and 2. At 
time 1745 the Units exited the associated LCOs after the damper returned to its normal, open position.

The direct cause of the smoke detector failure was an electrical short, due to condensation, on the control board 
of the Control Room Air Conditioning (A/C) return duct smoke detector (0AE5347) that sends an automatic 
closure signal to the Control Room A/C dampers. 
A cause that led to the event was procedure use and adherence by engineering personnel. The previous July 
2018 occurrence resulted in actions to address the automatic function of the damper. Site procedures were not 
adhered to which resulted in untimely component Life Cycle Management process completion and inadequate 
closure of risk elimination actions. Key information was not entered into approved Risk Management and 
System Health management software. The software tool is periodically reviewed by senior leadership. The 
review would have provided the opportunity to increase priority of open actions. 
E. SAFETY ANALYSIS 
System Design 
The Control Room Envelope air conditioning system is divided into three supply and return duct systems: 
one for each Cable Spreading Room and one for the Main Control Room. A portion of supply and return air is 
also routed to the Control Room heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC) equipment room. Each 
supply and return branch contains an isolation damper. Smoke detectors are located in the return duct from 
each zone. In the event of a fire in one zone, the smoke detector automatically closes the corresponding 
isolation dampers. The air conditioning system will then continue to serve the other two zones without 
interruption. The Control Room HVAC Supply Damper (0-CV-5342) shuts on actuation of a smoke detector 
(0AE5347) or the operation of the associated hand-switch to CLOSE. There was no fire alarm at the time the 
damper was observed in the closed position, and the Control Room HVAC Supply Damper Hand Switch (0-
HS-5342) was in the OPEN position. 
Both trains of CREVS and CRETS being inoperable satisfies the criteria in NUREG-1022, Revision 3, for any 
event or condition that could have prevented the fulfillment of the safety function of structures or systems that 
are needed to mitigate the consequences of an accident and a common cause of inoperability. Therefore, this 
event is being reported pursuant to 10 CFR 50. 73(a)(2)(v)(D) and 50. 73(a)(2)(vii). 
No structures, systems, or components that were inoperable at the start of the event contributed to the event. 
The safety significance of the event was minimal due to the short duration of the event. MCR temperature and 
habitability were maintained during the event. 
F. CORRECTIVE ACTIONS 
Action(s) Completed: 
• 
The site has completed a permanent Engineering Change and associated 50.59 evaluation to 
permanently remove the automatic function of the smoke detector to close the MCR HVAC damper and 
credit a manual operator action to meet the credited fire safety function.

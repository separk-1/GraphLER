Abstract
On June 29, 2020 with Unit 1 In Mode 1 at.approximately 100 percent power and Unit 2 in Mode 1 at approximately 100 percent
power, McGuire detennined that Revision O of calculation MCC-1223.15-01-0015, Containment Floor and Equipment Sumps
Volume Calculation, contained non-conservative assumptions that would have prevented the containment floor and equipment
(CFAE) sump level monitors from detecting and alarming on a one gallon per minute input within one hour. Revision O of
calculation MCC-1223.15-01-0015 was approved for use on April 15, 1994.
Discovery of the non-conservative assumptions in Revision O of calculation MCC-1223.15-01-0015 resulted in a condition
prohibited by Technical Specification (TS) 3.4.15, Reactor Coolant System (RCS) Leakage Detection Instrumentation, since the
CFAE Sump Monitors for both Units was determined to be inoperable for longer than allowed by the TS 3.4.15 Limiting Condition
for Operation (LCO) action statements.
No causal analysis was performed due to the legacy nature of Revision O of calculation MCC-1223.15-01-0015.
An Engineering Change has been implemented to Insert the revised containment floor and equipment sump monitor rate of rise
set point, from revised calculation MCC-1223.15-01-0015, on the Operator Aid Computer.

BACKGROUND
The following infonnation Is provided to assist readers in understanding the event described In this LER. Applicable Energy
Industry Identification [EIIS] System and component codes are enclosed within brackets. McGuire Nuclear Station unique
system and component identifiers are contained within .parentheses.
n.
Liquid Waste Recycle [WO] (WL}, [LIT] (L Containment Floor and Equipment (CFAE;) Sump Level Monitors
The CFAE Sump Level Monitors function by taking the CFAE sump level change, rate•of rise, as measured by the level
transmitters (1/2WLLT5250 and 5260) and calculated by the Operator Aid Computer to provide indication in the Control Room
of possible leakage from Reactor Coolant System [AB] (NC), Main Steam System [SB] (SM), Main Feedwater System [SJ]
(FW), or Auxlliary Feedwater System [BA] (CA). The sump level monitor Is capable of detecting a 1 gpm leak In 1 hour. The
sensitivity Is based upon the ability of the sump level Indication to sense a change in level of 1/4 Inch and the following
assumptions
• Sump level Indication is between 5. 75 to 17 Inches
• Sump level indication moves in quarter-inch increments
• Operator Aid Computer rate alarm set at 0.0167 in/min
Operator Ald Computer [ID] (IKE)
The Operator Aid Computer System (OAC) at the McGuire Nuclear Station Is a non-QA system used to monitor parameters
and events in various plant systems and to provide data and alanns to the Control Room Operators and other station
personnel. The daw and alarms provided by the OAC are Important to the Control Room Operators' ability to perform routine
surveillances and other required activities during nonnal operation, as well as to respond quickly to changing plant conditions
during transients and abnormal events. The OAC perfonns the CFAE Sump Level Monitor function as described below.
CFAE Sump Level Instrumentation Is used to monitor changes in level due to Inputs and/or pump downs of the sump as
necessary. To convert the change in sump level values Into units that can be directly correlated to leakage, OAC algorithms
were developed. The algorithms are based on functional relationships (e.g. geometry and mathematics) and rate of change
transform calculations. The algorithm uses standard computer point types and predefined transform and logic functions on the
OAC to convert the level Instrumentation signals for comparison against a calculated threshold. The output of the algorithm Is
a displayed rate value for both sumps that is updated every minute on the OAC. The algorithm Includes rate alarm logic which
is processed every 5 seconds. The OAC alann threshold is selected to detect a total Jeakage rate change of 1 GPM within
one hour.

EVENT DESCRIPTION
On June 29, 2020 with Unit 1 in Mode 1 at approximately 100 percent power and Unit 2 in Mode 1 at approximately 100
percent power, McGuire determined that Revision O of calculation MCC-1223.15-01-0015, Containment Floor and Equipment
Sumps Volume Calculation, contained non-conservative assumptions that would have prevented the containment floor and
equipment (CFAE) sump level monitors from detecting and alarming on a one gallon per mim:Ite input within one hour.
Revision O of calculation MCC-1223.15-01-0015 was approved for use on April 15, 1994.
Discovery of the non-conservative assumptions in Revision O of calculation MCC-1223.15-01-0015 resulted In a condition
prohibited by Technical Specification (TS) 3.4.15, Reactor Coolant System (RCS) Leakage Detection Instrumentation, since
the CFAE Sump Monitors for both Units was determined to be Inoperable for longer than allowed by the TS 3.4.15 Limiting
Condition for Operation (LCO) action statements.
REPORTABILITY DETERMINATION
10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(I)(B) requires, in part, the licensee to report "Any operation or condition which was prohibited by the
plant's Technical Specifications ... ". As tl'le CFAE sump level monitors rate of rise set point was derived from Revision O
of calculation MCC-1223.15-01-0015 the containment floor and equipment sump level monitors have been inoperable
since April of 1994.
CAUSAL FACTORS
No causal analysis was performed due to the legacy nature of Revision O of calculation MCC-1223.15-01-0015.
CORRECTIVE ACTIONS
Immediate Action: ..
1. Operations declared the CFAE sump monitor on both Units Inoperable.
Interim Action:
1. Engineering revised calculation MCC-1223.15-01-0015. (Complete)
2. McGuire Implemented temporary OAC alarm on each Unit using the new containment floor and equipment sump
monitor rate of rise set point from revised calculation MCC-1223.15-01-0015 to return the monitors to OPERABLE.
{Complete)
Subsequent Action:
1. An Engineering Change has been Implemented to insert the revised containment floor and equipment sump monitor
rate of rise set point, from revised calculation MCC-1223.15-01-0015, on the Operator Aid Computer. (Complete)

SAFETY ANALYSIS
Safety Analysis
The safety impact associated with this issue ls negligible for the following reasons:
• Alternate means of detecting small leaks were available using the Cpntalnment Atmosphere Particulate Radioactivity
monitor, Ventilation Unit Condensate Drain Tank level indication, lncore Instrument Sump level Indication, along with
frequent containment sump level monitoring and RCS leak rate calculations. Because leakage into the containment
sump is typically very low, an abnormal RCS leakage of one gallon per minute would be quickly recognized. If the leak
rate was more than 1.5 gallons per minute, the CFAE sump level monitors would have gone Into alarm and performed
their function to alert Control Room operators.
• RCS leaks of this non•alarmlng range between 1.0 and 1.5 gpm are not risk significant in the McGuire PRA, as small
RCS leaks are well within the capacity of normal make-up pump flowrates and are not expected to challenge the
capability of plant systems to achieve an otherwise normal and safe plant shutdown.
As a result, the event Is of minimal safety significance since the alarm setpoint was only slightly non--conservative and other
diverse means of detection were available. Therefore, It is concluded that the impact of this issue had no impact on public
health and safety.

At 2137 EDT on 06/03/2025, while Unit 1 was operating at 98% power in MODE 1, the High Pressure Coolant Injection (HPCI)
turbine stop valve (TSV) failed to open as required during the testing of the auxiliary oil pump, resulting in the HPCI system
being declared INOPERABLE. The cause of the TSV failing to open was a degraded reset spring located in the trip knob
assembly of the valve.
The reset spring was replaced, the valve was tested satisfactorily, and the safety function was restored at 0115 EDT on
06/05/2025 when the HPCI system was declared OPERABLE. The Reactor Core Isolation Cooling (RCIC) and low pressure
Emergency Core Cooling Systems (ECCS) were OPERABLE during the time HPCI was INOPERABLE. However, because
HPCI does not have a redundant system, the failed TSV resulting in HPCI being INOPERABLE represents a condition that
could have prevented the fulfillment of the HPCI safety function to mitigate the consequences of an accident and is reportable
per 50.73(a)(2)(v)(D).

NARRATIVE
EVENT DESCRIPTION
At 2137 EDT on 06/03/2025, while Unit 1 was operating at 98% power in MODE 1, the High Pressure Coolant Injection
(HPCI) (EIIS: BJ) turbine stop valve (TSV) failed to open as required during the testing of the auxiliary oil pump, resulting in
the HPCI system being declared INOPERABLE. The safety function was restored at 0115 EDT on 06/05/2025 when HPCI
was declared OPERABLE. The Reactor Core Isolation Cooling (RCIC) System (EIIS: BN) and the low-pressure Emergency
Core Cooling Systems (ECCS) remained operable during the time the HPCI was INOPERABLE.
FAILED COMPONENT INFORMATION:
Master Parts List Number: 1 E41 F3053
Manufacturer: AMETEK
Model Number: 69-XC-103
Type: Turbine Stop Valve (TSV)
EVENT CAUSE ANALYSIS
The cause of the TSV failing to open was a degraded reset spring located in the trip knob assembly of the valve. The
degradation of the reset spring occurred because there was no preventive maintenance scheduled for replacing the spring.
As a result, the degraded reset spring lacked the necessary spring force to assist in opening the valve.
SAFETY ASSESSMENT
Even though HPCI was declared INOPERABLE, the RCIC System and the low-pressure ECCS remained OPERABLE
during the event. However, because HPCI does not have a redundant system, the failed TSV resulting in HPCI being
INOPERABLE represents a condition that could have prevented the fulfillment of the HPCI safety function to mitigate the
consequences of an accident and is reportable per 50.73(a)(2)(v)(D). There were no actual safety consequences as a result
of this event, and the operating crew responded correctly. The event was within the analysis of UFSAR Chapter 15.
CORRECTIVE ACTIONS
The reset spring was replaced, the valve was tested satisfactorily, and the safety function was restored at 0115 EDT on
06/05/2025 when the HPCI system was declared OPERABLE. Additionally, the HPCI maintenance procedure and the HPCI
twelve-year preventive maintenance overhaul are being revised to include the replacement of the reset spring. Also, the
HPCI surveillance procedure is being updated with a more conservative spring force acceptance criteria.

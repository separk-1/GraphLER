16. Abstract (Limit to 1326 spaces, i.e., approximately 13 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On February 8, 2025, at 2248 Central Standard Time, while at approximately 70 percent rated thermal
power, Unit 3 experienced a main generator trip causing an automatic reactor scram. Plant equipment
responded as expected and Unit 3 was transitioned to Mode 4. The Unit 3 isophase bus electrical fault
was most likely initiated by a combination of particulate buildup on standoff insulators and elevated
humidity. The root cause of the event was that the station failed to maintain an adequate maintenance
strategy for the bus duct system due to perceived system reliability.
Repairs were made to the main generator bus which allowed the reactor to restart and sync to the
electrical grid on March 6, 2025. Actions planned include revising the applicable Preventive Maintenance
items and procedure to inspect and clean all insulators and ensure proper grounding connections.

NARRATIVE
I. Plant Operating Conditions before the Event
At the time of this event, Browns Ferry Nuclear Plant (BFN) Unit 3 was in Mode 1 at approximately
70 percent Rated Thermal Power (RTP).
II. Description of Event
A. Event Summary
On February 8, 2025, at 2248 Central Standard Time (CST), while at approximately 70 percent
RTP, BFN Unit 3 experienced a main generator [EL] trip causing an automatic reactor scram [JC].
Plant equipment responded as expected and Unit 3 was transitioned to Mode 4.
During the scram response, there was a valid actuation of the Primary Containment Isolation
System (PCIS) [JM] Groups 2, 3, 6, and 8. Upon receipt of these signals, components actuated
as required. A loss of power to the 3EA and 3EB 4KV shutdown boards resulted in a valid
actuation of the 3A and 3B emergency diesel generators (EDGs) [EK] which successfully
re-energized the boards as designed.
The Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA) is submitting this report in accordance with Title 10 of the
Code of Federal Regulations (10 CFR) 50.73(a)(2)(iv)(A), as an automatic actuation of the
Reactor Protection System (RPS), the PCIS, and the EDGs.
B. Status of structures, components, or systems that were inoperable at the start of the
event and that contributed to the event
There were no structures, systems, or components whose inoperability contributed to this
event.

III. Cause of the event
A. Cause of each component or system failure or personnel error
The Unit 3 isophase bus electrical fault was most likely initiated by a combination of
particulate buildup on standoff insulators and elevated humidity. The root cause of the event
was that the station failed to maintain an adequate maintenance strategy for the bus duct
system due to perceived system reliability.
Contributing causes to this event were 1) roles and responsibilities for Engineering and
Maintenance were not clearly defined to ensure Doble Electromagnetic Interference (EMI) test
reports were reviewed and acted upon, and 2) original isophase bus duct design does not
allow access to all standoff insulators for inspection, cleaning, and replacement.
B. Cause(s) and circumstances for each human performance related root cause
N/A
IV. Analysis of the event
On February 8, 2025, the Browns Ferry Nuclear Plant Unit 3 main generator tripped on an
electrical fault within the isophase bus duct which led to a reactor scram.
V. Assessment of Safety Consequences
The plant responded as designed, while maintaining defense-in-depth for nuclear safety. All
nuclear safety systems functioned as designed. This event was of very low nuclear safety
significance. At no time was the health and safety of the public at risk.

This event did not occur when the reactor was shut down.

VI. Corrective Actions
Corrective Actions are being managed by the TVA corrective action program under condition
report 1990732.
A. Immediate Corrective Actions
Following repairs to the main generator bus, the reactor was restarted and synced to the
electrical grid on March 6, 2025.
B. Corrective Actions to Prevent Recurrence or to reduce the probability of similar events
occurring in the future
Root Cause:
 Scope will be added to the Preventive Maintenance items (PMs) for each unit to
inspect and clean insulators from the generator to the transformers (1990732-032 and
-033).
 The procedure (EPI-0-242-BUS001) used in each PM will be revised to reflect working
all insulators (1990732-034); provide location diagrams (1990732-038, -039, and -040)
of the insulators, links, and access hatches; add inspections of the airboxes for
evidence of partial discharge or arcing; and replace cleaning solvent LPS (brand
name), which leaves a residue, with isopropyl alcohol.
 Actions to ensure proper grounding connection and proper electrical isolation between
bus duct sections will be added to EPI-0-242-BUS001. (1990732-020, -021, -032,
-033, and -034).
Contributing Cause 1:
 The two-year PM that directs Doble EMI data gathering will be revised to ensure a CR
is initiated and a review performed for the final report (1990732-028).
Contributing Cause 2:
 During the Unit 2 2025 refueling outage, access hatches were installed on the Unit 2
isophase bus duct between the generator and the Generator Circuit Breaker (GCB)
allowing access to all insulators (1990732-014).
 A follow-up action will ensure access hatches are installed on the Unit 2 area between
the GCB and the transformers (1990732-015).

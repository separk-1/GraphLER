16. Abstract (Limit to 1326 spaces, i.e., approximately 13 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On November 2, 2025, at 1346 CST, LaSalle Station Unit 1 experienced a Unit Auxiliary Transformer (UAT) trip. A generator
lockout, turbine control valves fast closure, and reactor scram was automatically initiated by the plant in response to the trip. All
control rods fully inserted as expected; no Emergency Core Cooling Systems actuated or injected. Plant operators effectively
maintained reactor pressure and level.
I I
This event did not pose a threat to the health a~d safety of the public. This event was reported via Event No~ification #58018
under 1O CFR 50. 72(b)(2)(iv)(B) as an event that resulted in actuation of the Reactor Protection System when the Reactor was
critical.
The cause of the transformer trip was corrosion and inadequate divisional separation of test switches which allowed potential
unintended interaction between DC divisions within protective relay circuitry. Due to this underlying condition, ground
troubleshooting activities caused an inadvertent actuation of the UAT sudden pressure relay trip circuit, resulting in the Unit 1
scram.

NARRATIVE
Plant and System Identification
LaSalle Country Station Unit 1 is a General Electric Boiling Water Reactor with 3546 Megawatts Thermal Rated Core
Power. The affected system was the Reactor Protection System (RPS).
Condition Prior to Event
Unit(s): 1 Date: November 2, 2025 Time: 1346 CST
Reactor Mode(s): 1 Mode(s) Name: Power Operation Power Level: 100 percent
No systems, structures, or components that were inoperable at the start of the event contributed to the event.
Description
On October 29, 2025, the Main Control Room received an unexpected 125 VDC ground detector alarm. Troubleshooting
activities narrowed the ground location to the Unit 1 UAT trouble alarm circuitry. On November 2, 2025, test switches
were opened to isolate the previously identified ground. Opening the switches resulted in a generator lockout, UAT
deluge system actuation, turbine control valves fast closure, and automatic reactor scram initiated by RPS in response to
the trip. All control rods fully inserted as expected and no Emergency Core Cooling Systems actuated or injected. Plant
operators effectively maintained reactor pressure and level, and reset the deluge system following verification of no fire at
the transformer.
Investigation following the scram identified the UAT sudden pressure relay circuit had energized, however circuit
monitoring light indication and oil sample analysis concluded that sudden pressure conditions were not present to trigger a
legitimate actuation. Inspection of the UAT local control cabinet identified corrosion at the test switch blocks.
Cause
The cause of the transformer trip was corrosion and inadequate divisional separation of test switches which allowed
potential unintended interaction between DC divisions within protective relay circuitry. Due to this underlying condition,
ground troubleshooting activities caused an inadvertent actuation of the UAT sudden pressure relay trip circuit, resulting in
the Unit 1 scram.
Reportability and Safety Analysis
The safety significance of this event was minimal. All control rods fully inserted and all systems responded as expected to
the scram. Emergency Core Cooling Systems were not challenged. This event did not meet the NEI 99-02 definition of
an unplanned scram with complications. There were no safety system functional failures.
RPS actuation is reportable under 1© C FR 50. 72(b )(2)(iv)(B) as an event or condition that results lin the actuation of the
RPS when the reactor is critical. AnjENS report was made to the NRC at 1837 EST on Novemb9r 2, 2025 (EN #58018).
Corrective Actions
Replaced test switch blocks that had exhibited corrosion
Relocated test switches to provide divisional separation
Disabled sudden pressure relay logic trips of the UAT

16. Abstract (Limit to 1326 spaces, i.e., approximately 13 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On June 3, 2024, an increase in Browns Ferry (BFN) Unit 3 Drywell Unidentified Leakage was identified.
This condition was monitored and trended from June 3, 2024, to October 4, 2024. The unidentified
leakage came from two different sources. Prior to shutdown, total drywell unidentified leakage was
approximately 1.0 gallon per minute.
On October 5, 2024, at 0456, during the initial drywell entry of BFN Unit 3 Planned Outage 13 for
replacement of a suspected defective fuel bundle, the drone operator observed water leaking from the
elbow near the 1-inch sensing line off the 3B recirculation pump. In addition to this, the drone operator
also observed water leaking from near the 3A recirculation pump vent line on a 3/4-inch elbow.
The direct cause of the code class boundary leakage were cracks in two socket welds. The corrective
actions for the direct cause were to replace the small bore piping elbows. The apparent cause was
high-cycle fatigue. The corrective actions to address this are to implement modifications on the 3A and
3B Recirculation Pump Vent and Seal Vent Line Piping and sensing lines.

NARRATIVE
I. Plant Operating Conditions before the Event
At the time of discovery, Browns Ferry Nuclear Plant (BFN) Unit 3 was in Mode 4 at 0 percent
power.
II. Description of Event
A. Event Summary
On June 3, 2024, an increase in BFN Unit 3 Drywell Unidentified Leakage was identified. This
condition was monitored and trended from June 3, 2024, to October 4, 2024. The unidentified
leakage came from two different sources. Prior to shutdown, total drywell unidentified leakage
was approximately 1.0 gallons per minute (gpm).
On October 21, 2024, Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA) completed a Past Operability Evaluation
(POE). Technical Requirements Manual (TRM) Limiting Condition of Operability (LCO) 3.4.3,
Structural Integrity states that the structural integrity of American Society of Mechanical Engineers
(ASME) Code Class 1, 2, and 3 equivalent components shall be maintained in accordance with
Technical Specification Requirement (TSR) 3.4.3.1 and TSR 3.4.3.2 throughout the life of the
plant. Technical Specification (TS) 3.4.4 LCO (a) states that Reactor Coolant System (RCS)
operational leakage shall be limited to no pressure boundary leakage while in Modes 1, 2, and 3.
The structural integrity of the Reactor Coolant Pressure Boundary (RCPB) was inoperable for
both TRM LCO 3.4.3 and TS LCO 3.4.4 (a) due to the cracks in the 1-inch elbow on the high
pressure side sensing line for 3-PDT-068-0082 (BFN-3-VTV-068-0255) and the 3/4-inch
recirculation pump vent elbow weld, which are both ASME Code Class 2 pipes. The starting date
of inoperability is June 3, 2024, when Unit 3 showed an increase in Drywell Unidentified Leakage,
and the ending date is October 5, 2024, when Unit 3 shutdown for BFN Unit 3 Planned Outage 13
(F313) and the sources of the leaks were discovered.
The Tennessee Valley Authority is submitting this report in accordance with Title 10 of the Code
of Federal Regulations (10 CFR) 50.73(a)(2)(i)(B), as any operation or condition which was
prohibited by the plant’s Technical Specifications.

III. Cause of the event
A. Cause of each component or system failure or personnel error
The direct cause of the code class boundary leakage from the 3/4-inch and 1-inch elbows
were cracks in the socket weld, and the apparent cause was high-cycle fatigue. The primary
stresses driving the fatigue cracking were likely operating stresses exacerbated by uprates in
plant power and implementation of Maximum Extended Load Line Limit Analysis Plus
(MELLLA+) which resulted in changes in recirculation pump speed. These operating changes
increase vibrational loads and can cause pipes and systems to fall into resonant frequencies
of the area.

There were no human performance related causes.

IV. Analysis of the event
The Recirculation System [AD] is designed to provide forced coolant circulation through the
reactor core. Adjustment of the core coolant flow rate changes reactor power output which
provides a means of following plant load demand without adjusting control rods.
On October 5, 2024, the visual inspection of the weld interface between the elbow near the
BFN-3-VTV-068-0255 and weld interface on the BFN-3-SHV-068-6603, (VTV-68-6601 Shut Off
Valve) marked the time of discovery for this condition. The condition was first identified on June
3, 2024, when BFN Unit 3 showed an increase in unidentified leakage. The condition was
monitored and trended from June 3, 2024, to October 4, 2024.
V. Assessment of Safety Consequences
The safety significance of this event is low. The cracks in both the 1-inch Elbow on the High
Pressure Side Sensing Line for 3-PDT-068-0082 and the 3/4-inch Recirculation Pump Vent
elbow weld are bounded by two calculations. Calculation MDQ0999950033 Rev. 3, Exclusion
Criteria for In-Service Inspection (ISI) Scope, shows that the normal reactor coolant makeup
systems, Reactor Core Isolating Cooling (RCIC)[BN], Control Rod Drive (CRD)[AA], and
Feedwater (FW)[SJ] have adequate capacity to makeup reactor coolant in the worst-case
scenario of a small break loss of coolant accident (LOCA). Calculation NDQ0074880118 Rev.
8, Evaluation of Low Pressure Coolant Injection (LPCI)[BO] Flow to Reactor Pressure Vessel
(RPV) with Failed Open Min Flow Bypass Valve, shows that the LPCI mode of the Residual
Heat Removal (RHR)[BO] system can still meet the required LPCI flow to the RPV in the
worst-case scenario of a small break LOCA for the duration of its mission time.
Probabilistic Risk Analysis (PRA) personnel were notified of the condition and reported the
following results of likelihood of core damage frequency event (CDF) to be very small at
5.33E-8/year or once every 533,000,000 years. Similarly, the likelihood for a large early
release frequency (LERF) to be 6.88E-9/year or once every 6,880,000,000 years.
Based on the above, TVA has concluded that sufficient systems were available to provide the
required safety functions needed to protect the health and safety of the public.

A. Availability of systems or components that could have performed the same function as
the components and systems that failed during the event
The Reactor Recirculation System shall be designed so that failure of piping integrity does
not compromise the ability of the reactor vessel internals to provide a re-floodable volume.
The normal reactor coolant makeup systems, RCIC, CRD, and FW have an adequate
capacity to makeup reactor coolant in the worst-case scenario of a small break LOCA.
During the time period from June 3, 2024, until BFN Unit 3 was depressurized below
150 psig on October 5, 2024, at 0207, the BFN Unit 3 RCIC System was unavailable for
0.6 hours on July 31, 2024. The unavailability time was during the performance of
3-SR-3.5.3.3, RCIC System Rated Flow at Normal Operating Pressure. This period of
RCIC System unavailability was due to the closure of 3-FCV-071-0009, RCIC Turbine
Stop Valve, and the valving out of service of 3-FT-071-0036, RCIC Flow Transmitter, in
accordance with procedure 3-SR-3.5.3.3. The CRD system was available as a makeup
source during the time RCIC was unavailable. Also, a complete failure of the 3/4-inch
Recirculation Pump Line Vent Valve elbow weld or the 1-inch Elbow on the High Pressure
Side Sensing Line for 3-PDT-068-0082 would result in a high drywell pressure SCRAM
which would affect the capability of the FW system as a makeup water source.

components needed to shutdown the reactor and maintain safe shutdown conditions,
remove residual heat, control the release of radioactive material, or mitigate the
consequences of an accident
This event did not occur when the reactor was shut down.

VI. Corrective Actions
Corrective Actions are being managed by the TVA’s Corrective Action Program (CAP) under
Condition Reports (CRs) 1963793, 1963794, and 1965828.
A. Immediate Corrective Actions
The leak source documented by CR 1963793 was repaired by WO 124871621, and the other
leak source documented by CR 1963794 was repaired by WO 124871622.
B. Corrective Actions to Prevent Recurrence or to reduce the probability of similar events
occurring in the future
The following corrective actions have been completed or are planned:
1. Replace 3/4-inch small bore piping elbow located between the 3A Recirc Pump and
the Pump Vent Shutoff Valve.
2. Replace 1-inch small bore piping elbow located on the 3B Recirc Pump sensing line
(high pressure side) prior to the branch connection.
3. Implement a design change on 1A, 2A, 3A, 1B, 2B, and 3B Recirc Pump Vent and
Seal Vent Line Piping and sensing lines.
VII. Previous Similar Events at the Same Site
A review of the BFN Licensee Event Reports (LERs) for Units 1, 2, and 3 over the last four
years revealed three (3) previous, similar events. (LERs 259/2022-001-01, 260/2023-001-01,
and 296/2022-003-01).

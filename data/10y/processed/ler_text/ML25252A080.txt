16. Abstract (Limit to 1326 spaces, i.e., approximately 13 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On May 12, 2025, at 1353 Central Standard Time (CST), Browns Ferry Nuclear Plant, Unit 2, experienced a
significant electro-hydraulic control (EHC) leak, resulting in a turbine trip and automatic reactor SCRAM on low EHC
pressure. The trip was not complex with all systems responding normally post trip except for the turbine bypass
valves, which subsequently failed closed due to loss of EHC pressure. At 1407 CST, with Unit 2 in Mode 3, there
was a second automatic RPS actuation due to a low reactor water level transient caused by manually opening and
closing a main steam relief valve. Event Notification 57705 was made to the Nuclear Regulatory Commission
(NRC) Operations Center on May 12, 2025, at 1731 CST.
It was documented that three broken mounting cap screws were found. Failure analysis was completed on the
broken cap screws and determined that the cap screws failed due to hydrogen embrittlement. Investigation into the
source of hydrogen intrusion determined that there were two potential sources: environmental intrusion and pickling
during the manufacturing process.
The Tennessee Valley Authority is submitting this report in accordance with Title 10 of the Code of Federal Regulations
(10 CFR) 50.73(a)(2)(iv)(A), as any event or condition that resulted in manual or automatic actuation of the Reactor
Protection System and the Primary Containment Isolation System.

NARRATIVE
I. Plant Operating Conditions before the Event
At the time of discovery, Browns Ferry Nuclear Plant (BFN), Unit 2, was in Mode 1 at 100 percent
power.
II. Description of Event
A. Event Summary
On May 12, 2025, at 1353 Central Standard Time (CST), BFN Unit 2, experienced a significant
electro-hydraulic control (EHC)[JA] leak resulting in a turbine trip and automatic reactor SCRAM
on low EHC pressure. The trip was not complex with all systems responding normally post trip
except for the turbine bypass valves [V], which subsequently failed closed due to loss of EHC
pressure. Operations responded and stabilized the plant. At 1407, with Unit 2 in Mode 3, there
was a second automatic Reactor Protection System (RPS) actuation due to a low reactor water
level transient caused by manually opening and closing a main steam relief valve. Event
Notification 57705 was made to the Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) Operations Center.
The Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA) is submitting this report in accordance with Title 10 of the
Code of Federal Regulations (10 CFR) 50.73(a)(2)(iv)(A), as any event or condition that resulted
in manual or automatic actuation of the Reactor Protection System and the Primary Containment
Isolation System (PCIS).
B. Status of structures, components, or systems that were inoperable at the start of the
event and that contributed to the event
There were no structures, systems, or components (SSCs) whose inoperability contributed to
this event.

G. The failure mode, mechanism, and effect of each failed component
The mounting cap screws for BFN-2-047-0154C ultimately failed due to hydrogen embrittlement.
Hydrogen embrittlement could have been introduced from environmental sources (plant
environment, improper cleaning, improper lubricant, etc.), or during the manufacturing process
(black oxide coating).
H. Operator actions
Operations responded and stabilized the plant following the reactor scram and subsequently
initiated a plant cooldown to Mode 4.
I. Automatically and manually initiated safety system responses
The trip was not complex with all systems responding normally post trip except for the turbine
bypass valves [V], which subsequently failed closed due to loss of EHC pressure. Operations
responded and stabilized the plant. At 1407, with Unit 2 in Mode 3, there was second
automatic RPS actuation due to a low reactor water level transient caused by manually
opening and closing a main steam relief valve. PCIS Groups 2, 3, 6, and 8 isolation signals
were received. Upon receipt of these signals, components actuated as required.
III. Cause of the event
A. Cause of each component or system failure or personnel error
The Unit 2 SCRAM on low EHC pressure was caused by the failure of three of four ASTM
A574 grade 4137 black oxide coated mounting cap screws for shutoff valve
BFN-2-FCV-047-0154C. Procedure MCI-0-047-VLV002 does not incorporate vendor manual
VTD-G080-3255 guidance potentially leading to environmental degradation of the mounting
cap screws and introducing another source of hydrogen embrittlement. Hydrogen was
potentially introduced during the manufacturing process of coating the mounting cap screws
with black oxide.
B. Cause(s) and circumstances for each human performance related root cause
Review of the Human Performance (HU) factors of the event determined that neither the EHC
leak nor the associated broken cap screws were the result of a human performance error.

IV. Analysis of the event
At 13:52 CST, approximately one minute prior to the SCRAM, BFN Operations personnel placed
the alternate EHC pump (B) into service for testing. Initial EHC system pressure was
approximately 1564 pounds per square inch gauge (PSIG) and increased approximately 54 PSIG
(~3.5%) to 1618 PSIG when the B pump was placed in service. A plot of EHC pressure versus
time indicates that the leak began when the B pump was placed in service. The EHC control
system attempted to recover system pressure via the A and B pumps and accumulators but
ultimately failed, and a turbine trip was generated. Although this pressure surge was within EHC
operating pressure limits, the pressure surge was the most likely initiating event for the cap screw
failures and subsequent SCRAM. Post event walkdowns identified three sheared mounting cap
screws on 2-FCV-047-0154C, breaching the EHC pressure boundary and draining the Unit 2 EHC
tank.
Operations responded and stabilized the plant. At 1407 CST with Unit 2 in Mode 3, there was a
second automatic RPS actuation due to a low reactor water level transient caused by manually
opening and closing a main steam relief valve. Reactor water level was maintained via feed water
pump. Decay heat was removed by discharging steam (via main steam line drains) to the main
condenser. Units 1 and 3 were not affected.
V. Assessment of Safety Consequences
The plant responded as designed, while maintaining defense-in-depth for nuclear safety. All
nuclear safety systems functioned as designed. This event was of very low nuclear safety
significance. At no time was the health and safety of the public at risk.

VI. Corrective Actions
Corrective Actions are being managed by the TVA’s Corrective Action Program (CAP) under
Condition Report (CR) 2012739.
A. Immediate Corrective Actions
Shutoff valve and mounting cap screws were replaced.
B. Corrective Actions to Prevent Recurrence or to reduce the probability of similar events
occurring in the future
The source of the hydrogen could not be conclusively determined; therefore, the cause
evaluation addressed both the potential for environmental sources and manufacturing
sources. Potential manufacturing sources will be corrected via Corrective Action to Prevent
Recurrence (CAPR) 2012739-005, which will change procurement strategy of control valve,
safety valve, bypass valve, and combined intermediate valve mounting cap screws for
peripheral valves associated with procedures MCI-0-047-VLV002 and MCI-0-047-VLV001 to
QA3 and specify additional documentation and testing requirements. Potential environmental
sources will be corrected by CAPR 2012739-007, which will revise procedures
MCI-0-047-VLV002 and MCI-0-047-VLV001 to align with vendor manual VTD-G080-3255
recommendations with regards to cleaning, reusing mounting cap screws, using lock washers,
and lubricating practices.

16. Abstract  (Limit to 1326 spaces, i.e., approximately 13 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On August 19, 2025, at 1128 Central Daylight Time (CDT), during the performance of 3-SR-3.5.3.3, RCIC System 
Rated Flow at Normal Operating Pressure, the Reactor Core Isolation Cooling system (RCIC) failed to achieve 
required flow rate and pump discharge pressure due to an issue with the governor control system.  Upon 
investigation, it was determined that the Unit 3 RCIC Ramp Generating Signal Converter (RGSC) module had failed 
on July 20, 2025.  On August 23, 2025, at approximately 0108 CDT, the Unit 3 RCIC was declared operable 
following replacement of the RGSC and successful Cold Quick Start Post Maintenance Testing. 
The apparent cause of this condition was accumulated stress over time on the operational amplifiers leading to 
failure of the RGSC.  The corrective actions for this condition are to replace the Unit 3 RCIC RGSC, to replace aging 
RGSCs and Electro-Governor Magnetic Pickups at the earliest opportunity, and to create new PMs to periodically 
replace similar components on the Unit 1, 2, and 3 RCIC and High Pressure Coolant Injection systems.

I.
Plant Operating Conditions before the Event
At the time of discovery, Browns Ferry Nuclear (BFN) Unit 3 was in Mode 1 at approximately 100
percent power.
II.
Description of Event
A. Event Summary
On August 19, 2025, at 1128 Central Daylight Time (CDT), during the performance of
3-SR-3.5.3.3, RCIC System Rated Flow at Normal Operating Pressure, the Reactor Core
Isolation Cooling system (RCIC) [BN] failed to achieve required flow rate and pump discharge
pressure due to an issue with the governor control system.  Upon investigation, it was
determined that the Unit 3 RCIC Ramp Generating Signal Converter (RGSC) [SC][CNV]
module had failed on July 20, 2025.  On August 23, 2025, at 0108 CDT, the Unit 3 RCIC was
declared operable following replacement of the RGSC and successful Cold Quick Start Post
Maintenance Testing (PMT).
The Tennessee Valley Authority is submitting this report in accordance with Title 10 of the Code 
of Federal Regulations (10 CFR) 50.73(a)(2)(i)(B), as a condition that was prohibited by the 
plant’s Technical Specifications (TS). 
B. Status of structures, components, or systems (SSCs) that were inoperable at the start
of the event and that contributed to the event
There were no SSCs whose inoperability contributed to this event.

I.
Automatically and manually initiated safety system responses
There were no automatic or manual safety system responses associated with this event.
III.
Cause of the event
The direct cause of this condition was a failure of the Unit 3 RCIC RGSC.
A. Cause of each component or system failure or personnel error
The apparent cause of the Unit 3 RCIC RGSC failure was accumulated stress over time on
the operational amplifiers [AMP].
Additionally, the failure to implement Preventive Maintenance (PM) to replace the RGSC on a
frequency conducive to preventing age-related failures was identified as a contributing cause.
B. Cause(s) and circumstances for each human performance related root cause
The RCIC System Engineer failed to follow the system monitoring plan for RCIC.
IV.
Analysis of the event
The RCIC provides makeup water to the reactor vessel during shutdown and isolation from the
main heat sink to supplement or replace the normal makeup sources and operates automatically
in time to obviate any requirement for the Core Standby Cooling Systems.
Unit 3 TS Limiting Condition of Operability (LCO) 3.5.3 requires, in Mode 1 or in Modes 2 and 3
when reactor steam dome pressure is greater than 150 psig, that RCIC be operable.  TS LCO
3.5.3 Conditions A.1 and A.2 require, when RCIC is inoperable, that the High Pressure Coolant
Injection system (HPCI) [BJ] be immediately verified operable by administrative means; and that
the RCIC system be restored to operable status within 14 days or in accordance with the Risk
Informed Completion Time program.  A Past Operability Evaluation determined that this condition
existed since July 20, 2025, excluding the period from August 5, 2025, at 0132 CDT to August 9,
2025, at 0801 CDT when the Unit 3 reactor steam dome pressure was less than 150 psig due to
an ongoing Unit 3 maintenance outage, which exceeded the TS LCO 3.5.3 A.1 and A.2 required
completion times.

V.
Assessment of Safety Consequences
A Probabilistic Risk Analysis determined that this condition was of very low safety significance.
The health and safety of the public was not affected.

the components and systems that failed during the event
The periods where the Unit 3 RCIC was inoperable included several windows where the
Unit 3 HPCI was concurrently inoperable.
B. For events that occurred when the reactor was shut down, availability of systems or
components needed to shutdown the reactor and maintain safe shutdown conditions,
remove residual heat, control the release of radioactive material, or mitigate the
consequences of an accident
This event did not occur when the reactor was shut down.
C. For failure that rendered a train of a safety system inoperable, estimate of the elapsed
time from discovery of the failure until the train was returned to service
The RCIC system is not credited in the BFN Final Safety Analysis Report.  This event did not
affect the operability of any safety system.

Abstract (Limit to 1400 spaces, i.e., approximately 14 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On 3/14/18, during a review of the Fire Protection Program Safe Shutdown Analyses, station personnel identified two
valves that establish the boundary between a high pressure system and a low pressure system did not meet
requirements contained in 1O CFR 50, Appendix R, Section 111.G for protection against spurious operation during a fire.
A flow path exists through the Reactor Water Cleanup (RWCU) System into the Reactor Core Isolation Cooling (RCIC)
pump discharge line into the 'B' feedwater line and into the low-pressure piping for the feedwater recirculation line back
to the main condenser. During a fire, the cables could be damaged in such a way that the power cable to the outermost
valve could be reenergized and the valve could open. The second valve is also normally closed but could spuriously
open. If the fire also caused three other isolation valves in the RWCU system to not close, a flow path to the main
condenser could exist, resulting in a loss of reactor vessel water inventory.
This condition was not identified during the original analysis in the 1980's. There were no actual safety consequences
as a result of this condition.

NARRATIVE
Unit Conditions Prior to the Event
Unit 2 and Unit 3 were both operating in Mode 1 at approximately 100% rated thermal power. There were
no structures, systems or components out of service that contributed to this event.
Description of Event
On 3/14/18, station personnel were reviewing and updating the Fire Protection Program Safe Shutdown
Analyses. The update including classifying two motor operated valves in the feedwater system as "high/low
pressure interface" valves. An analysis originally performed in the 1980's did not identify the valves as
high/low pressure interface valves. It was determined that a flow path existed from the reactor vessel
through the Reactor Water Cleanup (RWCU) System into the Reactor Core Isolation Cooling (RCIC) pump
discharge line into the '8' feedwater line and into the low-pressure piping for the feedwater recirculation line
back to the main condenser. The two valves (in each unit) are:
Unit 2 Unit 3
M0-2-06-0388 M0-3-06-0388 FW Long Path Recirculation Isolation Valve
M0-2-06-2663 M0-3-06-3663 Long Path Recirculation Common Return Line
As high/low pressure interface valves, both valves must be assumed to have the potential to spuriously
open due to fire damage to electrical cables for the valves.
A review performed in 2015 determined the two valves are a potential path for diversion of flow from the
RCIC pump discharge if they should spuriously open during a fire, which could affect post-fire safe
shutdown scenarios that rely on RCIC. Due to the potential for diversion of flow during a fire, power to the
normally closed 2663/3663 valves was removed by opening the breaker during normal operations.
During the 2015 review, it was assumed that removing power from the closed valves during normal
operations would prevent the valve from opening during a fire and would eliminate the risk of loss of reactor
vessel inventory during a fire. It was determined that the only action needed was to update fire protection
documentation and calculations to classify the valves as high/low interface valves.
During the March 2018 updating process, it was determined that the possibility of a 3-phase hot short during
a fire also needed to be considered. The power cable between the breaker and the motor operator for the
valve is in a cable tray with other energized cables. A 3-phase hot short could re-energize the valve
operator and open the 2663/3663 valve. For this to create an actual flow path to the main condenser, fire
damage to four additional valves would need to occur that would result in the valves spuriously opening or
not closing if already open. The valves are the M0-2/3-06-0388 valve and three normally open primary
containment isolation valves in the RWCU system:

Once this issue was identified, it was entered into the corrective action program. Because the valves had
not been properly evaluated as high/low interface valves, compensatory measures were established in fire
areas 6N for Unit 2 and 13N for Unit 3.
Further review determined the issue is also considered an unanalyzed condition and is reportable as
required by 10 CFR 50.72(b)(3)(ii). See ENS event number 53265.
Analysis of Event
10 CFR 50, Appendix R, Section 111.G requires at least one train of cables and equipment necessary for
achieving and maintaining hot shutdown to be protected against fire damage. This includes damage from
hot shorts, open circuits, or shorts to ground that could prevent operation or cause maloperation of such
equipment. These requirements are further clarified in NRC guidance in Generic Letters 81-12 and 86-10.
GL 86-10, Section 5.3.1 states:
For three-phase AC circuits, the probability of getting a hot short on all three phases in the proper
sequence to cause spurious operation of a motor is considered sufficiently low as to not require
evaluation except for any cases involving Hi/Lo pressure interfaces.
The exception for high/low pressure interfaces is due to the potential severity of a loss of reactor coolant
event concurrent with a fire.
Section 6.2 of the site's FPP provides an analysis of high/low pressure interfaces that address the above
Appendix R requirements. Fire Safe Shutdown Program Specification NE-00296, Section 6.1, provides
program and design requirements for high/low pressure interfaces.
Cause of the Event
The flow path from the RWCU system to the feedwater return to condenser line was not identified as a
high/low pressure interface during original Appendix R analysis in the 1980's. The exact cause is unknown,
although it was likely due to lack of technical rigor in the system analysis performed when implementing
Appendix R requirements.

Corrective Actions
Since unprotected cables existed in fire areas 6N for Unit 2 and 13N for Unit 3, compensatory measures
were conservatively established in accordance with Technical Requirements Manual (TAM) Section 3.14.8
Condition A. A design change has been developed to add a disconnect switch to the power cable for the
2663/3663 valve such that a 3-phase hot short could not occur. Additional corrective actions are
documented in the corrective action program.

No previous similar occurrences have been identified.

OnAugust20, 2020, while performing testing on the Standby Liquid Control (SLC) system, waterwas inadvertently added tothe
SLC storagetank causing an unintended dilution ofthe boron solution. On August21, 2020 at 0953 hours, sampling ofthe boron
solution concentration revealed thatTechnical Specification (TS) Limiting Condition ofOperation (LCO) 3.1.7 was not met, and
Action Condition B was entered fortwo SLC subsystems INOPERABLE. Boron addition and sampling were performed, with SLC
returning to OPERABLE on August21, 2020 at 1307.
The directcause ofthe unintentional addition ofwatertothe SLC StorageTank during testing was avalve mis-position dueto
human error. Acorrective action will add independentverification tothe procedureforclosing oftheTransfer Line Isolation valve.
The safety significance ofthis event is considered to be ofvery low safety significance, in accordancewith the Regulatory
Guidance. This event is reported in accordancewith 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(A) and 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(D) as an eventor
condition that prevented thefulfillment ofthe safetyfunction ofthe Standby Liquid Control System. The eventwas alsofound to
be reportable under 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(i)(B) as an operation orcondition prohibited by Technical Specifications.

INTRODUCTION
The purpose ofthe Standby Liquid Control (SLC) System [BR] is to provide an alternate method ofshutting down the
reactor, independentofthe Control Rod Drive System [AA]. This is accomplished by pumping a neutron absorbing
solution intothe reactor in sufficient concentration and quantityto providethe required shutdown margin withoutcontrol
rod movementand toovercomethe maximum positive reactivity resulting from cooldown and xenon decay.
EVENT DESCRIPTION
On August 20, 2020, while the plantwas at 100 percent rated thermal power, PeriodicTest Instruction, PTI-C41-P0001,
Standby Liquid Control TransferSystem Pump and Valve OperabilityTest, was being performed on the Standby Liquid
Control (SLC) system. During thetest, the Transfer Line Isolation valve [ISV], was directed to be closed, but instead was
left open dueto a human performance errorby a plantoperator. The errorled tothe inadvertent addition ofapproximately
226 gallons ofdemineralizedwatertothe existing 4872 gallons ofboron solution in the SLC Storage Tank [TK].
Subsequently, preparationswere made to perform sampling ofthe boron solution in thetankto verify SLC system
operability.
On August21, 2020, at 0953 hours, whilethe plantwas at 100 percent rated thermal power, the SLC system was
declared INOPERABLE duetothe sampleforthe SLC storagetank boron solution concentration not meeting Technical
Specification (TS) criteria. Condition B ofTS 3.1.7was entered fortwo SLC subsystems being INOPERABLE.
SLCwas declared OPERABLE on August21, 2020 at 1307 hours, following boron addition tothe SLC StorageTank and
sampleverification thatthe boron solution concentration waswithin TS limits.
CAUSE
Direct Cause:
The direct cause ofthe unintentional addition ofapproximately 226 gallons ofwatertothe SLC Storage Tank during the
performance ofPTI-C41-P0001 was avalve mis-position dueto human error. A procedure step in the PTI directed the
Non-Licensed Operator(NLO) to Unlock and ClosetheTransfer Line Isolation valve. The NLO unlocked thevalve but
failed to closethevalve. A subsequent step opened the Transfer Line Outboard Containment Isolation valve aligning the
discharge ofthe running transferpumptothe SLC StorageTank, which caused the Storage Tankto fill with waterfrom the
TransferTank.
Contributing Cause:
Acontributing causetothe eventwasa latent procedural risk evaluation errorthat occurred in 2011. The Maintenance
Plan for PTI-C41-P0001 was classified as a GREEN RISK activity, however, a single error resulted in the inoperability ofa
safety system. Per procedure NOP-OP-1007, Risk Management, an "activity ifperformed incorrectly (i.e. any single
unrecoverable error) thatwould cause a loss ordefeat ofa safety system. Ifyes, this activity shall beYellow Risk". Had
the PTI been classified as aYellow Risk activity, increased oversight and monitoring usually associated with Yellow Risk
activities may have eliminated orminimized the impact ofthe error.

EVENTANALYSIS
The eventwas reported tothe Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) on August 21, 2020 at 1242 hours, as an event or
condition thatatthetime ofdiscoverycould have prevented thefulfillmentofa system needed to shut down the reactor
and maintain it in a safeshutdown condition (10 CFR 50.72(b)(3)(v)(A)) and mitigatethe consequences ofan accident (10
CFR 50.72(b)(3)(v)(D)).
Based upon theeventtimeline, this eventwas alsofound to be reportable under 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(i)(B) as an operation
orcondition prohibited byTechnical Specifications, dueto exceeding the completion timeforLimiting Condition of
Operation ofTS 3.1.7, Condition B, Two SLC subsystems inoperable.
A Probabilistic RiskAssessment (PRA) evaluation was performed fortheAugust 21, 2020 Standby Liquid Control
Inoperability event. The analysis ofthis event results in delta Core Damage Frequency (CDF) and delta Large Early
Release Frequency (LERF) values that arewell belowthe acceptablethresholds of 1.OE-06/yrand 1.OE-07/yr, as
discussed in Regulatory Guide 1.174, An Approach for using Probabilistic RiskAssessment in Risk Informed Decisions on
Plant-Specific Changestothe Licensing Basis. Therefore, this event is considered to be ofvery lowsafety significance in
accordancewith the Regulatory Guidance.
A Reactor Engineering evaluation was performed, following the event. The analysis showed thatthe reactorwould have
been shut down had the SLC tank been injectedwith a 2.6% boron concertation with sufficient margin totheanalytical
requirement. As such, the reactorwould have been shutdown with the measured SLC tank boron concentration of
2.73%.
This event is reportable under 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(A) and 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(D) as an event orcondition that
prevented thefulfillmentofthe safetyfunction ofthe Standby Liquid Control System, and 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(i)(B) as an
operation orcondition prohibited byTechnical Specifications.
CORRECTIVEACTIONS
The immediatecorrective action forthe personnel errorwas handled through station performance management.
A planned corrective action will change procedure PTI-C41-P0001 to add an independentverification stepforthe closure
oftheTransfer Line Isolation valve.

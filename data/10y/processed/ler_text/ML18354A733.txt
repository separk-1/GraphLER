Abstract (Limit to 1400 spaces, i.e., approximately 14 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On June 15, 2017, Clinton Power Station (CPS) commenced procedure CPS 9069.01, Shutdown Service Water
Operability Test. The purpose of this procedure is to verify operability of the Division 3 Shutdown Service Water (SX)
System Pump 1S X01 PC and selected valves per the lnservice Testing program on a quarterly basis. At 0958, SX
pump 1S X01 PC was started and after approximately 30 seconds, it tripped due to thermal overload. The pump was
declared inoperable and Operations entered Technical Specifications (TS) Limiting Condition for Operation (LCO)
3.7.2, Condition A which requires the High Pressure Core Spray (HPCS) system to be declared inoperable and enter
TS LCO 3.5.1 Condition B which requires verification by administrative means that the Reactor Core Isolation Cooling
(RCIC) system is operable and within 14 days restore the HPCS system to operable status. The cause of the event
was an incremental increase in internal pump resistance that could not be overcome by the developed torque of the
motor starting from standby conditions. This increased pump resistance was due to unidentified impacts during a
design modification (i.e., dry packing at pump start, unidentified corrosion effects on packing shaft sleeve, and
inadequate hard-face material). Another root cause was the untimely implementation of a corrective action that
resulted from a 2014 causal evaluation due to inadequate procedure guidance. Corrective actions have been
completed to restore operability of 1S X01 PC and ensure failure mechanisms introduced by design changes have been
assessed. This event is reportable under 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(D) as a condition that could have prevented the
fulfilment of a safety function and 10 CFR50.73(a)(2)(i)(B) as a condition prohibited by Technical Specifications.

B. Description of Event
On June 15, 2017, Clinton Power Station (CPS) attempted to perform the quarterly surveillance test of
the Division 3 shutdown service water (SX) [Bl] pump [P] (1 SX01 PC). The pump ran for approximately
30 seconds before thermal overloads tripped the pump motor [M].
At 0958, Operators declared the Division 3 SX, Division 3 Emergency Diesel Generator (EOG) [EK],
Division 3 Inverter [EF], Division 3 Direct Current Distribution [EJ], Division 3 Alternating Current
Distribution [ED], and the High Pressure Core Spray (HPCS) [BG] system inoperable due to the pump
trip. Operators entered the Technical Specification Actions Requirements for Limiting Condition for
Operation (LCO) 3.7.2 and LCO 3.5.1, ECCS - Operating. Operators verified within one hour that the
Reactor Core Isolation Cooling (RCIC) system was operable and entered the action to restore HPCS to
operable status within 14 days.
A second pump run was attempted with video recording that showed the motor spinning the shaft more
quickly for the first rotation, then immediately slowing down to approximately 70 RPM. The run lasted
approximately 8 seconds before the pump was manually secured.
At 2220 hours, Operators completed an 8-hour non-emergency notificatio·n to the NRC (Event
Notification Number 52806) under 10 CFR 50.72(b)(3)(v)(D) due to the loss of a single train safety
system, that is, inoperability of the HPCS system function. This event is reported under
1O CFR 50.73(a)(2)(i)(B) as a condition prohibited by Technical Specifications and under
1O c·FR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(D) as a condition that could have prevented the fulfillment of a safety function.
This is because the Division 3 SX pump was potentially inoperable greater than the 14 days allowed for
an inoperable HPCS system by TS 3.5.1 Condition B, (last successful run of the Division 3 SX ·pump
was on March 15, 2017, prior to pump failure on June 15, 2017, and return to service on June 23,
2017). This condition could have prevented the fulfillment of the safety function of structures or
systems that are needed to mitigate the consequences of an accident.

C. Cause of the Event
The causal evaluation identified two root causes fundamental to the failure of the Division 3 SX Pump:
1. Incremental increase in internal pump resistance that could not be overcome by the developed
torque of the motor starting from standby conditions. This increased pump resistance was due
to unidentified impacts during a design modification (i.e., dry packing at pump start, unidentified
corrosion effects on the packing shaft sleeve, and inadequate hard-face material).
2. The corrective action to ·replace the Division 3 SX Pump resulting from a 2014 failure to start
was not timely to prevent a recurrence. This was due to inadequate .procedural guidance for
promptly identifying and validating failure modes and effects and developing a remediation
strategy for the deficient design condition to support well-timed corrective action in the event
that the design condition cannot be immediately corrected.
D. Safety Consequences
The purpose for the Division $ SX system is to provide cooling water to Division 3 cooling loads
including the HPCS pump room coolers [CLR], the Division 3 EOG and the Division 3 Switchgear Heat
Removal System (VX). The Division 3 SX header is normally supplied cooling water from the non
safety related Plant Service Water Sy$tem (WS) [KG]. Under design basis event conditions such as a
Loss of Offsite Power (LOOP) or Loss of Coolant Accidents (LOCAs), a Division 3 SX pump start signal
is generated and the cross-tie valve [V] (1 SX014C) from the WS system closes. At this point the
Division 3 SX pump would be providing cooling water flow from the lake to the Division 3 SX cooling
loads.
Divisions 1 and 2 safety-related equipment remained fully capable of mitigating the consequences of an
accident and were available during the period of this event.
The failure of the Division 3 Shutdown Service Water Pump resulted in inoperability of Division 3
Emergency Diesel Generator safety function and inoperability of the HPCS System safety function.
The event described in this report constitutes a safety system functional failure.

E. Corrective Actions
Several component changes were made to the overall pump/motor assembly. A new motor with higher
starting torque characteristics was installed. The pump breaker and thermal overloads were replaced
with new units. The pump shaft sleeves were replaced and the pump packing was replaced with a
flexible self-lubricating style·equivalent.
On June 23, 2017, following a successful uncoupled run of the new motor, a coupled run· was
performed with no abnormalities noted. All monitored points showed normal starting characteristics for
the pump. The baseline and surveillance test procedures were performed without incident.
The Division 3 SX system, including the Division 3 EOG and HPCS system werE;) declared operable at
2345 hours on June 23, 2017.
Additional corrective actions include establishing a preventative maintenance task to periodically
inspect the pump packing shaft sleeve for corrosion.
Corrective Actions for the second root cause were to revise plant procedures to include specific
guidance to take actions for informing the timeliness of corrective actions for design related issues in
cases where the deficient design is still installed or in use and the failure modes and effects of that
design are not fully validated. In addition, a design verification of the Division 3 SX pump was
completed to ensure failure mechanisms introduced by design changes have been identified. No
additional design failure modes were identified.
F. Previous Similar Occurrences
. LER 2014-005-01 Failure of Shutdown Service Water Pump Results in Loss of Division 3
Emergency Diesel Generator and High Pressure Core Spray Safety Functions
On September 16, 2014 at approximately 1905 hours, Operators were performing an operability test of
the Shutdown Service Water (SX) system. The Division 3 SX pump (1 SX01 PC) was started to support
the 2-year Comprehensive Pump Test and the motor thermal overload protection tripped off the pump
after approximately 36 seconds. The cause of the event was that procedures used to develop plant
modification change packages contained an inadequate process to identify the need for further reviews
and the level of design detail required by those reviews. A new pump was installed and the pump was
restored to operable status on September 21, 2014. (ML 16006A047).

G. Component Failure Data
The Division 3 SX pump is a Sulzer Model 8X14A VCM pump with a Siemens-Allis Type RGV motor.
The pump is a vertical two-stage deep well pump that consists of a discharge head, six columns, and a
bowl assembly to extend 30 feet below the floor elevation. The pump is driven by a 75 horsepower,
1800 RPM, 480-volt motor.

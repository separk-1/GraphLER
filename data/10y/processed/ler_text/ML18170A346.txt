Abstract (Limit to 1400 spaces, i.e., approximately 14 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On April 30, 2018 with the Reactor operating in Mode 1 at 100% power, the results of a periodic surveillance test on Division II
Control Room Fresh Air charcoal filter were received. The sample results indicated that the activated charcoal methyl iodide
penetration test was above the allowable level. The test failure was determined to be due to the age of the charcoal. The
condition was not immediately recognized because the sample results were received from the vendor thirteen days after the
sample was obtained. This condition existed for a time longer than permitted by Technical Specifications. The activated
charcoal was replaced and the filter has since been declared Operable. This event was of minimal significance to the health
and safety of the public because the redundant Division I filter was Operable and available for use for the duration of the event.

NARRATIVE
BACKGROUND
IThe Control Room Fresh Air system (CRFA) [VI] is designed to filter radioactive nuclides in accordance with General
Design Criteria 19 and Regulatory Guide 1.52. The design consists of two 100% independent/redundant carbon filter trains
that are division ally separated. Control Room habitability includes maintaining Control Room operator dose within the limits
of General Design Criteria 19, assuming an accident with onsite release. The dose calculations for Control Room
habitability are based on the specified 1% penetration limit.
REPORTED CONDITION
On April 17, 2018 with the Reactor operating in Mode 1 at 100% power, a sample of activated charcoal from the Division II
CRFA Filter (**FL T**) was obtained to be shipped to an offsite vendor for methyl iodide penetration analysis in accordance
with a normally scheduled surveillance. On April 30,2018 the sample results were received. The test result indicated
1.4 7% penetration with an acceptance criterion of 1.0% or less. A confirmatory sample was acquired and tested with a
similar result. The charcoal was replaced and the Division II CRFA Filter was declared Operable on May 3,2018.
Firm evidence indicates that this condition began on April 17, 2018 when the sample was obtained and was not rectified
until May 3, 2018. This condition existed for a time longer than permitted by the Technical Specification (TS) for having one
CRFA subsystem inoperable. This condition is being reported in accordance with 10CFR50.73(a)(2)(i)(B), Operation or
Condition Prohibited by Technical Specifications.
CAUSAL ANALYSIS
iThe charcoal failed the methyl iodide penetration analysis due to age related degradation. The untimely identification of the
condition was a result of procedural guidance that allowed 31 days from sample time to receive analytical results.
jAdditionally, only one charcoal analysis was being tracked and trended. There were other available analysis and
parameters that could have been trended to better predict the life expectancy of the charcoal.
CORRECTIVE ACTION TO PREVENT RECURRENCE
fAn extent of condition found that the Standby Gas [VF] and Fuel Building Ventilation [VG] Charcoal Filter Trains are
susceptible to this event. The Division I and Division II Standby Gas trains were sampled on May 9, 2018 and May 8, 2018
respectively. Both trains were found to be well within specification. The Division I and Division II Fuel Building Filters were
sampled on July 14.2017 and December 12, 2016 respectively with both trains within specification. The Division I CRFA
Filter was sampled on December 7,2016 with satisfactory results. The following actions are planned and documented in
the station' s corrective action program.
Procedure changes that would require sample time coordination with the lab vendor in order to receive sample results
within three days of receipt of the sample for all systems susceptible to this condition.
~dditional charcoal analysis trending will be performed to improve life expectancy predictions.

PREVIOUS OCCURRENCE EVALUATION
There have been no similar events reported by RBS in the last five years.
SAFETY SIGNIFICANCE
The Division II CRFA system was considered to be Inoperable from the time the charcoal sample was obtained to the time
the charcoal was replaced. However, the Division I CRFA system was Operable for the duration of this event.
Furthermore, the Division II CRFA system was returned to Operable status three days from the time of discovery. This
event was of minimal significance to the health and safety of the public.

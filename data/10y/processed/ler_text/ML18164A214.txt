Abstract (Limit to 1400 spaces, i.e., approximately 14 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On April 10, 2018, at 05:38 p,m. COT, with the plant operating at 100 percent power, a design vulnerability in the Control
Building Chill Water (HVK) logic was identified during a review of HVK Chiller and Chill Water Pump control logic. This design
vulnerability could prevent the auto start of the HVK Chillers upon restoration of safety related power by emergency diesel
generators following a Loss of Offsite Power. This HVK control logic vulnerability was introduced in error by a system
modification in 1987. A compensatory measure was put in place to restore operability of the system until a modification can be
installed to permanently remove the design vulnerability. Operators are prepared to take action in the event of a loss of Control
Building Ventilation to restore the safety function in accordance with existing procedures. Therefore this condition was of
minimal significance with respect to the health and safety of the public.

BACKGROUND
!fwo 1O O-percent capacity Chillers provide cooling to each ventilation system sub-loop, each of which provides cooling,
heating, ventilation, pressurization, and smoke removal for several areas within the building. The ventilation system
comprises two independent, redundant trains of components and subsystems either of which supports the safety function of
providing a controlled environment in the main control room and the environmental requirements of the safety-related
electrical equipment in the building. Each control building ventilation subsystem is supported by the respective
safety-related emergency diesel generator.
!fhe HVK System provides chilled water to the cooling coils in three separate air handlers within each division. The air
handling system (HVC) removes the heat generated within the main control room and electrical equipment rooms. Air
handlers serve the main control room, standby switchgear rooms, battery / charger rooms, inverter rooms, and chiller
rooms. Each air handler serves both trains of their assigned areas (e.g., both the Division 1 and 2 standby switchgear
rooms cooling may be fully served by the air handler in either train).
REPORTED CONDITION
On April 10, 2018 at 05:38 p.m. CDT, with the plant operating at 100 percent power, Engineering determined that time delay
relays fURL Y**] in the Division" Control Building Chilled Water System (HVK) (VI) could fail in a manner that challenges
he design safety function of the HVK System during a Loss of Offsite Power (LOP) event. Additionally, because there is no
ime delay function in the Division I start logic, if a lOP occurs with Division I HVK Chillers [**CHU**] running both the
Division I and Division" HVK Chilled Water Pumps [**P**] would start immediately after the lOP with no auto-start of either
Division I or Division" Chillers.
IThe start logic of the Division" Chillers is configured such that if there is a lOP, when power is restored to the emergency
buses by the diesel generators, the Division" Chilled Water Pump will restart immediately, while the Division I Chilled
Water Pump is prevented from starting for 30 seconds by Division" time delay relays. This allows the Division" logic to
complete, starting the Division" Chiller. Completion of the Division" Chiller start logic requires a low flow signal from the
opposite Division, which would be present because the time delay relays are inhibiting start of the Division I Chilled Water
Pump.
IThe HVK System conforms to the single failure criterion. Failure of either Division" time delay relay to provide the delay
!function could allow both the Division I and Division" Chilled Water Pumps to immediately restart after a lOP when power
is restored to the emergency buses. With both Chilled Water Pumps running, there would be no low flow signal from either
Division, and neither the Division I nor Division II Chiller would receive a start signal.
~dditionally, if a lOP occurs with Division I HVK Chillers running and Division" Chillers in standby, neither Division I nor
Division II chiller would auto start when power is restored to the emergency buses because the time delay function does not
exist in the Division I control logic.

NARRATIVE
CAUSAL ANALYSIS
The control logic problem was traced to a latent deficiency in a 1987 modification, which rerated the chillers from 50%
capacity to 100% capacity each, based on actual building loads being lower than those originally calculated. Accordingly, it
would not be necessary for both chillers in either Division to be operable for the Division to be considered operable.
The modification established a level of redundancy that was not previously creditable. To do this, a redesign of the control
logic for the multi-unit system was required, setting up a relationship between (pre-selected) lead and standby units, while
still assuring the correct restart of the system following a LOP.
The 1987 modification inadvertently created the logic issue identified in this event.
The 1987 modification represents a legacy human performance related event. Further investigation into the 1987
modification is not possible, due to the passage of time and unavailability of personnel involved.
CORRECTIVE ACTION TO PREVENT RECURRENCE
Compensatory measures have been incorporated into procedures that direct operators to secure the Division I HVK Chilled
Water Pump if both the Division I and Division II HVK Chilled Water Pumps are found running following a LOP.

was identified_

PREVIOUS OCCURRENCE EVALUATION
No similar events have been reported by River Bend Station in the last three years.
SAFETY SIGNIFICANCE
In the unlikely event of a LOP coincident with a failure of the Division II HVK time delay relays, control room operators would
have responded to the event with established procedures for a loss of Control Building Ventilation to restore the safety
~unction_ Operators would have responded similarly in the event of a LOP with Division I HVK running and Division II in
standby_ Thus, this event was of minimal significance to the health and safety of the public.

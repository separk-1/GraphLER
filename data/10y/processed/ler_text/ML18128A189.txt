~BSTRACT (Limit to 1400 spaces, i.e., approximately 15 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On November 26, 2017, with the Reactor in the Run Mode at 100 percent power, while reviewing a procedure to
be performed during scheduled testing, it was determined that the test as written would cause both trains of the
Standby Gas Treatment System (SGTS) to be made inoperable during the test. This also would make
secondary containment inoperable. This LER is submitted to acknowledge that Pilgrim Nuclear Power
Station missed providing Event Notifications and LERs for past occurrences. With both trains of SGTS and
secondary containment inoperable while in Run, the occurrences would have been reportable if identified at
he time in accordance with 10 CFR 50.72(b)(3){v)(C) and 10 CFR 50.72(b)(3){v)(D) as conditions that could
have prevented the fulfillment of the safety function of a structure or system needed to control the release
of radioactive material and mitigate the consequences of an accident. This has been determined to be a
reportable condition involving SGTS and secondary containment inoperability in accordance with 1O CFR
50.73{a)(2)(v)(C) and 1O CFR 50.73{a){2)(v)(D) which has not been reported during the past three years.
rrhis event had no impact on the health and/or safety of the public.

NARRATIVE
BACKGROUND
[The Pilgrim Nuclear Power Station (PNPS) Standby Gas Treatment System (SGTS) is part of the Secondary
Containment System (SCS) and addresses the SGTS air filtration trains, including the ducting, the exhaust
~ans, the dampers, and the various filtration media. The SCS is designed, in conjunction with other engineered
safeguards and nuclear safety systems, to limit the release of radioactive material during normal plant
operations within the limits of Title 1 O Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) Part 20 and to limit the release of
radioactive material so that off-site dose from a postulated design basis accident will be below the guideline
tvalues in 10 CFR Part 100.
PNPS Technical Specification (TS) 3/4.7.B.1 governs the operability requirements of the SGTS. The
specification requires that with certain exceptions, the SGTS shall be operable during periods that include
reactor power operation (RUN, STARTUP, and HOT SHUTDOWN modes), during movement of irradiated fuel
assemblies in secondary containment, during movement of new fuel over the spent fuel pool, during CORE
ALTERATIONS, and during operations with a potential for draining the reactor vessel (OPDRVs).
PNPS TS 3.7.C.1 requires secondary containment to be OPERABLE when in the RUN, STARTUP, and HOT
SHUTDOWN modes, during movement of recently irradiated fuel assemblies in the secondary containment,
and during OPDRVs.
There are two principal accidents for which credit is taken for secondary containment operability. These are a
loss of coolant accident and a fuel handling accident involving "recently irradiated fuel." The SCS performs
no active function in response to each of these limiting events; however, its leak tightness is required to ensure
that the release of radioactive materials from primary containment is restricted to those leakage paths and
associated leakage rates assumed in the accident analysis and that fission products entrapped within the
secondary containment structure will be treated by the SGTS prior to discharge to the environment.
EVENT DESCRIPTION
On November 26, 2017, with the Reactor in the Run Mode at 100 percent power, while reviewing a procedure
o be performed during schedule.d testing, it was determined that if the test was performed as written, would
cause both trains of SGTS and secondary containment to be made inoperable. A follow up review determined
that this procedure and other similar procedures have been performed previously within the past three years
during scheduled testing of SGTS.
Therefore it has been determined that PNPS has missed providing Event Notifications and Licensee Event
Reports (LERs) for occurrences of a reportable condition during the past three years involving the SGTS and
secondary containment inoperability.

With both trains of SGTS and secondary containment inoperable in the Run mode, this event would have been
reportable per the requirements of 1O CFR 50.72(b)(3)(v)(C) and 1O CFR 50.72(b)(3)(v)(D), any event that
could have prevented the fulfillment of the safety functions to control the release of radioactive material and
mitigate the consequences of an accident, had the conditions been identified at the time.
A Root Cause Evaluation has been completed and additional information is being provided within this LER.
CAUSE OF THE EVENT
The Root Cause of this event is that in 1994 a revision to PNPS Procedure 8.M.3-18, "Standby Gas Treatment
System Exhaust Fan Logic Test and Instrument Calibration," was made without evaluating the impacts on
Technical Specification operability requirements for SGTS and SCS. Since both trains of SGTS were
breached during performance of Procedure 8.M.3-18 when Secondary Containment was required, a condition
was created that could have prevented fulfillment of the safety function for SCS and SGTS while performing the
surveillance.
CORRECTIVE ACTIONS
Immediate corrective action was to place all procedures that breached SGTS on Hold.
~n interim corrective action was put in place to prevent use of the procedures until they were revised or verified
~hat both the opposite SGTS train and secondary containment remain operable during the maintenance activity.
ifhe Corrective Action to Prevent Recurrence requires procedures to be revised so that the activities can be
performed without a loss of both trains of SGTS and/or SCS when Secondary Containment is required.
SAFETY CONSEQUENCES
ifhere were no consequences to the safety of the general public, nuclear safety, industrial safety, or radiological
safety due to this event. The actual consequence was creating a condition that could have prevented fulfillment
of the safety function for SCS and SGTS while performing the surveillance.
rrhere was no adverse impact on the public health or safety.
REPORT ABILITY
With both trains of SGTS and SCS inoperable in the Run mode, this event is reportable per the requirements of
1O CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(C) and 1O CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(D), any event or condition that could have prevented the
~ulfillment of the safety functions to control the release of radioactive material and mitigate the consequences of
an accident.

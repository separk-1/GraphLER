16. Abstract  (Limit to 1326 spaces, i.e., approximately 13 single-spaced typewritten lines)
At Browns Ferry Nuclear Plant, Units 1 and 2, on November 5, 2025, at 1157 Central Daylight Time, the 
performance of 480V Load Shedding Logic System Functional Test (Division I) and subsequent 
troubleshooting discovered that the tripping circuit for the 480V Control Bay Vent Board (CBVB) A Normal 
Feeder Breakers position switch was not closed.  From the past operability evaluation, it was concluded that, 
in the event of there were a Unit 1 and/or Unit 2 loss of offsite power and an accident signal, the loads on the 
480V CBVB A would not have shed, and the potential to overload Diesel Generator (DG) A existed.   
Based on the results of the analysis and the overloading scenarios associated with this condition, it was 
concluded that DG A would not have been overloaded and was capable to perform its intended safety 
function for its mission time.  Because of this condition, the 480V Load Shed logic did not meet Technical 
Specifications (TS) Limited Condition of Operation 3.8.1 multiple times in Modes 1, 2, and 3 from 
November 5, 2022, to November 5, 2025.  Because it was determined that DG A was fully capable of 
performing its safety functions with the additional load from 480V CBVB A, there is no safety significance 
with this condition present.

I. 
Plant Operating Conditions before the Event 
At the time of discovery, Browns Ferry Nuclear Plant (BFN), Units 1 and 2, were both in Mode 1 at 
approximately 100 percent power. 
 
II. 
Description of Event 
A. Event Summary 
On November 5, 2025, at 1157 Central Daylight Time, performance of Technical Specifications 
(TS) Surveillance Requirement (SR) procedure 0-SR-3.8.1.8(I), 480V Load Shedding Logic 
System Functional Test (Division I), and subsequent troubleshooting discovered that the tripping 
circuit for the 480V Control Bay Vent Board (CBVB) A Normal Feeder Breakers position 
switch [33] was not closed.  From the past operability evaluation, completed December 17, 2025, 
it was concluded that, in the event of there were a Unit 1 and/or Unit 2 loss of offsite power and 
an accident signal (reactor low level or reactor low pressure and drywell high pressure), loads on 
the 480V CBVB A [EC] would not have shed, and the potential to overload Diesel Generator (DG) 
A [EK] existed. 
Based on the results of an engineering analysis and the overloading scenarios associated with 
this condition, it was concluded that DG A would not have been overloaded and was capable to 
perform its intended safety function for the 0 to 3 minute period of operation with the maximum 
starting and running loads from Unit 1 or Unit 2 plus the loads on the 480V CBVB A through its 
7-day mission time.  Because of this condition, the 480V Load Shed logic [JE] did not meet TS 
Limited Condition of Operation (LCO) 3.8.1 multiple times in Modes 1, 2, and 3 from  
November 5, 2022, to November 5, 2025.   
Because it was determined that DG A was fully capable of performing its safety functions with the 
additional load from 480V CBVB A, there is no safety significance with this condition present. 
The Tennessee Valley Authority is submitting this report in accordance with Title 10 of the Code 
of Federal Regulations 50.73(a)(2)(i)(B), as any operation or condition which was prohibited by 
TS.

Cause of the event 
A. Cause of each component or system failure or personnel error

B. Cause(s) and circumstances for each human performance related root cause 
The cause, suspected inadequate procedure/maladjustment of a position switch, from this

IV. 
Analysis of the event 
Due to a maladjustment of a position switch, an engineering evaluation concluded that, with the 
breakers tripping circuit open, 480V Load Shed Logic for Divisions I and II would not have shed loads 
on 480V CBVB A if an accident signal was received from Unit 1 and/or Unit 2.  Tripping of 
nonessential loads is a required function for DG operability.  It also determined that with the loads on 
the 480V CBVB A not shedding, DG A was capable to perform its specified safety functions in the 
event an accident signal and loss of offsite power occurred between 0 minutes through its 7-day 
mission time. 
 
V. 
Assessment of Safety Consequences 
From the engineering evaluation, it was concluded that DG A remained capable of performing its 
safety functions and carrying designated loads plus the additional load from the CBVB A from initial 
start through its 7-day mission time.  Regarding Division I and II of 480V Load Shed Logic, because 
the position switch for the tripping circuit was open, the Normal Feeder Breaker for the CBVB A would 
not have tripped open to shed the load on an accident signal from Unit 1 and/or Unit 2, both divisions 
of 480V load Shed Logic were inoperable. 
A. Availability of systems or components that could have performed the same function as 
the components and systems that failed during the event 
There are no systems or components that could have performed the same function. 
B. For events that occurred when the reactor was shut down, availability of systems or 
components needed to shutdown the reactor and maintain safe shutdown conditions, 
remove residual heat, control the release of radioactive material, or mitigate the 
consequences of an accident 
This event did not occur when the Units 1 and 2 reactors were shut down.

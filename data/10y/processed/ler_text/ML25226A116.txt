 16.  Abstract (Limit to 1326 spaces, i.e., approximately 13 single-spaced typewritten lines) 
On 6/17/25 around 1755 Eastern Prevailing Time (EPT), Station Service Transformer (SST) 64 low voltage alarm was 
received on Division I Bus after starting Circulating Water Pump #4. SST 64 Load Tap Changer (LTC) was found to be 
non-functional requiring manual operation of the LTC to be initiated. Subsequent diagnostics revealed a failed time-
delay closing relay in the LTC circuitry, which was replaced on 6/19/25 at 1932 EPT.  After reviewing historical data, 
the LTC was last found to be functioning on 2/7/2024.  The non-functional LTC could potentially result in lower than 
minimum required voltages during a postulated loss of coolant accident coincident with degraded voltage conditions. 
This Licensee Event Report (LER) is being made under the corresponding requirement in Title 10 of the Code of Federal 
Regulations (CFR) 50.73(a)(2)(i)(B), due to a condition that is potentially prohibited by Technical Specifications.   This 
LER is also being made pursuant to 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(D) as a potential condition that could have prevented the 
fulfillment of the safety function needed to mitigate the consequences of an accident. Further investigation is being 
conducted to determine the extent of the condition, and a supplement is planned.  The event had no significant effect on 
public health and safety.

DESCRIPTION OF THE EVENT 
On 6/17/25 around 1755 Eastern Prevailing Time (EPT), Station Service Transformer (SST) [XFMR] 64, Maximo 
Location Number R1200S002, low voltage alarm was received on Division I Bus [EB] [BU] after starting Circulating 
Water Pump #4 [KA][P]. SST 64, Load Tap Changer (LTC) [TTC] (General Electric, Model LR-65) was found to be 
non-functional requiring manual operation of the LTC to be initiated. The LTC was manually adjusted, which cleared 
the alarm and raised the bus voltage to 118 V. The LTC was then returned to automatic mode. SST 64 voltage was 
recorded hourly to ensure voltage was maintained within the operability band (122-to-124-meter volts) and adjusted as 
necessary to maintain the band.  The transformer was confirmed to be operable after manual voltage control was 
maintained within the required band. Subsequent diagnostics revealed a failed time-delay closing relay [TDC] in the 
LTC circuitry, which was replaced and tested on 6/19/25 at 1932 EPT.  After reviewing historical data, the LTC was 
last found to be working on 2/7/2024.   
The primary function of SST 64 is to provide a means to step down voltage (13.8 kV) from Bus 11 to 4160V for 
distribution to Division 1 Engineered Safety Feature (ESF) (class 1E power requirements) and Balance of Plant loads.  
Operability is based on the ability to supply power from the 120 kV switchyard, through SST 64, to 4160V buses 64B 
and 64C. This transformer is also sized to accommodate the simultaneous starting of all ESF loads on receipt of an 
accident signal, without the need for load sequencing. The LTC modulates the voltage on the transformer to keep a 
nominal 121.8 meter-Volts to ensure Division 1 offsite source has adequate voltage to support the startup and 
subsequent functionality of the Fermi 2, Division 1 ESF loads without tripping during a Loss of Offsite Coolant Accident 
(LOCA) transient. 
While SST 64, LTC control switch was set to automatic, would not automatically tap to control voltage. While the LTC 
is in manual mode, procedure states that if the voltage range of 122-to-124-meter volts is not met, then Buses 64B 
and 64C are considered INOPERABLE. The basis of this requirement is the offsite circuit cannot be lost (i.e. by 
actuation of the degraded voltage relaying) during a design basis accident and must be capable of starting all required 
ESF loads on the receipt of an accident signal, so this condition impacts the ability to meet the requirements of 
Technical Specification (TS) 3.8.1.   
TS 3.8.1 “AC Sources – Operating” requires (During MODES 1,2 and 3) two qualified circuits between the offsite 
transmission network and the onsite Class IE AC Electrical Power Distribution System and two emergency diesel 
generators (EDGs) per division. When one offsite circuit is inoperable, then CONDITION D is entered to restore the 
offsite circuit to OPERABLE status within 72 hours. If the REQUIRED ACTION and COMPLETION TIME is not met, 
MODE 3 needs to be entered within 12 hours. Further investigation is being done to determine when the voltage level 
was outside of the voltage band exceeding the completion time of 72 hours along with determining whether the 
condition was a potential loss of a safety function.  The results from the investigation will be provided in a 
supplemental report.   
SIGNIFICANT SAFETY CONSEQUENCES AND IMPLICATIONS 
Further investigation is being conducted for the safety consequences and implications and will be supplemented.

This Licensee Event Report (LER) is being made under the corresponding requirement in Title 10 of the Code of 
Federal Regulations (CFR) 50.73(a)(2)(i)(B), due to a condition that is potentially prohibited by Technical 
Specifications.  This LER is also being made pursuant to 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(D) as a potential condition that could 
have prevented the fulfillment of the safety function needed to mitigate the consequences of an accident  
There were no safety consequences or radiological releases associated with this event.  At no time during this event 
was there potential for endangering the public health and safety.   
 
CAUSE OF THE EVENT 
The LTC failed due to a failed time-delay relay within the LTC controls.    
 
CORRECTIVE ACTIONS 
Diagnosis identified a failed Time Delay Relay. LTC was placed back in AUTO mode following replacement of the 
relay.  Actions to address procedural and human performance gaps will be addressed in the Corrective Action 
Program. 
 
PREVIOUS OCCURRENCES 
An examination of Condition Reports from the past three years yielded the following information: 
CR-2024-39536 - On 5/13/24, SST 64 LTC did not maintain voltage automatically. Operators manually increased the 
voltage from approximately 119V to 121V. Although the transformer continued to supply proper voltage and frequency 
to essential safety buses, a work order was requested to investigate and repair the issue.  However, this work was 
never performed until the alarm in June 2025.

Abstract (Umit lo 1400 spaces, i.e .• approximately 14 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On August 22, 2019 at 2234 EST with Unit 2 operating at 100% rated thermal power, the "High Pressure Coolant Injection (HPCI) system
inverter circuit failure" alarm was received on a Main Control Room panel. This alarm indicates a loss of AC power from the HPCI inverter,
and is caused by either a loss of DC power to. or a failure of, the inverter. Operating personnel immediately noticed the loss of indication on
several digital recorders and on the HPCI flow controller. At 2235 EST, the "HPCI system inverter circuit failure" alarm cleared, and all
referenced digital indications returned to normal.
Subsequent investigation into the HPCI inverter circuit failure revealed a failed ceramic capacitor in the HPCI turbine speed control power
supply. The failed capcitor caused a short-circuit condition that momentarily drew power down on the HPCI inverter. This caused a
momentary loss of indication to the referenced digital recorders and a momentary loss of power to the HPCI flow controller. In the event of
a valid HPCI initiation signal, the momentary loss of power to the HPCI flow controller would have resulted in a loss of HPCI function.
Operations personnel therefore declared HPCI inoperable at 2234 EST.
The cause of the capacitor failure was not determined. Ceramic capacitors of this type are not known to fail at a specified frequency and are
not recommended for replacement on a periodic basis. The HPCI turbine speed control power supply was subsequently removed from
service, replaced, and the new power supply tested satisfactorily.

NARRATIVE
EVENT DESCRIPTION
On August 22, 2019 at 2234 EST with Unit 2 operating at 100% rated thermal power, the "High Pressure Coolant Injection (HPCI)
system inverter circuit failure" alarm was received on a Main Control Room panel. Operating personnel immediately noticed the loss of
indication on several digital recorders and on the HPCI flow controller. At 2235 EST, the "HPCI system inverter circuit failure" alarm
cleared and all digital indications returned to normal. At 2234 EST, HPCI was declared inoperable due to the failure of a ceramic
capacitor inside of the HPCI turbine speed control power supply. The failed capacitor caused a short-circuit condition that momentarily
drew down power on the HPCI inverter and resulted in a loss of function to the HPCI flow controller.
Failed Component Information:
Master Parts List Number: 2E41 K065
Manufacturer: Lambda Electronics
Model Number: LRS 52-48
Type: Power Supply
The capacitor failure would have ultimately resulted in a loss of HPCI function during a demand and rendered HPCI inoperable.
EVENT CAUSE ANALYSIS
Upon investigation into the "HPCI system inverter circuit failure" alarm, a loss of indication was noticed on several digital indicators
and a burning smell was noted on one of the Main Control Room back panels. Subsequent visual inspection revealed a failed ceramic
capacitor in the HPCI turbine speed control power supply. The cause of the capacitor failure was not determined. Ceramic capacitors
of this type are not known to fail at a specific frequency and are not recommended for replacement on a periodic basis.
SAFETY ASSESSMENT
This event is reportable per 10 CFR 50. 73(a)(2)(v)(D) due to an event or condition that could have prevented the fulfillment of the safety
function of a structure or system that is needed to mitigate the consequences of an accident.
The HPCI system is a single-train system that is designed to ensure that the reactor is adequately cooled to limit fuel-cladding
temperature in the event of a small break in the nuclear system and a loss of coolant does not result in a rapid reactor pressure
vessel depressurization. The HPCI system will continue to operate until the Reactor Pressure Vessel (RPV) is below the pressure at
which either operation of the Low Pressure Coolant Injection (LPCI) mode of the Residual Heat Removal (RHR) system or the Core
Spray (CS) system will maintain core cooling.
Upon a small break which results in a loss of coolant accident (LOCA), HPCI provides sufficient inventory to prevent the core from
becoming uncovered. In the event that HPCI is unavailable or inoperable, the Automatic Depressurization System (ADS) is credited to
depressurize the RPV to enable operation of the low pressure ECCS systems, i.e., CS or LPCI. Reactor Core Isolation Cooling (RCIC)
is also available to restore and maintain water level.
Although HPCI was declared inoperable, ADS, CS, LPCI, and RCIC were operable and could have provided core cooling in the event
of a small break LOCA or a Design Basis Accident (DBA) LOCA. Therefore. based on the ability of the station to mitigate the
consequences of a potential loss of the HPCI system combined with a small break LOCA, this event is considered to have very low
safety significance.

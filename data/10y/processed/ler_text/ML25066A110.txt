16. Abstract (Limit to 1326 spaces, i.e., approximately 13 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On January 16, 2025, at 1657 CDT, it was discovered that manual Secondary Containment Isolation Valve (SCIV) 2-1279-
21A, 2A Reactor Water Clean Up Filter Demineralizer Rad Waste Drain Valve, was in an Open position. This resulted in
discovery of a condition which was prohibited by Technical Specifications (TS). The valve should have been in a Closed
position or should have had appropriate administrative controls applied when left in an Open position under the applicable
tagout boundary that had been established. The valve was closed on January 16, 2025, at 1801 CDT in accordance with
TS 3.6.4.2 Condition A. However, a past functionality review indicated the valve had been open for approximately 86 hours
total, which exceeded TS 3.6.4.2 Condition A.1 to isolate the line in 8 hours.
The cause of the event was a failure to implement administrative controls for the SCIV left in an open position during tagout
boundary dispatch and placement.
Immediate action was to close the SCIV. Follow up actions will be to improve SCIV tagout boundaries and valve labels.
This report is being submitted under 10 CFR 50. 73(a)(2)(i)(B) for an operation or condition which was prohibited by the
plant's Technical Specifications.

B. DESCRIPTION OF EVENT
On January 16, 2025, at 1657 CDT, it was discovered that manual secondary containment isolation valve (SCIV)[ISV] 2-
1279-21A, 2A Reactor Water Clean Up [CE] Filter Demineralizer Rad Waste Drain Valve, was in an Open position.
Operations discovered this during maintenance activities within the tagout boundary that was to be provided by this valve.
The valve was tagged in an Open position using a Caution/Information card to ensure the piping remained drained during
the work on an adjacent valve. The 2-1279-21A could have been used as a drain for the volume, under applicable
procedural and administrative controls, and then later Closed, although there were no specific instructions in the tagout to
close the valve after draining. The tagout did include cautions and procedure references for the 2-1279-21A related to it
being a SCIV.
When the Open position of the valve was discovered, Technical Specification (TS) 3.6.4.2 Condition A was entered. The
2-1279-21A valve was closed at 1801 CDT, and TS 3.6.4.2 Condition A was exited. A past functionality review indicated
that the valve had been left in an Open position from when the tagout was validated at 0409 on January 13, 2025, until it
was closed at 1801 CDT on January 16, 2025, for a total of 85 hours and 52 minutes. This exceeds the 8 hour
completion time in TS 3.6.4.2 Condition A Action A.1 to isolate the secondary containment flow path. Issue Report
04830292 was generated.
A tagout package includes multiple sections, including placement instructions at the front of the package, and card notes
at the end, in addition to the actual physical tags, called EPN (Equipment Part Numbers) Tags, that are hung on the
equipment. Specific to this tagout, the EPN Tag had a note referring to a governing procedure for SCIV openings, and a
note that secondary containment could be violated. These secondary containment impacts were not fully recognized by
the operations team hanging the tagout, and the necessary administrative controls were not discussed in detail in a pre
job brief.

C. CAUSE OF EVENT
The cause of the self-identified event was a failure to implement administrative controls for the SCIV left in an open
position as part of the tagout.
The Senior Reactor Operator responsible for dispatching the tagout was overly confident in their knowledge of the specific
system and previous tagouts. They were preoccupied with a different tag that had expected alarms, which led to an
incomplete review of all tagout documents. The Nuclear Station Operator responsible for dispatching the tagout reviewed
all documents, but their lack of attention to detail led to a failure to recognize the potential TS implications. They also
assumed that the valve would ultimately be left in a closed position. The Equipment Operators hanging the tagout did not
question an abnormal note on the EPN tag for a valve they were leaving in the open position. Collectively, the individuals
did not recognize the need to establish appropriate administrative controls from a procedure referenced in the tagout for
valve 2-1279-21A being left in an open position.
Contributing were enhancement opportunities in the tagout. The secondary containment impacts could have been located
in the front section of the tagout and could have been color coded for improved recognition. The reference to a
procedure number for SCIVs could have been enhanced with a statement that the valve should not be left in an open
position without an operator in attendance.
D. SAFETY ANALYSIS
System Design
The function of the SCIVs, in combination with other accident mitigation systems, is to limit fission product release
during and following postulated Design Basis Accidents (DBAs). Secondary containment isolation within the time limits
specified for those isolation valves designed to close automatically ensures that fission products that leak from primary
containment following a OBA, or that are released during certain operations when primary containment is not required
to be operable or take place outside primary containment, are maintained within the secondary containment boundary.
The normally closed manual SCIVs are considered operable when the valves are closed and blind flanges are in place,
or open under administrative controls.
Updated Final Safety Analysis Report (UFSAR) Section 6.2.3.1 provides that the safety objective of the secondary
containment system, in conjunction with other engineered safeguards and nuclear safety systems, is to limit the
release of radioactive materials so that offsite doses resulting from a postulated OBA will remain below 10 CFR 100
guideline values.
Both Units 1 and 2 share a common Reactor Building (secondary containment). However, there is a piping difference
between the two QCNPS units. Specifically, Unit 2 valve 2-1279-21A is an SCIV; whereas, the equivalent valve on
Unit 1 does not serve an SCIV function.
Safety Impact
There were no safety consequences as a result of this event. Although maintenance activities did result in an open flow
path through this SCIV related pipe, the breach size was determined to be 7.1 square inches. Existing Engineering
Evaluation EC 347977 concludes that any hole found in Secondary Containment which is less than 12.5 sq in would not

E. CORRECTIVE ACTIONS
Immediate:
1. Closed SCIV 2-1279-21A.
Follow-up:
1. Improve the tagout for SCIVs to more prominently highlight the requirements for administrative controls any time
the valve is left in an Open position.
2. Improve labeling of SCIVs to indicate that administrative controls are required when left open.
3. Removed qualifications of workers involved and performed remediations.
4. Performance Management has been applied to workers involved.

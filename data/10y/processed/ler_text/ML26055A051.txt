16. Abstract (Limit to 1326 spaces, i.e., approximately 13 single-spaced typewritten lines) 
On October 28, 2025, at approximately 19:04, Susquehanna Steam Electric Station (SSES) Unit 2 experienced a main turbine 
trip followed by an automatic reactor scram when an arc flash on the 500 kilovolt (kV) lines occurred during a hydrogen vendor 
manual venting evolution. The event was reported by Event Notification 58012 in accordance with 10 CFR 50.72(b)(2)(iv)(B) as 
an event that resulted in an automatic actuation of the Reactor Protection System and 10 CFR 50. 72(b )(2)(xi) as an event that 
resulted in an offsite notification made to the Pennsylvania Emergency Management Agency. This event is also reportable in 
accordance with 10 CFR 50. 73(a)(2)(iv)(A) as an event that resulted in an automatic actuation of the Reactor Protection 
System. The cause of the event was arc flash as a result of hydrogen gases vented from the vendor-owned Hydrogen Tank 
Farm during normal vendor evolution contacting overhead 500 kV transmission lines. Key corrective actions include 
establishment of an alternative location for venting the vendor hydrogen tank trailer, revision of venting evolution instructions, 
and evaluation and implementation of options to increase clearance between the Hydrogen Tank Farm vent and overhead 
transmission lines.

EVENT DESCRIPTION 
On October 28, 2025, at approximately 19:04, Susquehanna Steam Electric Station (SSES) Unit 2 experienced a main 
turbine trip [EIIS System Code - IT] followed by an automatic reactor scram when an arc flash on the 500 kilovolt (kV) 
lines occurred during a hydrogen vendor manual venting evolution. The event was reported by Event Notification 58012 in 
accordance with 10 CFR 50. 72(b )(2)(iv)(B) as an event that resulted in an automatic actuation of the Reactor Protection 
System and 10 CFR 50. 72(b )(2)(xi) as an event that resulted in an off site notification made to the Pennsylvania 
Emergency Management Agency. This event is also reportable in accordance with 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(iv)(A) as an event 
that resulted in an automatic actuation of the Reactor Protection System (including a reactor scram). 
The following timeline demonstrates the sequence of events: 
10/28/2025 Before 18: 16: Vendor driver notifies Control Room that they will be filling the liquid hydrogen tank at the 
Hydrogen Tank Farm. 
10/28/2025 18: 16: Vendor driver begins filling the liquid hydrogen tank at the Hydrogen Tank Farm. 
10/28/2025 19:04: Liquid hydrogen tank is filled. Vendor driver begins to depressurize the tanker truck via the hydrogen 
tank exhaust stack at the Hydrogen Tank Farm. 
10/28/2025 19:04: An arc is visible in the area of the resultant plume from the hydrogen release on the 500 kV 
conductors overhead the hydrogen tank in the Hydrogen Tank Farm. 
10/28/2025 19:04: Unit 2 experiences an automatic scram due to Main Turbine trip caused by Main Generator [EIIS 
System Code - TB] differential line current and enters Mode 3. 
All plant systems responded normally following the automatic reactor scram. Decay heat was removed by discharging 
steam to the main condenser using the turbine bypass valves. Unit 1 was unaffected by the event and remained at 
approximately 100% Rated Thermal Power. 
The affected 500 kV lines were placed in service in August 2023. While similar liquid hydrogen activities are routinely 
performed at this location, this type of event has not previously occurred. 
Following visual inspection and testing of the 500kV lines and a review of the post-trip plant response, SSES Unit 2 was 
returned to Mode 1 operation on October 31, 2025.

The cause of the event was arc flash as a result of hydrogen gases vented from the vendor-owned Hydrogen Tank 
Farm during normal vendor evolution contacting overhead 500 kV transmission lines. This cause was attributed to the 
modifications to install the 500 kV transmission lines to the 500 kV substation not adequately accounting for clearance 
limits between the transmission lines and the hydrogen exhaust stack in the Hydrogen Tank Farm. 
ANALYSIS/SAFETY SIGNIFICANCE 
The actual consequence of the event was the automatic scram of Unit 2 from approximately 100% power operation due 
to a Generator Load Reject and Main Turbine Trip. The scram was not complex; all safety systems operated as designed. 
Main Turbine Bypass Valves [EIIS System Code - JI] and Safety Relief Valves [EIIS Component Code - RV] opened 
automatically to maintain reactor vessel pressure. Reactor water level was stabilized and maintained with reactor 
feedwater [EIIS System Code - SJ]. There was no Emergency Core Cooling System or Engineered Safeguard Function 
[EIIS System Code - JE] initiations nor were any required. 
The shutdown was reviewed regarding FSAR Chapter 15 and determined to be fully bounded by the analysis. Chapter 
15.2.2 specifically addresses the Generator Load Rejection with the Steam Bypass System operable and classifies this 
as a non-limiting event. Main Turbine Bypass and Safety Relief Valves operated as designed during this event to mitigate 
reactor pressure excursion. All safety systems were observed to operate as designed. 
The condition described herein did not result in a Safety System Functional Failure. Accordingly, this event will not be 
counted as a Safety System Functional Failure in the Reactor Oversight Process Performance Indicators. There were no 
actual consequences to the health and safety of the public as a result of this event. 
CORRECTIVE ACTIONS 
1. An alternative location for venting of the vendor hydrogen tank trailer has been specified and the vendor has accepted 
updated delivery instructions prohibiting intentional manual venting at the Hydrogen Tank Farm. 
2. Identify and implement a solution that ensures clearances between the Hydrogen Tank Farm hydrogen exhaust stack 
and overhead transmission lines meet the identified clearance requirements.

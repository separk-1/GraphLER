Abstract (Limit to 1400 spaces, i.e., approximately 14 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On September 25,2018, with the plant operating at 90 percent power, a design vulnerability in the Control Building Ventilation
(HVK) system control logic was identified during an extent of condition review of a previously reported condition. The review
identified a vulnerability in the HVK Chiller start logic. The design vulnerability had been in place since original plant
construction and could cause a loss of all Control Building Cooling for up to 20 minutes following a Loss of Offsite Power.
Compensatory measures were put in place to restore operability of the system until a temporary modification could be installed
to remove the design vulnerability. This condition was of minimal significance with respect to the health and safety of the public.

NARRATIVE
BACKGROUND
!The Control Building Ventilation (HVK) [VI] system consists of two 1O O-percent capacity Chillers (**CHU**) which provide
cooling to each ventilation system sub-loop. Each of which provides cooling, heating, ventilation, pressurization, and smoke
removal for several areas within the building. The ventilation system comprises two independent, redundant trains of
components and subsystems either of which supports the safety function of providing a controlled environment in the main
control room and the environmental requirements of the safety-related electrical equipment in the building. Each HVK
subsystem is supported by the respective safety-related emergency diesel generator and conforms to the single failure
criterion.
Irhe HVK System provides chilled water to the cooling coils in three separate air handlers within each division. The air
handling system (HVC) removes the heat generated within the main control room, electrical equipment rooms and chiller
rooms. Each air handler serves both trains of their assigned areas (e.g., both the Division I and II standby switchgear
rooms cooling may be fully served by the air handler in either train).
Normally, one HVK Chiller is in operation, with one from the opposite division in standby. This provides for an immediate
and automatic swap with no manual actions should a problem exist in the operating division. Division I HVK consist of HVK
Chillers 'A' and 'C' and Division II HVK consist of HVK Chillers 'B' and '0'.
REPORTED CONDITION
On September 25,2018, with the plant operating at 90 percent power, Engineering identified that if either HVK Chiller 'A' or
HVK Chiller 'B' was started up to 20 minutes prior to a Loss of Offsite Power (LOP) event then any single failure in the
opposite division that causes that division not to run would cause a loss of all Control Building cooling until the HVK Chiller
20 minute quick restart timer times out. Additionally, if either the Division I or the Division II HVK start sequence is initiated
(manually or automatically) before the 20 minute time out, then the associated chilled water pump would start and then trip
as the Chiller would not start in the required time period. In this condition, HVK Chiller 'A' and HVK Chiller 'B' cannot be
used as the running or standby Chiller. When this condition exists, if either of these Chillers is started then the Control
Building cooling system does not meet single failure criteria until the 20 minute quick restart timer times out.
HVK Chiller 'C' and HVK Chiller '0' have not been affected by this deficiency since 2016 when a digital control system
upgrade was installed. The new control system changed the allowed number of starts in a 20 minute period from 1 to 2.
This event is being reported in accordance with 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(i)(B), 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(ii)(B) and 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)
(v)(D).
CAUSAL ANALYSIS
The HVK control logic design deficiency was introduced during original plant design and construction. The deficiency was
previously identified several times over the life of the plant. However, the deficiency was not recognized as a violation of
licensing basis requirements and corrective actions were not put in place to remediate the deficiency.

CORRECTIVE ACTION TO PREVENT RECURRENCE
Compensatory measures were put in place so that only HVK Chiller 'C' or HVK Chiller '0' could be the running or standby
Chiller until a temporary modification was put in place to bypass the Quick Restart timer in the HVK Chiller 'A' and HVK
Chiller '8' start logic. If either HVK Chiller 'A' or HVK Chiller '8' was already running and had been running for longer than 20
minutes, then it was allowed to continue to run.
IA
temporary modification to bypass the quick restart timer in the HVK Chiller 'A' and HVK Chiller '8' start logic was installed
in October 2018. Administrative controls were put in place to restrict operators from unnecessarily restarting the Chillers
within 20 minutes of a previous start.
Procedure revisions were made to restrict operators from restarting a Chiller before 20 minutes have elapsed from the
previous start. An additional procedure change was made so that following a LOP, if a Chiller is restarted within 20 minutes
of the previous start, that Chiller must be run for 2 hours or secured for 4 hours to allow the motor to cool before another
start attempt.
An extent of condition for HVK single failure vulnerabilities and design deficiencies was performed. No new vulnerabilities
were identified.
A permanent modification will be performed to eliminate the HVK control logic deficiency and is being tracked by the
Corrective Action Program.
PREVIOUS OCCURRENCE EVALUATION
A similar HVK control logic vulnerability event was identified in April 2018 and reported by LER-2018-001 on June 7, 2018.
SAFETY SIGNIFICANCE
In the unlikely event of a LOP within 20 minutes of an HVK Chiller 'A' or HVK Chiller '8' start and a single failure in the
opposite division, control room operators would have responded to the event with established procedures for a loss of
Control 8uilding Ventilation to restore the safety function with either HVK Chiller 'C' or HVK Chiller '0'. Thus, this event was
of minimal significance to the health and safety of the public.

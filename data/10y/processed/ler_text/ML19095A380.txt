Abstract (Limit to 1400 spaces, i.e., approximately 14 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On February 5, 2019, at 1804 hours, a Motor Speed Changer (MSC) logic fuse blew during performance of Quad Cities Nuclear ·
Power Station (QCNPS) Unit 1 High Pressure Coolant Injection (HPCI) operability surveillance following planned maintenance
activities. Had HPCI been demanded while the fuse was blown the HPCI turbine would have been unable to reach its required speed.
During investigation of the blown fuse, the MSC motor and the MSC gearbox were discovered tG> be mechanically bound. Intermittent
failure of the Low Speed Stop (LSS) Limit Switch (LS) due to age related degradation of internal electrical contacts allowed the MSC
motor to remain energized when it should have been deenergized, leading to degradation. The limit switch contacts were found with
high resistance attributed to observed deposits and pitting. None of these components were part of the work scope of this HPCI
maintenance window.
The immediate corrective action was replacement of the fuse, the LSS LS, the MSC motor and select MSC gearbox components. the
safety impact was minimal based on the availability of other high pressure and low pressure Emergency Core Cooling Systems.
This is being reported in accordance with 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(D) for an Event or Condition that Could Have Prevented Fulfillment of
a Safety Function to Mitigate the Consequences of an Accident.

8. DESCRIPTION OF EVENT
On February 5, 2019, at 1804 hours, during a Unit 1 High Pressure Coolant Injection (HPCl)[BJ] operability
surveillance, a fuse [FU] blew in the logic for the Motor Speed Changer (MSC)[SM] for the HPCI Turbine [TRB].
The blown fuse disabled both the manual and automatic injection functions of HPCI. Had HPCI been demanded
while the fuse was blown the HPCI turbine would have been unable to reach its required speed. The system had
been out of service for planned maintenance, but no maintenance activities took place on equipment protected by
this fuse. On February 6, 2019, at 0222 hours, ENS #53816 was made to the NRC under
10 CFR 50.72(b)(3)(v)(D), to report this event as an event or condition that could have prevented the fulfillment of a
safety function. November 8, 2018 was the last successful test of Unit 1 HPCI.
During troubleshooting, it was found that the MSC motor [MO] and the MSC gearbox were mechanically bound.
The fuse .• the MSC motor, and select gearbox components were replaced. Subsequent troubleshooting determined
that intermittent failure of the Low Speed Stop (LSS) Limit Switch (LS)[33], caused intermittent operation of the
associated relay. The LS intermittent failure was due to high resistance contacts attributed to deposits and pitting
caused by age related degradation. The LSS LS was replaced.
After troubleshooting and repairs, a HPCI operability surveillance was performed successfully. HPCI was declared
operable on February 8, 2019, at 1434 hours. Since HPCI is a single train safety system, this notification is being
made in accordance with 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(D) for an Event or Condition that Could Have Prevented Fulfillment
of a Safety Function to Mitigate the Consequences of an Accident.
C. CAUSE OF EVENT
The cause of the event is the intermittent failure of the MSC LSS LS, which impacted both the MSC motor and
gearbox. LS failure is attributed to age related degradation of internal LS electrical contacts. The LS had been
installed for more than 9 years and was due for replacement at 10 years.

D. SAFETY ANALYSIS
System Design
According to the Quad Cities Nuclear Power Station (QCNPS) Units 1 and 2 UFSAR Section 6.3.2.3, "High
Pressure Coolant Injection Subsystem," the HPCI subsystem is designed to pump water into the reactor vessel
under Loss of Coolant Accident (LOCA) conditions which do not result in rapid depressurization of the pressure
vessel. The loss of coolant might be due to a loss of reactor feedwater or to a small line break which does not
cause immediate depressurization of the reactor vessel. The sizing of the HPCI subsystem is based upon providing
adequc;1te core cooling during the time that the pressure in the reactor vessel decreases to a value that the Core
Spray [BM] subsystem and/or the Low Pressure Coolant Injection (LPCI) [BO] subsystem become effective. The
HPCI subsystem is designed to pump 5600 gallons per minute into the reactor vessel within a reactor pressure
range of about 1120 pounds per square inch gage (psig) to 150 psig. Initiation of the HPCI subsystem occurs
automatically on signals indicating reactor low-low water level or high drywell pressure. HPCI injection into the
reactor vessel may be accomplished manually by the operator or without operator action by the HPCI automatic
initiation circuitry. HPCI can also operate in a pressure control mode of consuming steam from the reactor vessel
without providing full injection into the vessel (down to and including zero injection).
Safety Impact
The safety significance of this event is minimal. HPCI was inoperable for approximately 68.5 hours. The Reactor
Core Isolation Cooling (RCIC)[BN] system, Automatic Depressurization System (ADS), and multiple low-pressure
Emergency Core Cooling Systems (ECCS) were available during this time.
Risk Insights
The HPCI system is a risk significant system in the QCNPS PRA, supports High Safety Significant and Low Safety
Significant Maintenance Rule Functions and is monitored under the Mitigating System Performance Index (MSPI)
for reliability and availability. Unavailability of the HPCI system during this condition was addressed under the
QCNPS Configuration Risk Management program per the requirements of 1O CFR50.65 (a)(4) to assess and
manage the risk increase.
This event is a Maintenance Rule Functional Failure (MRFF), and a MSPI failure, since both the manual and
automatic injection functions of HPCI were lost.
E. CORRECTIVE ACTIONS
Immediate:
1. Replaced the LSS LS, MSC Motor, and select MSC gearbox components.
2. Failed LS and Motor were sent to Exelon PowerLabs for further failure analysis.
Follow-up:
1. The preventive maintenance replacement frequency of the LSS LS will be reduced from 10 years to 8 years.

F. PREVIOUS OCCURRENCES
The station events database, LERs, and INPO Consolidated Event System (ICES) were reviewed for similar events
at QCNPS. This event was attributed to intermittent LSS LS failure resulting in MSC motor and gearbox binding
and a blown fuse.
Quad Cities Unit 1 LER 05000254/95-004-00, "HPCI System Declared Inoperable From Motor Speed Changer
Malfunction Due to Limit Switch Failure," involved the same system components as the current event. In 1995, the
LSS LS was found to have improper setting of the internal contact block, which also resulted in intermittent
performance, causing MSC damage. Corrective actions addressed proper maintenance instructions for setting of
the limit switches. The corrective actions from the 1995 event were not found to contribute to the current event.
Quad Cities Unit 2 Issue Report 593863, "MSC Full Reposition Greater Than 15 Seconds," also involved the same
system components as the current event. In 2007, a loose screw associated with the LSS LS allowed the MSC
motor to remain energized and driving into the LSS since a logic test six days earlier. Unlike the current event, it
did not result in damage to the MSC motor or gear box, and HPCI remained capable of meeting its design functions.
Corrective actions addressed verifying that the MSC motor has de-energized after returning HPCI to standby. The
corrective actions from the 2007 event were not found to contribute to the current event.

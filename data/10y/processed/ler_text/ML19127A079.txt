Abstract (Limit to 1400 spaces, i.e., approximately 14 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On February 2, 2019 at 1500, Westinghouse reported that while reviewing the Low Temperature Overpressure (L TOP) system
analysis of record (CN-SCS-04-100, Rev. 3) for areas of margin that could offset the increase in differential pressure required to
support the Mechanical Stress Improvement Project (MSIP), errors were discovered. On March 9, 2019 an evaluation was
completed that determined the Unit 2 LT OP system had been inoperable in the past for longer than allowed by Technical
Specifications. Corrective actions include changing the LT OP PORV lift settings, on both Units 1 and 2 to meet the protection
requirements of 10 CFR 50 Appendix G, Fracture Toughness Requirements.
All times listed in this report are Central Standard Time (CST).

I. DESCRIPTION OF REPORTABLE EVENT
A. REPORTABLE EVENT CLASSIFICATION
This event is reportable in accordance with 1 OCFR50.73(a)(2)(i)(B);
Any operation or condition which was prohibited by the plant's Technical Specifications except when:
(1) The Technical Specification is administrative in nature;
(2) The event consisted solely of a case of a late surveillance test where the oversight was corrected, the test was
performed, and the equipment was found to be capable of performing its specified safety functions; or
(3) The Technical Specification was revised prior to discovery of the event such that the operation or condition was no
longer prohibited at the time of discovery of the event.
B. PLANT CONDITION PRIOR TO THE EVENT
Both Unit 1 and Unit 2 were operating at 100% Rated Thermal Power.
C. STATUS OF STRUCTURES, SYSTEMS, OR COMPONENTS THAT WERE INOPERABLE AT THE START OF THE
EVENT AND CONTRIBUTED TO THE EVENT
Prior to the event, no Structures, Systems, or Components were inoperable that contributed to the event.
D. NARRATIVE SUMMARY OF THE EVENT, INCLUDING DATES AND APPROXIMATE TIMES • ·
On February 4, 2019, Westinghouse informed Comanche Peak that calculation errors had caused the maximum allowable
PORV LT OP lift settings to be higher than allowed to meet 10 CRR 50 Appendix G, Fracture Toughness Requirements.
On March 13, 2019, an evaluation was completed that determined the Unit 2 LT OP system was inoperable for greater
than the 7 day Completion Time while in MODE 4 on one occasion. The LT OP system was not operable because it did not
comply with 10 CFR 50, Appendix G, Fracture Toughness Requirements. This single event would have required
depressurizing the Reactor Coolant System (RCS) and establishing an RCS vent>/= 2.98 square inches within 8 hours.
The evaluation also determined the Unit 2 LT OP system was inoperable for greater than the 24 hour Completion Time on
three occasions while in MODE 5. The L TOP system was not operable because it did not comply with 10 CFR 50,
Appendix G, Fracture Toughness Requirements. Each of the three events would have required depressurizing the
Reactor Coolant System (RCS) and establishing an RCS vent>/= 2.98 square inches within 8 hours.
In all four cases the failure to vent the RCS caused operation in a manner prohibited by Technical Specifications. The
current PORV L TOP lift settings would not have resulted in a challenge to reactor vessel integrity and would have
performed their safety function
E. THE METHOD OF DISCOVERY OF EACH COMPONENT OR SYSTEM FAILURE, OR PROCEDURAL PERSONNEL
ERROR
A past operability evaluation was completed on March 13, 2019 that identified four times that Unit 2 was operating outside
of Technical Specification requirements associated with completion times for L TOP inoperability in the three years prior to
event discovery. Unit 1 was not operated outside of Technical Specifications in the three years prior to event discovery.

Ill. ANALYSIS OF THE EVENT
A. SAFETY SYSTEM RESPONSES THAT OCCURRED
No safety system responses occurred due to this event.
B. DURATION OF SAFETY SYSTEM TRAIN INOPERABILITY
1. Unit 1 had no LT OP inoperabilities due to the event.
2. Unit 2 had one inoperability that exceeded 7 days while in MODE 4 on 6/13/2017.
3. Unit 2 had three separate inoperabilities that exceeded 24 hours while in MODE 5 on 4/29/17, 7/29/17, and 1/9/19.
C. SAFETY CONSEQUENCES AND IMPLICATIONS OF THE EVENT
Based on Westinghouse and Comanche Peak evaluation LT OP was inoperable four different times as described earlier in
this report. Those evaluations also indicate that L TOP was capable of protecting the reactor vessel from low temperature
overpressure failure even with the less restrictive LT OP PORV lift settings as described in this report. Therefore, this event
had no impact on nuclear safety, reactor safety, radiological safety, or health and safety of the public. This event has been
evaluated to not meet the definition of a safety system functional failure per 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v).
IV. CAUSE OF THE EVENT
Incorrect PORV LT OP lift settings were calculated due to inappropriate application of uncertainties causing incorrect lift
settings to be placed in the PTLR. Those incorrect settings lead to non-compliance with 10 CFR 50 Appendix G, Fracture
Toughness Requirements which caused the four LT OP inoperabilities.
V. CORRECTIVE ACTIONS
Recalculate PORV LT OP lift settings utilizing the correct uncertainties, update the PTLR, and adjust plant installed PORV
LT OP lift settings in accordance with the revised PTLR.

16. Abstract (Limit to 1326 spaces, i.e., approximately 13 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On October 16, 2023, during performance of a surveillance test while beginning a refueling outage, the stroke times of Main
Steam Isolation Valves (MSIVs) exceeded the Technical Specifications limit of five (5) seconds. The apparent causes
attributed to two (2) of the MSIVs had the potential to exist during the previous operating cycle. Therefore, the two (2) MSIVs
are being reported under 10CFR50.73(a)(2)(i)(B) as a condition prohibited by Technical Specifications because the apparent
causes had the potential to have existed longer than allowed by Technical Specifications.
The condition was corrected and the valves were returned to satisfactory performance by adjusting the dashpots and exhaust
restrictors as needed. There were no safety consequences associated with this condition, as the valve stroke times did not
exceed the times considered in the safety analysis.

NARRATIVE
Event Description
On October 16, 2023, during performance of a surveillance test, the stroke time of the ‘A’ Outboard Main
Steam Isolation Valve (MSIV) exceeded the Technical Specifications limit of five (5) seconds. The valve stroke
time was 6.2 seconds. This test was performed in Mode 3 (hot shutdown) while the unit was being shut down
for a refueling outage. Transition to Mode 4 (cold shutdown) was made shortly after completion of the test, and
the mode of applicability was exited. The cause of the slow stroke time was attributed to drift in the valve
dashpot. Follow-up testing was performed during the outage and adjustments were made as needed. The
stroke time was verified to be satisfactory before the unit was restarted from the refueling outage.
Additionally, the ‘B’ Inboard MSIV also exhibited a slow stroke time of 5.1 seconds. The cause of this condition
was attributed to delayed actuation, likely due to debris or contamination in the solenoid valve. The ‘B’
Outboard MSIV stroked in 5.5 seconds, however this could not be replicated in subsequent testing. The stroke
times for these valves were adjusted accordingly and were verified to be satisfactory before the unit was
restarted from the refueling outage. The remaining valves stroked within specified Technical Specifications
limits.
There are two (2) MSIVs welded in a horizontal run of each of the four (4) main steam lines, with one (1) valve
as close as possible to the primary containment barrier inside, and the other just outside the barrier. The
valves, when closed, form part of the nuclear system process barrier. The MSIVs are operated by pneumatic
pressure and by the action of compressed springs. The upper end of the valve stem is attached to a
combination air cylinder and hydraulic dashpot that are used for opening and closing the valve and for speed
control, respectively. Speed is adjusted by a valve in the hydraulic return line alongside the dashpot.
Verifying that the isolation times of each MSIV is within the specified Technical Specifications limits is required
to demonstrate operability. The isolation time test ensures that the MSIVs will isolate in a time period that does
not exceed the times assumed in the design basis accident analyses. In all cases, the times in the accident
analysis were not exceeded because the analysis is performed assuming at least 10 seconds for the MSIV
closure time.
This report is being made under 10CFR50.73(a)(2)(i)(B) as a condition prohibited by Technical Specifications.
Although there was no evidence of degradation during the prior refueling outage or operating cycle, the
condition associated with the ‘A’ Outboard MSIV likely existed for longer than allowed by Technical
Specifications. Similarly, there was no evidence of degradation for the MSIVs on the ‘B’ main steam line. The
time frame associated with the condition which caused the ‘B’ Inboard MSIV to exhibit a slow stroke time
cannot be determined, but the valve is conservatively being included in this report. The ‘B’ Outboard MSIV
stroke time that was not repeatable has no firm evidence of existing prior to the time of discovery and is not
included within the reportable scope of this LER. The performance of the valves has been entered into the
Corrective Action Process for further evaluation of the test performance and evaluation methodology.
There were no other systems, structures or components that were inoperable that contributed to the event.

Safety Consequences
The Technical Specification Surveillance Requirement (SR) 3.6.1.3.9 requires the MSIVs to close within 3 to 5
seconds. The Updated Final Safety Analysis Report (UFSAR) conservatively utilizes a maximum valve closure
time of 10 seconds for analyses where the loss of reactor coolant inventory is the controlling variable, which
provides conservatism between the SR and the analyzed performance. The observed stroke times for the
noted MSIVs were no greater than 6.2 seconds. Therefore, the stroke times observed were not outside the
bounds of the analyses and the safety function was maintained. There were no actual or potential safety
consequences as a result of this event because this issue was identified during testing and the MSIVs closed
within their analyzed times.
Since the remaining MSIVs closed satisfactorily, the overall containment isolation function would have been
accomplished in 5.1 seconds.
Corrective Actions
This condition was corrected by performing follow up stroke timing for each MSIV and making adjustments
such as dashpot and exhaust restrictor tuning as required to return the valves to desired performance.
The valve performance has been entered into the Corrective Action Program for further evaluation of the test
performance and evaluation methodology.

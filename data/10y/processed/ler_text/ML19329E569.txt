Abstract (Limit to 1400 spaces, i.e., approximately 14 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On September 24, 2019, at 2338 PDT during maintenance to drain an air receiver in the High Pressure Core Spray (HPCS)
Diesel Starting Air (DSA) system a pressure control valve (PCV) vented, due to a leaking isolation valve, causing the in-service
air receiver pressure to drop below the minimum Technical Specifications value. Operations declared the HPCS Diesel
Generator, HPCS Service Water, and the HPCS system inoperable.
The root cause of the event was that the plant procedure was revised in 2009 to open the air start motor cross connect valve to
retain all 4 air start motors available for diesel start which is not in accordance with current licensing/design bases. Corrective
actions include revision to station procedures and repair of the leaking isolation valve.
This is reportable under 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(D) as an event or condition that could have prevented the fulfillment of the safety
function of structures or systems that are needed to mitigate the consequences of an accident. This event was reported under
10 CFR 50.72(b)(3)(v)(D) as Event Notification #54292.

NARRATIVE
Plant Conditions
Before the event, the plant was operating in Mode 1 at 100% power. All Emergency Core Cooling Systems (ECCS) were
operable.
Event Description
On September 24, 2019, during maintenance, one of the High Pressure Core Spray (HPCS)[BG] Diesel Starting Air (DSA)
[LC] air receivers [RCV] vented from the in-service air header through a DSA Pressure Control Valve (PCV) [PCV] causing
the in-service air receiver's pressure to lower.
As part of the maintenance evolution, the DSA air receiver to be drained was isolated from the starting air header and the
air start motor cross connect valve [V] was opened per procedure. The isolation valve [V] leaked by creating a lower
pressure upstream of the PCV then downstream of the PCV which caused the PCV to relieve the pressure, dropping the
in-service air receiver pressure below the Technical Specification operability limit for the HPCS DSA system.
At 2338 PDT operators noted that pressure degraded below the operability limit for the HPCS DSA system. This resulted
in the HPCS Diesel Generator (DG)[EK], HPCS Service Water (SW)[BI] and HPCS system being declared inoperable, per
plant Technical Specifications.
The leak was isolated and the in-service air receiver pressure was restored at 0104 PDT on September 25, 2019. All
Technical Specifications entered due to the degraded air receiver pressure were exited. Total inoperable time was one
hour and twenty-six minutes.
This inoperability of HPCS is reportable under 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(v)(D) as an event or condition that could have
prevented the fulfillment of the safety function of structures or systems that are needed to mitigate the consequences of an
accident. An ENS report was made to the NRC at 0526 hours PDT (EN# 54292) on September 25, 2019 pursuant to 10
CFR 50.72(b)(3)(v)(D).
Immediate Corrective Actions
The air start motor cross connect valve was closed and in-service air header pressure was restored resulting in the HPCS
DG, HPCS SW, and HPCS system being declared operable.
Assessment of Safety Consequences
Safety was maintained as the Automatic Depressurization System (ADS), Low Pressure Core Spray (LPCS) [BM], and
Low Pressure Coolant Injection (LPCI)[BO] mode of Residual Heat Removal (RHR) [BO] system remained operable at the
time of the event to mitigate the consequences of an accident. In addition, the Reactor Core Isolation Cooling (RCIC)[BN]
system was operable and the HPCS system was maintained available during the period of HPCS inoperability using
Dedicated Operators.
Cause of Event
The root cause of the event was that the plant procedure was revised in 2009 to open the air start motor cross connect
valve to retain all 4 air start motors available for diesel start which is not in accordance with current licensing/design bases.
The direct cause of the event was leak by of the isolation valve. A contributing cause was that a Condition Report to
assess the controlling procedure for the maintenance was not fully evaluated.

NARRATIVE
Similar Events
A similar event happened on July 24, 2017. While attempting to drain only one HPCS DSA air receiver, the PCV started
venting the other air receiver at the same rate, maintaining equal pressure between the receivers. HPCS was already
inoperable due to maintenance at the time of this previous occurrence.
Corrective Actions
Revise the Standard Operating Procedure for Diesel Starting Air to remove steps to open the air start motor cross connect
valve when draining an air receiver.
Repair the leaking isolation valve.
Revise station procedures to provide additional guidance when assessing Condition Reports.

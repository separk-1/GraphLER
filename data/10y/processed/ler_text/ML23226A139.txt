Abstract (Limit to 1560 spaces, i.e., approximately 15 single-spaced typewritten lines)
On April 14, 2023, at 05:11, James A. FitzPatrick Nuclear Power Plant (JAF) received indication of a
half-scram on the “B” side Reactor Protection System (RPS). The cause was determined to be an overvoltage
condition that tripped the Electrical Protection Assembly (EPA) for the “B” RPS Motor Generator (MG) Set.
The same condition occurred on May 25, 2023, at 23.59. These events caused invalid primary containment
isolation signals in multiple systems, reportable in accordance with 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(iv)(A). The cause was
corrosion buildup in the voltage adjust potentiometer which led to the overvoltage trip of the EPA. Corrective
actions included replacing the voltage adjust potentiometer.

NARRATIVE
Background
Reactor Protection System (RPS) [EIIS: JE] Electric Power Monitoring System is provided to isolate the RPS
bus from the motor generator (MG) set or an alternate power supply in the event of overvoltage, undervoltage,
or underfrequency. This system protects the loads connected to the RPS bus against unacceptable voltage
and frequency conditions and forms an important part of the primary success path of the essential safety
circuits. Some of the essential equipment powered from the RPS buses includes the RPS logic, scram pilot
valve solenoids, and various valve isolation logic.
RPS electric power monitoring assembly will detect any abnormal high or low voltage or low frequency
condition in the outputs of the two MG sets or the alternate power supply and will de-energize its respective
RPS bus, thereby causing all safety functions normally powered by this bus to de-energize. (Safety functions
powered by the RPS buses deenergize to actuate.)
Two redundant Class IE circuit breakers are connected in series between each RPS bus and its MG set, and
between each RPS bus and its alternate power supply. Each of these circuit breakers has an associated
independent set of Class IE overvoltage, undervoltage, and under frequency sensing logic. Together, a circuit
breaker and its sensing logic constitute an electric power monitoring assembly. If the output of the inservice
MG set or alternate power supply exceeds predetermined limits of overvoltage, undervoltage, or
underfrequency, a trip coil driven by this logic circuitry opens the circuit breaker, which removes the associated
power supply from service.
Event Description
On April 14, 2023, at 05:11 when James A. FitzPatrick Nuclear Power Plant (JAF) was at 100% power, the
station received indication of a half-scram on “B” side RPS. AOP-60, Loss of RPS Bus B Power, was entered
and the cause was determined to be a trip of the “B” RPS MG set due to an overvoltage condition. The direct
cause of the output breaker tripping was due to an Electric Power Monitoring System overvoltage trip on
71EPA-RPS1B1G, Electrical Protection Assembly (EPA).
On May 25, 2023, at 23:59, when JAF was at 100% power, the station received indication of a half-scram on
the “B” side RPS. AOP-60 was entered and the cause was determined to be a trip of the “B” RPS MG set due
to an overvoltage condition. The direct cause of the output breaker tripping was due to an Electric Power
Monitoring System overvoltage trip on 71EPA-RPS1B1G, EPA.
In both events, the RPS bus power was restored when it was placed on the alternate power supply.
Both events resulted in invalid system actuations of "B" side half scram and Primary Containment Isolation
System (PCIS) [JM] isolations. The following systems isolated as a result of the loss of “B” RPS bus: Reactor
Water Cleanup [CE], Reactor Building ventilation [NG], “B” Containment Atmosphere Dilution [BB], Torus Vent
and Purge, Drywell Equipment and Floor Drain Sumps [WK], “B” Drywell Containment Atmospheric Monitors
[IP], Recirculation System Sample Line, and Main Steam Line Drains [SB]. “B” Standby Gas Treatment
System [BH] started as designed. These system actuations were not initiated by signals in response to actual
plant conditions or parameters satisfying the requirements for initiation of the system.
This event is reportable in accordance with 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(iv)(A).

Event Analysis
Following the April 14 “B” RPS MG set trip, initial troubleshooting determined the most likely cause of the
event was a degraded voltage regulator. The voltage regulator was replaced and sent to PowerLabs for
analysis. The voltage adjust potentiometer “1P” was not replaced. On April 16, 2023, at 22:27, the “B” RPS
MG set was restored to service as the RPS bus power supply.
PowerLabs testing concluded that there was no issue with the voltage regulator component in a report dated
May 15, 2023; however, they identified the most probable cause to be the voltage adjust potentiometer “1P”
component.
After the May 25 trip, a Simpson meter (analog multimeter) was used during troubleshooting and the meter
was observed to waiver erratically across the potentiometer full range of movement. The expected response is
smooth movement as the potentiometer is moved through its full range. The potentiometer was replaced and
sent to PowerLabs for analysis.
The erratic performance of the
potentiometer “1P” was determined to be
poor electrical connection, likely between
the wiper and winding. The most likely
cause of the poor electrical connection is
oxidation on the wiper and winding mating
surfaces. This corrosion introduced an
elevated electrical resistance between the
wiper and coil. Elevated resistance caused
the voltage regulator output to rise, which
caused the RPS MG set voltage to rise
above the overvoltage trip voltage of the
EPA.
Potentiometer wiping preventive
maintenance (PM) should detect and prevent failure. The current PM has technicians take an ‘as-found’
resistance reading using an ohmmeter, then wipe the potentiometer, and take an as-left reading. Best
practices were determined to be a Simpson meter or similar analog type should be used so the potentiometer
resistance can be measured across the entire range to determine if high-resistance spots exist on the
potentiometer. Wiping should continue until the high-resistance sections are eliminated and if that is not
possible, the potentiometer should be replaced. Based on failure modes team input and an industry
benchmark preventative maintenance guidance is being updated to reduce the likelihood of future failures.
Cause
The "B" side half scram, system actuations, and Primary Containment Isolation System (PCIS) isolations were
caused by corrosion buildup in the voltage adjust potentiometer which led to an overvoltage trip of the
Electrical Protection Assembly (EPA).

Similar Events
Condition Report – CR-JAF-2012-01491 dated March 14, 2012, “B” RPS bus failed to transfer from alternate
power supply to MG Set. Most probable cause is momentary high resistances across the voltage adjust
potentiometer “1P”. Corrective actions included revising the PM for the RPS MG sets to include periodic
exercise of the potentiometer. This new maintenance practice was ineffective at preventing the event reported
in this report.
Corrective Actions
Completed Actions
Replaced voltage adjustment potentiometer “1P”.
Planned Actions
Revise “A” and “B” RPS MG set voltage adjustment potentiometer PM to incorporate the use of analog
multimeter and include expected response.

Safety Significance
There were no actual nuclear consequences. RPS Electric Power Monitoring System responded as
designed to an overvoltage condition to protect the electrical power systems. Then system actuations
responded to the loss of power condition as expected without any issue. There was no loss of any safety
functions during these events.
